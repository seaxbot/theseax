Essex pupils to shape year-long county push against bullying

Essex County Council has launched Let's Talk... Safe Relationships across Essex, built with the Young Essex Assembly and the Multi-Schools Council.

Essex · Monday 16 November 2026, 16:29 · Reported by Essex County Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

The campaign started on Monday 16 November, the first day of Anti-Bullying Week, and will run in Essex schools for a year. It teaches the Essex Safe Relationships Model, which rests on five pillars: belonging, respect, safety, repair and speak up. Members of the Young Essex Assembly suggested that "recover" could replace "repair".

Cllr Isi Rocha, Essex County Council's cabinet member for education, skills and SEND, met assembly members for the launch and said children's own voices would shape the work. Young people at the meeting said cliquey, isolating behaviour can begin as early as Year 4, when many children first get social media. Kierran Pearce, the Multi-Schools Council lead, said young people with SEND in particular need to be understood and accepted by classmates to feel safe.

Essex County Council says it will update its guidance for schools, produce new anti-bullying resources and keep asking pupils for feedback. Students aged 11 to 19 will be asked how far each pillar matches their experience through the Young Essex Assembly elections in November, and a survey at the end of the campaign will be compared with those answers. The council will also track whether bullying complaints to Ofsted fall.

Key points

  • It builds on the Essex Inclusion Strategy published in 2024
  • Research by the Anti-Bullying Alliance informs the approach
  • Schools will take a whole-school approach to the model
