The week in Essex: Brentwood School review, oysters and Three Shells

Essex · Updated Sunday 22 November 2026, 20:18 · First published Sunday 22 November 2026, 17:38 · Reported by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

The ten stories that shaped the county this week, where each stands now, and the dates to watch in the week ahead.

It was a week of a damning school review, a decision on a Southend landmark and fresh hope for the county's oyster farmers. Here is where the main stories stand on Sunday evening, and what happens next.

Brentwood School's independent safeguarding review, published on Friday, found that Gareth Stafford Bull, the school's fencing coach from about 1982 until he was sacked in 1997, probably abused a number of boys. It also found the school let down former pupils who came forward after his death: in 2008 the headmaster of the day told one of them he had seen no evidence, although the school's own files recorded that police had found prima facie evidence against Bull in 1997. British Fencing is now setting up its own inquiry into what was known.

Oyster farmers on Mersea, the Colne, the Crouch and the Roach could win back European buyers under the food and farming deal the UK and the EU agreed in Brussels on Friday. Since 2021 the EU has refused shellfish from waters graded Class B unless it has been purified first, and a Seax analysis of the Food Standards Agency's list found that 27 of the 33 oyster zones on the Essex coast carry that grade all year round. The change is expected from March 2028.

On Monday Southend-on-Sea City Council's cabinet backed an £800,000 plan to remove Three Shells Lagoon beside the pier and return the site to natural beach. The lagoon opened in July 2016 and has been fenced off for most of this year, after a mother and daughter had to be pulled out of waist-deep sand in March. Building, repairing and dismantling it will have cost about £2.84m.

On the Dengie Peninsula, Low Carbon's plan for one of the UK's biggest solar farms would cover 4,200 acres across seven sites between Bradwell and Burnham-on-Crouch and could generate up to 1GW. Simon Morgan, leader of Maldon District Council, estimates it would take up almost a third of the peninsula's land. A wider public consultation is planned for early 2027, before the firm applies to the government for consent.

Maldon Salt stayed in the spotlight after an Observer investigation found the firm mixes German rock salt into its sea salt. Maldon District Council says it has no concerns about the firm's sites. Labelling is a matter for Essex County Council's trading standards service, which looked at how the salt is made in 2019 and decided no further action was needed.

A Seax analysis of police.uk data found police in Essex logged 2,042 reports of anti-social behaviour in September, up 76% on a year earlier, with rises in 50 of the force's 56 neighbourhood areas. Recorded crime rose more slowly, to 12,530 offences, while robbery fell 19%.

In Braintree district, the Home Office has sent its plan to extend the Wethersfield asylum centre straight to ministers through the Urgent Crown Development route, bypassing Braintree District Council. It wants the centre to stay open beyond April 2027 and to raise its capacity from 800 to 1,245 people. A police dispersal order around the former airbase runs until midnight on Monday.

Braintree's MP, Sir James Cleverly, was chosen as the Conservative candidate for London mayor at the election in May 2028, and opened his campaign by attacking Lord Khan's record on crime and housing. BAE Systems, which has a base at Great Baddow, announced plans for up to 1,070 UK job cuts, though it has not said whether staff in Chelmsford are affected.

Colchester lost a link to one of television's great satires. Peter Fluck, the co-creator of Spitting Image, who taught at Colchester School of Art in the 1970s, died at his home in Cornwall on Saturday, aged 85.

The week ahead

On Monday Colchester's Local Plan Committee decides whether to recommend sending the city's draft plan to a government inspector, after more than 1,800 responses to the final consultation. On Tuesday a government-appointed inspector opens the public inquiry into the land deals for Chelmsford's £81m Army and Navy scheme, at County Hall from 10am.

On Saturday B&M opens its first Colchester city centre store in the former Wilko unit at St John's Walk, empty since October 2023. Looking further ahead, Colchester City Council's cabinet decides on Wednesday 2 December whether to upgrade Leisure World rather than rebuild it.

Key points

  • British Fencing to hold its own inquiry into what was known
  • Wethersfield asylum centre plan goes straight to ministers
  • Army and Navy inquiry opens on Tuesday; B&M opens in Colchester on Saturday
