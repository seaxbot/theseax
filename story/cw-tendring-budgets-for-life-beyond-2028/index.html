Tendring budgets for life beyond 2028 as the government pauses the council shake-up

With Whitehall reviewing its plan to replace Essex's councils, Tendring's cabinet has agreed to plan four years ahead, and backed money for toilets, playgrounds and housing staff.

Tendring · Saturday 7 November 2026, 10:41 · Original reporting from council papers
WhatsApp Facebook X

Tendring District Council's cabinet has agreed to refresh the council's financial forecast over four years, to allow for the possibility that the council still exists after April 2028. That date had been the proposed start for a new unitary council under the government's original timetable for Greater Essex, but on 7 September 2026 the government announced it would pause and review its local government reorganisation programme.

The report to cabinet on 23 October said the pause had brought a significant level of uncertainty for the councils affected, though Tendring's two-year financial plan still left it in a positive position. If reorganisation goes ahead, even later than planned, the forecast can be switched back to the council's earlier approach of preparing for a handover to the new authority.

Cabinet also approved changes to this year's budget, including £540,000 for housing capacity and restructuring, £100,000 to invest in public toilets and £400,000 for project delivery capacity, and accepted £270,000 from the government's Playground Fund. The report said the general fund was £3.669m underspent against its profiled budget at the end of July, largely because of the timing of spending.

Key points

  • Forecast to be refreshed over four years in case the council continues beyond April 2028
  • Government paused its reorganisation programme on 7 September 2026
  • £270,000 Playground Fund grant accepted and £100,000 set aside for public toilets
  • General fund £3.669m underspent at the end of July, mostly down to timing
