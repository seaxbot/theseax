South Essex crisis team uses charity kit to keep older patients at home

Southend, Basildon, Chelmsford · Thursday 26 November 2026, 08:52 · Reported by Essex Partnership University NHS Foundation Trust
WhatsApp Facebook X

A south Essex NHS crisis team has bought puzzles, music and craft kits with charity money. They help older patients stay out of hospital.

Older people in a mental health crisis in Southend, Basildon and Chelmsford now have extra practical help at home. Essex Partnership University NHS Foundation Trust (EPUT) said on Thursday 26 November that its own charity had paid for the kit.

EPUT's Functional Intensive Response and Support Team, known as FIRST, visits older adults in their own homes. It works with families and other clinical teams so patients can manage their mental health and, where possible, avoid going into hospital.

The charity money bought whiteboards and pens, notebooks, large-print puzzles, craft materials, portable CD players and CDs, and sensory items such as fidget toys and stress putty. Occupational therapists share them across the FIRST teams in Southend, Basildon and Chelmsford.

Laura Dawson, an occupational therapist with the team, said the resources had been "really beneficial for our patients". She said whiteboards can work as memory prompts or help someone plan a daily routine. Silk painting has worked especially well, she said, because people can quickly make something they feel proud of.

Families can also try an item before deciding to buy one themselves. EPUT Charitable Funds pays for activities and improvements beyond what the NHS would normally fund. Anyone in a mental health crisis can call EPUT's 24-hour crisis line on 111.

Key points

  • FIRST visits older adults in their own homes
  • Silk painting has proved one of the most effective activities
  • Paid for by EPUT Charitable Funds, not core NHS budgets
  • The 111 mental health crisis line runs 24 hours a day
