Report warns worker shortage could delay Lower Thames Crossing and pylons

Thurrock, Tilbury · Updated Wednesday 25 November 2026, 19:05 · First published Wednesday 25 November 2026, 09:38 · First reported by Local Government East · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

Two of the East's biggest schemes are in Essex. Their busiest years clash with other projects, and too few local people are being trained.

A shortage of skilled workers could push up costs and delay major building projects across the East of England, including two in Essex, a report by MPs and council leaders warns.

Opportunity East: Closing the Infrastructure Skills Gap was launched at the House of Lords on Tuesday by Local Government East and the East of England All-Party Parliamentary Group. It says the region has 57 Nationally Significant Infrastructure Projects, more than any other part of the country.

Government figures suggest up to 30,080 workers will be needed to build them over the next five years, the report says. Many of the projects will be at their busiest between 2028 and 2030, and they need the same trades: civil engineers, electricians, steel fixers, plant operators and quantity surveyors.

The Essex projects

The Lower Thames Crossing, the new road and tunnel under the Thames between Thurrock and Kent, will need about 3,000 workers at its peak, according to the report. National Highways started early work in Essex and Kent in March.

National Highways aims to recruit at least 45% of the crossing's workforce from within 20 miles. It wants 20% from the communities closest to the route, including Thurrock and Brentwood.

National Grid's Norwich to Tilbury power line would need 1,720 workers at its peak. Planning inspectors had sent their recommendation on the line, about 180km long, to the energy secretary by 10 November. A decision is expected within three months.

The report says the crossing, the power line and the Sealink power cable will all be at their busiest around 2028 to 2030. At the same time, Sizewell C in Suffolk and the Universal theme park in Bedfordshire will still be building up their workforces.

It lists Stansted Airport's expansion among the region's major projects. It also says the Harlow and Gilston Garden Town will bring a further 23,000 homes in Essex and Hertfordshire, needing many of the same skills.

Young people left out

Around 90,000 people aged 16 to 24 in the East were not in education, employment or training at the end of 2025, the report says. That is 11.7% of the age group.

Among 16 and 17-year-olds, the Essex County Council area does worse than the national average. Department for Education figures show 6.2% of that age group there were not in education, employment or training, or their activity was not known.

That compares with 5.8% across England and 4.9% across the East. The rate was 5.1% in Southend and 3.8% in Thurrock. The figures are an average of December 2025 to February 2026.

What the report wants

Its main call is for the government to back a single workforce plan for the region's infrastructure, so colleges and training providers can see which jobs are needed, how many, where and when.

It also wants skills money handed to the region sooner, ahead of mayoral elections. Greater Essex's first mayoral election was due in May 2026 but has been put back to May 2028.

Other recommendations include requiring big infrastructure projects to commit to hiring young people, as Alan Milburn's recent report on young people proposed. It also wants better transport so people can reach training and jobs, and says adult skills funding has fallen by 25% in real terms over ten years.

Training in Essex

The report holds up the region's Clean Energy Technical Excellence College, run by Colchester Institute, as the kind of joined-up working that is needed. But it says funding for such schemes needs to improve.

Colchester Institute won that status in April. The college said then that up to 60,000 clean energy jobs are expected in the East by 2030.

What they said

Councillor Carl Smith, chair of Local Government East and leader of Great Yarmouth Borough Council, said: "Unless we invest now in skills, training and workforce planning, we risk missing a once-in-a-generation opportunity."

Jess Asato, the Labour MP for Lowestoft and co-chair of the parliamentary group, said the report tackled "one of the most important challenges facing our region and the country: matching jobs to people".

The growth minister, Dan Tomlinson, called the report "timely". He told the launch: "I have read your report and I will direct my civil servants to come and talk to you."

Key points

  • The report wants one workforce plan and skills money handed to the region sooner
  • Lower Thames Crossing needs about 3,000 at its peak, the Norwich to Tilbury line 1,720
  • 6.2% of 16 and 17-year-olds in the Essex County Council area are NEET or not known, above England's 5.8%
