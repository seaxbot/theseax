# Greater Anglia to give unclaimed lost property to Essex women's refuges

**Refuges across Essex can now ask Greater Anglia for items left on its trains. Deliveries go through lockers to keep refuge addresses secret.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 09:36 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Community.

## Key points

- Refuges in Suffolk, Cambridgeshire and Greater London are covered too
- Unclaimed items are held for three months first
- Rail to Refuge has given 13,054 survivors free travel since April 2020

## The story

Women's refuges in Essex, Suffolk, Cambridgeshire and Greater London can now send Greater Anglia a list of things they need. The train company will then supply what it can from unclaimed lost property. The trial, announced on Thursday 26 November, makes Greater Anglia the second train operator to join a scheme the Rail Delivery Group began with Southeastern in November 2025.

Rail UK reported that the first Greater Anglia delivery included toiletries and other personal items. Refuges can also ask for things such as clothing or toys. Parcels travel through InPost lockers, so nobody handling them learns where a refuge is.

According to RailAdvent, Greater Anglia keeps lost property for three months. Only after that can unclaimed belongings be donated or disposed of, and that policy has not changed.

Lynsey Flack, Greater Anglia's head of customer service, said things left on trains and stations "can often still be useful to someone else". She thanked the company's lost property team at Pakex, the Rail Delivery Group and InPost. Dan Mann, co-chief executive of the Rail Delivery Group, said the scheme builds on the success of the first trial.

## How the scheme began

Southeastern started it with the London charity Solace Women's Aid. The Rail Delivery Group said that first delivery, in November 2025, held coats, jumpers and cuddly toys. Solace supported 817 women and children across 19 refuges and 71 move-on spaces in its most recent year, the group said at the time.

Railway companies already run Rail to Refuge with Women's Aid. It gives people escaping domestic abuse a free train journey to a refuge, and the Rail Delivery Group says 13,054 survivors have used it since April 2020.

Neither company has said how long the Greater Anglia trial will run. The Rail Delivery Group has said it wants to bring more train operators into the scheme.
