# Harlow Council tells government to drop asylum plan for private homes

**The Home Office's contractor wants to rent homes across Harlow for asylum seekers. The council's leader wants the plan dropped.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Harlow, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 18:13 (UK time). Updated: Thursday 26 November 2026, 19:59. Section: News. Topic: Politics.

## Key points

- Six proposals arrived in a single week
- The council says it has objected to every one
- A 2025 plan for Endeavour House was withdrawn

## The story

Harlow Council has demanded that the government abandon plans to rent private homes across the town to house asylum seekers. Your Harlow reported on Thursday 26 November that the council had received six letters in a single week from the Home Office's accommodation contractor. Each one proposes placing several asylum seekers in a privately rented home over the coming weeks.

Council leader Dan Swords said the council has objected to every proposal and expects many more to follow. He has written directly to the Home Secretary. He argued that hundreds of Harlow families are already living in temporary accommodation. He asked why the same homes could not go to local families who are homeless or struggling to find somewhere to rent.

## How we got here

Harlow has fought the Home Office over asylum housing before. In October 2025 the government told the council it intended to use Redstone House, the town centre block now called Endeavour House, for asylum seekers. Council papers show the building had earlier been shut as unsafe. Mr Swords wrote to the Home Secretary to oppose that plan and promised to fight it politically, legally and publicly. Your Harlow later reported that the proposal was withdrawn after the building's owners turned it down.

Renting ordinary houses is an approach the Home Office has tried elsewhere in Essex. Earlier this month villagers in Tillingham were divided over a plan to house six asylum seekers in a home there, [as The Seax reported](https://theseax.com/story/tillingham-home-office-plan-six-asylum-seekers-house/).

Harlow's Labour group leader, councillor James Griggs, hit back on Thursday evening in a statement published by Your Harlow. He accused the Conservative-run council of trying to "outdo Reform" and of blaming asylum seekers for the town's housing shortage. He argued that the council's "Harlow Homes for Harlow People" approach covers only council-owned homes.

## What happens next

The decision on where asylum seekers live rests with the Home Office, not the council. Hannah Ellis, the Conservative parliamentary candidate for Harlow, has backed the council and called on the town's Labour MP, Chris Vince, to press the government. No Home Office response to the council's objections has been published.

Mr Vince replied on Thursday evening in a statement published by Your Harlow. He said the government had inherited a broken immigration system and pointed to the closure of asylum hotels and a fall in small boat crossings. He called Harlow a welcoming town and said people in genuine need should get protection, while those with no right to stay should be returned.
