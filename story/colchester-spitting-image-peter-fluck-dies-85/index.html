Spitting Image co-creator Peter Fluck, who taught art in Colchester, dies at 85

Colchester · Saturday 21 November 2026, 23:30 · Reported by ITV News
WhatsApp Facebook X

Peter Fluck, the artist and caricaturist who co-created the satirical television series Spitting Image, has died aged 85.

Fluck, who taught art at Colchester School of Art during the 1970s, died at his home in Cornwall in the early hours of Saturday, November 21, following a recent illness, his family said.

He became one of the most influential figures in British television satire through Spitting Image, the ITV programme which used grotesque puppet caricatures to mock politicians, celebrities, royalty and other prominent figures.

But before the show made him a household name, Fluck spent part of his career teaching aspiring artists in Colchester.

From art teacher to television satire

Born in Cambridge in 1941, Fluck studied at Cambridge School of Art, where he met fellow artist Roger Law.

The pair went on to establish a creative partnership, initially working as freelance illustrators and caricaturists before developing the idea that would transform their careers.

Spitting Image first aired in 1984, bringing their exaggerated puppet creations to television screens across Britain.

The programme became famous for its uncompromising portrayal of the country's political establishment, with Margaret Thatcher, John Major, members of the Royal Family and international figures among its regular targets.

At its peak, the series attracted audiences of around 15 million viewers and became one of the defining television programmes of the 1980s and 1990s.

It ran for 18 series before ending in 1996.

A lasting legacy

Fluck and Law's creations helped redefine political satire, combining caricature, puppetry and comedy to produce some of the most recognisable television characters of their era.

The programme won numerous awards, including two Emmy Awards, and its influence extended beyond television into music, popular culture and political commentary.

After Spitting Image ended, Fluck continued his artistic career, turning his attention to sculpture and moving installations from his studio in Cornwall.

His family described him as a private man who avoided fame and attention.

In a statement, they said: "The spirit that Spitting Image brought to the country was epoch defining.

"With Roger Law ('Luck and Flaw'), he brought a unique cutting edge to caricaturing that later evolved into a production company that created the TV series Spitting Image."

They added that his creative life continued for many years after the programme from his studio in Cornwall.

His death marks the loss of one of the artists behind a television phenomenon which remains synonymous with British satire.
