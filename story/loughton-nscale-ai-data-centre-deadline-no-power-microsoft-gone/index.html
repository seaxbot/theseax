Loughton's £2bn AI data centre was due this year. Its power may not arrive until the 2030s

Microsoft has walked away, the grid connection is years off and the 750 jobs promised for Langston Road no longer have an opening date attached.

Loughton · Wednesday 18 November 2026, 00:50 · Reported by BBC News
WhatsApp Facebook X

When Nscale bought a scaffolding yard on Langston Road, near Debden station, in January 2025, it said Britain's biggest artificial intelligence data centre would be running there by the last quarter of 2026. Ministers later held the scheme up as the country's largest sovereign AI site. With six weeks of the year left, the yard has been cleared but there is no supercomputer, and The Times this week described the plot as still "a pile of bricks".

The obstacle is electricity. The site was due to receive its supply by the end of this year, the BBC reports, but Nscale has been told a connection will not come until the next decade. The Guardian reported in September that UK Power Networks had put the date at the early to mid 2030s. In October Microsoft, the only customer announced for Loughton and the firm due to rent about 23,000 Nvidia chips there, ended its agreement. "This decision relates to a single site," a Microsoft spokesperson told Data Centre Review. Nscale told the BBC: "We remain committed to both the Loughton project and the UK." It says it is exploring other sources of power for the site.

For Loughton, the promise was work: Nscale said about 500 jobs in construction and a further 250 permanent roles once the centre opened. The scheme was never universally welcome. Loughton Town Council officers objected in March, asking for evidence that its energy demand would not strain the local grid or push up residents' bills. Epping Forest District Council approved a larger design in June over objections from councillors and residents, after investment minister Lord Stockwood wrote to the council's chief executive to stress the government's support. Nscale has not given a new opening date.

Key points

  • Microsoft was the only customer announced for the site
  • Epping Forest District Council approved an enlarged design in June
  • Nscale says it is looking at other ways to power the centre
