Air ambulance downwash seriously injured woman filming its take-off near Southend Airport, report finds

Accident investigators say the Essex & Herts Air Ambulance crew could not see the woman behind a hedge in Southend and flew on unaware she had been hurt.

Southend-on-Sea · Friday 13 November 2026, 06:14 · Updated Friday 13 November 2026, 11:28 · Reported by Air Accidents Investigation Branch
WhatsApp Facebook X

The woman had stepped out of her house to record the helicopter leaving a private field next to Southend Airport railway station at 6.08pm on Saturday 28 February 2026, and was standing by a parked car about 30 metres away when the blast of air from its rotors knocked her down. A relative helped her and she was taken to hospital, according to an Air Accidents Investigation Branch (AAIB) report published on 12 November.

The AW169 had landed in the field at about 5.46pm so its two paramedics could reach a patient in the homes east of the airport, and with sunset close the two pilots planned to fly night training at the airport before coming back for them. The commander asked bystanders with children by a fence to cross the road, and the crew waited for a train to leave the station before lifting off. The helicopter climbed almost vertically to 200ft but drifted east towards the road, and investigators say the hedge, the house and the parked car may have funnelled and strengthened the downwash.

The crew flew back to North Weald Airfield without knowing anyone had been injured, and the operator only learned of it when a relative got in touch four days later. The commander told investigators the hedge hid the houses from his view and that he would have warned the woman had he seen her. On 29 May 2026 the operator issued an instruction to all its pilots reminding them that downwash is a hazard on take-off as well as on landing.

Key points

  • Happened at 6.08pm on Saturday 28 February 2026
  • Fading light and the wind may have helped push the helicopter towards the road, the AAIB says
  • Pilots have since been reminded that downwash is a danger on take-off, not only landing
