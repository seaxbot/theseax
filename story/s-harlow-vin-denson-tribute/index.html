Harlow Cycling Club honours Vin Denson, first Briton to win a Giro d'Italia stage

The club's honorary member, who rode the Tour de France alongside Jacques Anquetil and Tom Simpson, has died aged 90.

Harlow · Monday 2 November 2026, 18:00 · Reported by Your Harlow
WhatsApp Facebook X

Harlow Cycling Club has paid tribute to Vin Denson, the pioneering professional cyclist who became one of its best-known members, after his death at the age of 90. Denson was the first British rider to win a stage of the Giro d'Italia and spent much of his later life back among Harlow's cycling community.

Denson won a Giro stage in 1966 and the Tour of Luxembourg, and rode the Tour de France as a support rider for stars including Jacques Anquetil and Rik van Loy. He was a close friend of Tom Simpson. ProCyclingUK reports he died on 14 October. He joined the Harlow clubroom in 1968 to 1969, rode for the Bantel team and in 1994 took third in the individual pursuit at the World Masters Track Championships in Manchester.

In a tribute published by Your Harlow, club member Willi Tarran remembered him as tough but kind and a constant source of encouragement for young riders, describing him as "a Giant of a man". He was made an honorary member of the club and kept turning up at its races, barbecues and social events.

Key points

  • First British stage winner at the Giro d'Italia, in 1966
  • Rode for Anquetil's Ford France team and was a friend of Tom Simpson
  • Honorary member of Harlow Cycling Club since joining in the late 1960s
  • Died aged 90, ProCyclingUK reports
