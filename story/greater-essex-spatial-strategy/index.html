Essex, Southend and Thurrock councils start work on 25-year plan for where homes, jobs and roads will go

The three upper-tier councils are preparing a Spatial Development Strategy for Greater Essex, a long-range blueprint that will steer growth while leaving individual sites to local plans.

Essex · Thursday 5 November 2026, 17:28 · Reported by Essex County Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Essex County Council, Southend City Council and Thurrock Council have begun preparing a Spatial Development Strategy for Greater Essex, a statutory planning document that will look at least 25 years ahead. It will shape how housing need, economic growth, major infrastructure and environmental resilience are handled across the whole area, so it matters to every community expecting new homes or roads.

According to the county council, the strategy will share out growth between local plan areas, coordinate strategic infrastructure and tackle issues such as nature recovery and climate resilience. It will not allocate individual sites or set detailed planning rules, which stay with district local plans; the Colchester Gazette reports it will not redraw green belt boundaries either. Cabinet member Jeff Bray said it gave councils the chance to plan for Greater Essex in a joined-up way.

The councils will act as the strategic planning authority until a mayoral combined county authority is created, which will then take the work over. The county council's timetable runs from scoping in 2026 to an agreed vision with an elected mayor in 2027, a draft in 2028, public consultation in 2029 and examination and adoption in 2030, with the plan reviewed every five years.

Key points

  • Covers at least 25 years, reviewed every five years
  • Will not pick specific sites, which stay with local plans
  • Public consultation on a draft planned for 2029
  • Responsibility passes to a future mayoral authority
