# Southend City Council urges Direct Debit sign-ups for £100 energy payment

**Households who already pay council tax this way will get the government's new payment automatically. Others will have to ask for it.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Southend-on-Sea, Essex, England. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 13:37 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Community.

## Key points

- Payments start in January 2027
- Non Direct Debit payers will be invited to apply
- Cost of living booklet at libraries and the Civic Centre

## The story

Southend-on-Sea City Council on Wednesday 25 November asked residents to switch their council tax to Direct Debit, so the government's one-off £100 Household Energy Payment reaches them automatically when payments begin in January.

Anyone already paying that way will see the money go straight into their bank account, with no form to fill in. People who pay another way will be invited by the council to apply, and the council says more details will follow. You can set up a Direct Debit online at southend.gov.uk/CtaxOnline.

Council leader Daniel Cowan said there would be "no need to apply" for Direct Debit users.

## Why it matters this winter

The payment arrives as household bills climb. The energy price cap rises by 26% to £2,166 a year in January, [as The Seax reported on Wednesday](https://theseax.com/story/essex-energy-bills-price-cap-rise-26-percent-january-2027/). The £100 is aimed at homes in council tax bands A to D, and in Basildon alone about 65,000 homes fall in those bands, [The Seax found earlier this week](https://theseax.com/story/basildon-100-household-energy-payment-65000-homes-bands-a-d/).

Southend's council has not said how many of its own households qualify or the exact date the money will land.

## Other help

Southend's cost of living pages at southend.gov.uk/costofliving set out other support, including Universal Credit, Pension Credit, Council Tax Reduction and local welfare assistance. A printed booklet with the same advice is available from the Civic Centre, libraries and partner organisations.
