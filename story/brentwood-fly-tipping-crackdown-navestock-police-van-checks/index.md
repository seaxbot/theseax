# Navestock fly-tippers targeted by Brentwood police van checks

**Police and council officers are patrolling Brentwood's country lanes and stopping vans. The borough sees about three fly-tips a day.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Navestock, Brentwood, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 16:36 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Environment.

## Key points

- Clearing fly-tips costs the council over £95,000 a year
- Fly-tipping fines can reach £50,000
- Report a fly-tip in progress on 999

## The story

Brentwood Neighbourhood Policing Team and Brentwood Borough Council's environmental officers have started a joint operation against fly-tipping, the council announced on Thursday 26 November. Officers are making extra patrols in the worst-hit parts of the borough and pulling over vans for spot checks.

Navestock and other quiet spots close to the M25 and A12 are being singled out by dumpers, the officers believe. Household rubbish, car parts and even burnt-out vehicles have been left at the roadside. Building waste has sometimes blocked roads altogether.

PC Tom Marriott is leading the police side of the operation. He said fly-tips had blocked entire country lanes, leaving rubbish that takes a long time to clear. Some people have dumped asbestos, which costs the council thousands of pounds to remove and can harm people and wildlife.

## What it costs

Clearing and disposing of dumped waste costs Brentwood Borough Council more than £95,000 a year, according to Councillor David Worsfold, who chairs its Clean and Green Committee. On top of prosecutions, the council hands out fixed penalty notices. A recent Brentwood case ended in a prison sentence, the councillor said.

In March the council reported that a man had admitted four fly-tipping offences across the borough, involving an estimated eight tonnes of waste. Neighbouring councils are also getting tougher. Epping Forest District Council crushed a seized fly-tipping truck for the first time this month, [as The Seax reported](https://theseax.com/story/epping-forest-council-crushes-fly-tipping-truck-first-time/), and Basildon Council set up its own [fly-tipping task force](https://theseax.com/story/basildon-fly-tipping-task-force-1696-dumps/) in November.

## How to report it

Anyone paying to get rid of waste should check the firm holds a waste carrier licence, PC Marriott said. Licences can be looked up on the Environment Agency website. A fly-tip happening now should be reported on 999. Dumped waste can be reported to the council with a photo and its exact location, for example from the What3Words app.
