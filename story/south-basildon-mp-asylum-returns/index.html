South Basildon MP says asylum system failing to act on refusals as returns fall to 10%

James McMurdock wants faster decisions and published return figures, citing Home Office data showing only about one in ten refused 2025 claims has a recorded return.

Basildon · Friday 6 November 2026, 12:00 · Reported by Basildon Today
WhatsApp Facebook X

James McMurdock, MP for South Basildon and East Thurrock, says the asylum system is failing to act on its own refusal decisions, and has called for faster decisions, more efficient appeals and published figures on how many refused claimants are eventually removed. He said a refusal cannot simply become the start of years of delay and uncertainty.

Home Office data to July 2026 shows about 10 per cent of refused asylum claims made in 2025 have a recorded return, around 3,300 against about 34,300 refusals, compared with 55 per cent for 2022 claims and 13 per cent for 2024. At the end of June about 90,000 appeals were outstanding with an average wait of around 72 weeks, a backlog Mr McMurdock called unacceptable, and 32,400 applications were awaiting a first decision.

Basildon Today notes the comparison is not clean, as some people counted as returned may have left before a decision and some refusals are still under appeal, so the 2025 figures will change. The figures are national and are not broken down for Basildon or Thurrock. No government response was reported.

Key points

  • About 3,300 returns against 34,300 refused 2025 claims
  • Around 90,000 appeals outstanding, average wait 72 weeks
  • MP wants return figures published
  • Figures are national, not local
