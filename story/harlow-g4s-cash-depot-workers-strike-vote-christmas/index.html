Harlow G4S cash depot workers vote to strike before Christmas

Harlow · Thursday 26 November 2026, 13:12 · First reported by BBC News · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

Staff who move cash for banks and shops from Harlow have rejected a 1% pay offer. A walkout could hit cash machines.

Workers at the G4S cash depot in Harlow have voted to take strike action in the run-up to Christmas, the GMB union said on Thursday 26 November. The union said 95% of its members who voted backed industrial action. The staff transport and guard cash and valuables for banks and businesses.

The dispute is over pay. According to the GMB, G4S has offered a rise of 1% and has not improved on it. BBC News reported that the workers want 7%, in line with inflation, and that the union represents about 90% of staff at the site. The union points out that Christmas is one of the busiest times of year for shops taking cash.

The GMB warned that a strike could affect cash supplies to ATMs and businesses across parts of the South East and north and east London. No strike dates have been announced. Andre Marques, the GMB regional organiser, said the company needed to "get back round the table and make a decent offer".

What happens next

G4S told the BBC it had not received formal notice of any strike. The company said it was open to talks with the GMB and hoped to reach an agreement without industrial action. Under rules brought in in February, unions must give 10 days' written notice of a strike, down from 14, and a ballot mandate now lasts 12 months rather than six.

Key points

  • 95% of GMB members who voted backed action
  • The workers are asking for 7%
  • No strike dates have been set yet
