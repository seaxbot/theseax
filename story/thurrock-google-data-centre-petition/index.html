Campaigners say Google turned away 54,000-name petition against Purfleet data centre on Arena Essex wildlife site

Buglife and Essex Wildlife Trust took their objections to Google's London headquarters over plans for four data centre buildings on the former raceway near Purfleet.

Purfleet · Thursday 5 November 2026, 11:30 · Reported by BBC News (via Yahoo News UK)
WhatsApp Facebook X

Protesters opposed to Google's plans for a data centre campus on the former Arena Essex Raceway near Purfleet say staff at the company's London headquarters refused to accept their petition, which carried about 54,970 signatures. The 52-hectare site in Thurrock has been a Local Wildlife Site since 2006, and campaigners say the scheme would wipe out more than 80 per cent of its protected habitats.

Google is proposing up to four data centre buildings totalling 130,500 square metres, plus a separate office block. Conservation charity Buglife and Essex Wildlife Trust say the land supports rare bumblebees, butterflies, nightingales and the endangered broad-leaved cudweed. Buglife's Jamie Robins said it was simply the wrong place for a data centre, while Emily Buttress of Essex Wildlife Trust said its loss would be devastating for the area.

Google would neither confirm nor deny that the petition was turned away, the BBC reports. The company says data centres are vital to the UK, that it is working with Natural England towards a 10 per cent biodiversity net gain, and that a substantial part of the site would be kept as public open space and habitat. Campaigners want the land opened up as green space instead, with some calling for the return of Thurrock Hammers speedway.

Key points

  • Petition carried about 54,970 signatures
  • Plans cover four data centre buildings of 130,500 sq m on a 52-hectare site
  • Site has been a Local Wildlife Site since 2006
  • Google promises a 10% biodiversity net gain
