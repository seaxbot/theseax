# Tendring Local Plan with 18,173 homes heads to government inspector

**Tendring District Council will send its blueprint for growth to the government by the end of December. Public hearings are expected in 2027.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Clacton-on-Sea, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 13:34 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Property.

## Key points

- The government's target for Tendring is 1,069 homes a year
- About 1,400 comments came in at the final consultation
- The inspector can recommend changes before adoption

## The story

Tendring District Council's full council voted on Tuesday 24 November to submit its new Local Plan to the government, the council announced on Thursday 26 November. The plan sets out where new homes, jobs and infrastructure should go across the district from 2026 to 2043.

A Local Plan is the document a council uses to decide planning applications. Tendring's has to meet the government's mandatory housebuilding target of 1,069 homes a year. The council aims to send the plan and its supporting evidence to the Secretary of State before the end of December. A planning inspector appointed by the government will then test whether it is legally sound.

About 320 people and organisations sent in around 1,400 comments during the final six-week consultation in September and October. Their worries included roads and traffic, pressure on health services and schools, drainage and wastewater, and the loss of countryside and farmland. Every comment from that consultation will go to the inspector.

Councillor Carlo Guglielmi, who chairs the council's Planning Policy and Local Plan Committee, said: "We know how deeply people care about the future of their communities." Council leader Mark Stephenson said the plan was key to lining up infrastructure with new building and to protecting communities from speculative development.

## What happens next

Public hearings are expected in 2027, and the inspector can recommend changes before the council adopts the plan. Some sites are already contested: Brightlingsea's ward councillors have vowed to fight Trinity College's bid to have land on the edge of their town included, [as The Seax reported](https://theseax.com/story/brightlingsea-councillors-oppose-550-homes-church-road-local-plan/).
