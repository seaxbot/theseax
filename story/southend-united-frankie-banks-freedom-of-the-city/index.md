# Southend United legend Frankie Banks given Freedom of the City

**The former Shrimpers full-back started the club's community work in 1991. The club says his impact goes far beyond the pitch.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Southend-on-Sea, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 18:24 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Community.

## Key points

- Signed professional for Southend in 1962
- Voted Hull City's greatest right-back
- Still leads the Senior Shrimpers club for over-60s

## The story

Southend United great Frankie Banks has been awarded the Freedom of the City of Southend-on-Sea, one of the highest honours Southend City Council can give.

The club congratulated him on Thursday evening, sharing a picture of the Mayor presenting him with his framed Freedom scroll, made out to Francis Stanley Banks. It called him 'a Shrimpers legend who has had an impact far beyond the football pitch'.

Southend Today reported on 20 November that he and long-serving councillor David Garston were to be given the honour.

## From a park kickabout to Roots Hall

Banks was born in Hull in 1945, but his family moved to Hadleigh when he was a baby. A Southend United scout spotted him playing in a local park in 1958, when he was 13.

He signed his first professional contract at 17, in 1962, and made his debut away at Colchester United. In 1966 he joined his home-town club, Hull City, where he played more than 300 games in ten years. Hull fans later voted him the club's greatest right-back.

He came back to Southend in 1976 and played on until 1979. He then went straight into coaching at Roots Hall, working as youth team coach, first team coach, assistant manager and interim first team manager.

## 'Mr Southend'

In 1991 he became the club's first Football in the Community Officer, starting out on his own on £200 a week. That work laid the foundations of today's Southend United Community Foundation. He led it for 16 years before retiring in 2007.

Retirement has not slowed him down. In 1992 he set up the Senior Shrimpers club, which he still leads. It runs 12 day trips a year for more than 100 members aged 60 to 92, plus a Christmas party at Roots Hall.

He also runs walking football, mentors young coaches, helps with school PE lessons and tutors FA coaching courses for young people, the Foundation says. In 2014 he won the Football League Awards' Unsung Hero award.

When he turned 80 last year, Foundation chief executive Dale Spiby called him 'the heartbeat of this club and our Foundation'.

## A rare honour

Southend became a city in 2022. The late Sir David Amess, who campaigned for city status, was the first person to be given its Freedom, after his death.

The Music Man Project founder David Stanley was honoured in July 2023. Shrimpers manager Kevin Maher followed in 2024, becoming only the third person to receive it.
