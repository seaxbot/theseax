South Basildon MP James McMurdock raises alarm over how few refused asylum claims end in returns

Home Office figures show only around one in ten asylum claims refused from 2025 has so far led to a recorded return, and the MP for South Basildon and East Thurrock wants the Government to publish clearer data.

Basildon, Thurrock · Saturday 7 November 2026, 16:02 · Reported by Your Thurrock
WhatsApp Facebook X

James McMurdock, MP for South Basildon and East Thurrock, has raised concerns about the small share of refused asylum claims that lead to a recorded return, as well as long appeal waits and a growing backlog. He said a refusal should not become the start of years of delay and uncertainty, and called the backlog unacceptable.

Home Office figures reported alongside his comments show about 3,300 returns had been recorded by July 2026 for claims made in 2025, against about 34,300 claims whose latest outcome was refusal, roughly 10%. The share of refused claims ending in a recorded return fell from 55% in 2022 to 13% in 2024. At the end of June 2026 about 90,000 asylum appeals were outstanding, with an average wait of around 72 weeks, and 32,400 applications were awaiting an initial decision.

Mr McMurdock is urging the Government to provide clear reporting on how many refused claimants are ultimately returned. The figures do not necessarily mean every other refused claimant remains in the UK: some people leave before a decision, some are removed for other reasons and some refusals are still under appeal.

Key points

  • About 10% of refused 2025 claims have led to a recorded return so far
  • Return share was 55% in 2022
  • Around 90,000 asylum appeals outstanding, average wait about 72 weeks
  • MP calls for clearer Government reporting on returns
