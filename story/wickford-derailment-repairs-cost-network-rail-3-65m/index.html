Wickford derailment repairs to cost Network Rail £3.65m

Wickford · Updated Monday 23 November 2026, 08:55 · First published Monday 23 November 2026, 07:17 · First reported by Railnews · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

Network Rail has put the cost of repairing the line south of Wickford at £3.65m, covering heavy machinery hire and track repairs, as first reported by New Civil Engineer and Railnews. Together with £5.4m for the derailment at Lewes in East Sussex the day before, it leaves Network Rail facing a bill of about £9m from two derailments in two days last summer.

The Seax has gone back over the official record of what happened on Friday 14 August. The Rail Accident Investigation Branch (RAIB) says the train, running from Southend Victoria to London Liverpool Street, left the track at about 2.08pm, around 2.1 miles south-east of Wickford station near Salcott Crescent. Its data recorder shows it was doing about 77mph. The driver had seen a kink in the track that rocked the cab and was reporting it when they braked, after an emergency call from a train coming the other way.

The rear coach was dragged across the sleepers and ballast for about a mile and a half before the train stopped. It stayed upright. Ambulance crews, backed by two air ambulances, checked all 62 people on board and nobody needed hospital. Rayleigh and Wickford MP Mark Francois said the coach acted like a "knife" on the line: about 3,700 sleepers had to be replaced, along with signals and overhead power lines.

The line between Billericay and Hockley stayed shut for 12 days. Passengers took a shuttle train from Shenfield to Billericay, then buses to Wickford, Rayleigh and Hockley. Trains to Southend Victoria ran normally again from Wednesday 26 August, while the Southminster branch stayed on buses until 28 August for planned track renewals.

The cause has not yet been established. The RAIB, which opened a full investigation on 28 August, is looking at how the track was maintained, what was done to protect it in hot weather and how it was managed, and will publish a final report when its work ends. Network Rail has said the extreme heat and drought caused exceptional problems for the railway, and dried-out clay led to slower timetables on the Southend Victoria line from 1 September. Jamie Burles, managing director of GBR Anglia, said the operator was sorry for the disruption and thankful no one was hurt.

Key points

  • 62 people on board, train doing about 77mph
  • Rear coach dragged a mile and a half, wrecking 3,700 sleepers
  • Billericay to Hockley line shut for 12 days
  • RAIB investigation into the cause still under way
