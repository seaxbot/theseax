# Army and Navy inquiry opens into £81m Chelmsford junction rebuild

**Up to 75,000 vehicles a day use the Chelmsford junction, which has been over capacity at peak times since its flyover came down in 2019.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Chelmsford, Army and Navy roundabout, Essex, England. Published: Monday 23 November 2026, 17:37 (UK time). Updated: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 00:41. Section: News. Topic: Transport.

## Key points

- Inspector Stephen Normington chairs the hearing, with extra days on 1 and 3 December
- Drivers would get through 53% quicker, the council's modelling says
- Building is now due to run from 2028 to 2031
- The Transport Secretary makes the final decision

## The story

A public inquiry opened at 10am on Tuesday 24 November, giving landowners and objectors their say. It is looking at whether Essex County Council can buy land, by compulsory purchase if it cannot strike deals, to rebuild the Army and Navy. Stephen Normington, the inspector appointed by the government, is hearing the council's case and the objections on the fourth floor of County Hall in Market Road. The inquiry is expected to sit for four days, with Tuesday 1 December and Thursday 3 December set aside as extra days, Essex Highways says.

Until 2019 a tidal flyover carried one-way car traffic over the five-arm roundabout. It also takes 72 buses an hour, and the council says journeys through it can take more than six times longer at peak times.

## What would change

The £81m Army and Navy Sustainable Transport Package would replace it with a "hamburger" roundabout: a two-way dual carriageway runs straight through the middle of the central island. Alongside it would come a segregated left-turn lane from Chelmer Road to Essex Yeomanry Way and a 350-space expansion of Sandon Park and Ride. Buses would get a new northbound lane on Princes Road and better priority both ways on Parkway. There are walking and cycling improvements too, and the existing subway would go.

## The case for it

According to the council's business case modelling, drivers would get through the junction 53% quicker, buses about 40% faster, cyclists 44% quicker and people on foot about 11% quicker. In October 2023 the Department for Transport agreed to put £68.75m from its Major Road Network fund towards the scheme, subject to conditions. The county council and Chelmsford City Council are funding the rest.

## The objections

Published in April 2026, the compulsory purchase order covers 11 plots of land in Chelmsford and Great Baddow. A side roads order covers the changes to the roads around the junction. The council published the orders in case it could not agree terms with landowners. Objections cover land acquisition, environmental mitigation, speed limits, a business car park and land for the bigger Sandon Park and Ride, Chelmsford City Hub reports.

## What happens next

When the hearing ends, the inspector reports to the Transport Secretary, who decides whether to confirm the orders. Building is now expected to start in 2028 and finish in 2031, subject to final funding approval and other agreements, Essex Highways said in March. In 2023 the council expected work to start in spring 2025 and finish in 2028.

The scheme has already been trimmed. Costs rose with construction inflation, and in March the council dropped a planned 500-space expansion of Chelmer Valley Park and Ride, though its land stays safeguarded.

The hamburger was the most popular design: 60% of more than 840 people who answered the council's 2021 consultation chose it. Councillors granted planning permission on 22 November 2024, and the government confirmed the scheme would go ahead in July 2025 after its spending review. The inspector's pre-inquiry note, which sets out how the inquiry will run, and the inquiry documents are on the Essex Highways Army and Navy page.
