Thurrock told to rewrite its SEND plan by end of November or miss out on government debt relief

The Department for Education has deferred approval of Thurrock Council's special educational needs strategy, putting help with a deficit heading for £15.3m on hold.

Grays & Thurrock · Friday 6 November 2026, 14:53 · Reported by Thurrock Nub News
WhatsApp Facebook X

Thurrock Council has until the end of November to revise and resubmit its strategy for children with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND), after the Department for Education deferred approval of the original. Until a plan is approved, the council cannot tap a government package designed to clear most historical SEND deficits, which matters for families and for the council's stretched budget.

The council is forecasting a £10.3m SEND overspend this year, with its accumulated deficit projected to reach £15.3m. More than 90% of councils had their strategies approved, and Thurrock is among a small number asked to try again. A scrutiny meeting last month heard that the cost of Education, Health and Care Plans and a heavy reliance on expensive out-of-borough specialist placements are major pressures.

Thurrock MP Jen Craft said the first plan did not meet the required standard and that the focus must now be on getting the revised submission right, including spotting needs earlier and better support for families. She has written to council leader Cllr Richard Bingley and children's services director Sheila Murphy offering help, and says she will keep working with parents and carers before the deadline.

Key points

  • Revised SEND plan due by end of November
  • Approval unlocks help clearing most historical SEND debt
  • £10.3m overspend forecast this year
  • Deficit projected to reach £15.3m
