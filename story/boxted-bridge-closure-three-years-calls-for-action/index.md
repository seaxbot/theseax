# Boxted Bridge load assessment published as closure passes three years

**Politicians on both sides of the Stour want action on the Victorian bridge near Colchester, which has been shut to drivers since June 2023.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Boxted, Essex, England. Published: Saturday 21 November 2026, 06:00 (UK time). Updated: Sunday 22 November 2026, 21:05. Section: News. Topic: Transport.

## Key points

- Shut to vehicles since 30 June 2023
- Path through the centre safe for walkers and cyclists, 2025 test found
- Replacement plan deferred by county planners in April 2025

## The story

Essex Highways has published its latest structural load assessment of Boxted Bridge, the 1897 crossing of the River Stour on Wick Road between Boxted and Suffolk, as pressure grows to settle its future. The county council says drivers must stay off it until the assessment of how much weight it can carry is complete, because it has no evidence yet that the bridge could safely take vehicles again.

The bridge was shut to everyone in an emergency closure on 30 June 2023, after an inspection found corrosion, cracking and deformation that had first been recorded in 2018. A load test in September 2025 found that a two-metre path through the centre could safely reopen to people on foot and on bikes, and the council consulted on that in January and February 2026. Its preferred long-term answer has been to replace the bridge, a plan its planning committee deferred in April 2025.

Bernard Jenkin, MP for Harwich and North Essex, has told the East Anglian Daily Times that council members need to "get a grip". He wants more testing to see whether the old bridge can be kept. Residents say traffic is being pushed through nearby villages. Campaigners from the Save Our Bridge group raised £12,300 for an independent load test, which the council did not allow. They say a freedom of information request showed Essex Highways spent £756,640 on plans for the bridge between 2016 and December 2025. The council says it sent further technical reports to its planners in October.
