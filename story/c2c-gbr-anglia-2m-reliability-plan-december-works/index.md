# c2c gets £2m plan to fix delays

**Rail bosses are spending more to fix the Southend line after months of delays. Some December trains will finish early or be replaced by buses.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Southend-on-Sea, Basildon, Upminster, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 15:40 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Transport.

## Key points

- Tamping machine works nights between Upminster and Benfleet
- Buses replace Leigh-on-Sea to Shoeburyness trains on 5 and 6 December
- Upminster to Pitsea via Laindon shut on 13 December

## The story

GBR Anglia, which now runs c2c alongside Greater Anglia and Network Rail's Anglia route, announced the extra £2m on Thursday 26 November. The money is meant to make trains between London and south Essex more reliable after a run of delays and cancellations this autumn.

Network Rail said the driest summer in 50 years dried out and shrank the clay under the tracks. That forced temporary speed limits on parts of the route. Signalling and power failures, a major fire at Stratford in August and a freight train derailment at Barking in October added to the trouble.

A Thameside Recovery Programme team has already laid more than 8,000 tonnes of ballast. In December a tamping machine, which packs the stones beneath the rails, will work through the night between Upminster and Benfleet. The aim is to lift the last speed limits before the new timetable starts on Sunday 13 December. Engineers are also strengthening the power supply so faults are found and fixed faster.

## What it means for passengers

Buses replace trains between Leigh-on-Sea and Shoeburyness on 5 and 6 December while lifts are fitted at Chalkwell and Southend East stations. From 7 to 11 December the last trains from London leave earlier than usual, such as 9.52pm via Laindon and 9.28pm via Tilbury. Some first trains into London run later from 8 to 11 December.

On 13 December the line between Upminster and Pitsea via Laindon is closed for engineering, with buses running between Pitsea and Shoeburyness. Trains on the Tilbury and Rainham lines still run direct to London. Network Rail said times may change and advised passengers to check c2c or National Rail before they travel.

Martin Beable, chief operating officer at GBR Anglia, said: "We know saying 'sorry' isn't enough." The company is also adding station staff, cleaning trains four times as often and planning more station work next year.
