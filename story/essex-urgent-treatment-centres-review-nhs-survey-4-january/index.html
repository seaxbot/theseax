Essex urgent treatment centres under review

Essex · Friday 27 November 2026, 10:09 · First reported by NHS Essex · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

NHS Essex wants to hear where people go when they need help fast. Its survey will shape a new plan for urgent care.

NHS Essex is reviewing the county's urgent treatment centres and minor injuries units, and wants to hear from patients before it decides what happens to them.

Urgent treatment centres sit between a GP and A&E. They see problems that need treating the same day but are not life-threatening, such as sprains, suspected broken bones, cuts, minor burns and eye or skin problems. Most treat adults and children aged two and over.

In Essex they include the centres at Colchester Hospital, Clacton Hospital and Princess Alexandra Hospital in Harlow, alongside a minor injuries unit at Thurrock Community Hospital in Grays, according to NHS Essex.

The health service says Essex sees more than 730,000 A&E attendances a year. Many people who turn up do not need an emergency department, and NHS Essex wants more of them seen closer to home so A&E can focus on the most seriously ill.

A new public survey asks where people would go when they need medical help quickly, what happened when they used local services and what would make care easier to reach. It is open to everyone, whether or not they have used urgent or emergency care recently.

The answers will feed into an urgent and emergency care strategy, due in the first half of 2027. It will look at options for the future of urgent treatment centres across the county. NHS Essex says any changes would need more planning, public engagement and formal approval first.

Samantha Goldberg, executive director of commercial, resilience and estates at NHS Essex, said: "We know how important it is for people to get help quickly when they are unwell or injured." She said the aim was a more consistent and joined-up approach across Essex, with emergency services kept free for those who need them most.

The review follows an earlier NHS Essex survey of 2,200 people. They said they wanted faster access to care, services closer to home and clearer information about where to go for help.

In August, NHS Essex set out plans to move Thurrock's minor injuries unit from Orsett Hospital to Thurrock Community Hospital, backed by £1m. In the longer term it wants a full urgent treatment centre there.

The survey takes about 10 minutes and is on the Essex Virtual Views website. It closes at 5pm on Monday 4 January 2027. Anyone who would prefer a paper copy can email essexicb.getinvolved@nhs.net.

If you are unwell and unsure where to go, NHS 111 can advise by phone or online at any time. In an emergency, call 999.
