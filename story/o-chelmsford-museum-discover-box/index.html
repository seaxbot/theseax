Great Baddow's Iron Age gold hoard goes into classrooms in new Museum of Chelmsford history kit

Primary schools can now hire a hands-on box built around the 935 gold coins found in Great Baddow in 2020, designed with help from local pupils.

Great Baddow, Chelmsford · Wednesday 4 November 2026, 09:00 · Reported by Chelmsford City Life (Chelmsford City Council)
WhatsApp Facebook X

The Museum of Chelmsford has launched a hands-on history kit that lets primary pupils investigate one of the county's most striking finds, the Great Baddow Coin Hoard. The Baddow Discover Box is aimed at Key Stage Two classes and turns the 935 Iron Age gold coins found in Great Baddow in 2020 into a set of lessons on Chelmsford's prehistory.

The box holds 21 Iron Age activities, from handling replica artefacts to weighing up the evidence about who buried the hoard and why, and can be used as eight separate lessons or combined into an Iron Age Day. Pupils and teachers at Beehive Lane Community Primary School helped design and trial it with creative practitioner Hannelore Smith, and Westlands Community Primary and Moulsham Junior schools tested it in early 2026. It was funded by the National Lottery Heritage Fund through the Timeless Treasures project.

Schools can hire the box by the half term, with the next slot available from January 2027, and schools outside Chelmsford and Essex can borrow it too. Councillor Jennie Lardge, the city council's deputy leader, called it "all at once fun, inspiring and educational". Prices and other boxes, covering the Romans, Victorian toys and the Suffragettes, are listed on the museum's website.

Key points

  • 935 Iron Age gold coins were found in Great Baddow in 2020
  • 21 activities for Key Stage Two pupils
  • Next hire slot opens in January 2027
  • Funded by the National Lottery Heritage Fund
