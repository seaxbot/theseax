Ex-Thurrock finance chief Sean Clark tells BBC reporter to 'go away'

The former finance director behind Thurrock Council's borrowing refused to answer questions about the borough's lost millions, a day after the BBC traced solar businessman Liam Kavanagh to Bucharest.

Grays, Thurrock · Tuesday 17 November 2026, 06:20 · Updated Wednesday 18 November 2026, 12:40 · Reported by BBC News
WhatsApp Facebook X

Sean Clark declined to answer questions about Thurrock Council when BBC reporter Catrin Nye approached him, and told her to "go away", in footage BBC News published on the afternoon of Tuesday 17 November. Earlier the same day BBC reporters said they had found Liam Kavanagh, the businessman known as "Mr Sunshine", at an exclusive apartment complex in northern Bucharest; his lawyers confirmed the address but said he did not want to speak to the BBC.

Clark, then Thurrock Council's finance director, arranged the borrowing from other councils that left Thurrock owing more than £1bn at one point, with more than £600m put into Kavanagh's solar farm companies against warnings that the deals were too risky. The Financial Reporting Council found Clark recklessly gave misleading information to councillors; he admitted professional misconduct, was severely reprimanded and excluded from his accountancy body for five years. Thurrock Council is suing Kavanagh for deceit, alleging millions were diverted for his personal benefit, which he denies.

The Serious Fraud Office is investigating suspected fraud involving Kavanagh's businesses and the council, and told The Bureau of Investigative Journalism on 17 November 2026 that it had no update to give; no one has been charged. Thurrock Council's debt stood at £862m in March, down from £1.5bn two years earlier, and the council has permission to borrow almost £70m in 2026/27 for day-to-day costs, more than 90% of it to pay interest and repayments on existing loans, the Bureau reports. The BBC reporting is part of the Radio 4 series Intrigue: Chasing Mr Sunshine.

Key points

  • Kavanagh's company was Rockfire Capital, Thurrock Nub News reports
  • Thurrock forecasts a £3.5m overspend this year, mainly on adult social care, the Bureau reports
  • Kavanagh denies the council's deceit claim
