# Wickford man jailed over £30m cocaine pick-up from the sea

**Cameron Weedon helped fishermen fetch 374kg of cocaine dumped in the Channel. A gang leader from Rainham was also jailed.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Published: Friday 27 November 2026, 17:05 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Court.

## Key points

- Drugs dropped in the Dover Strait on 10 November 2024
- Boat sailed from Queenborough on the Isle of Sheppey
- Border Force cutter HMC Vigilant helped stop the crew

## The story

Cameron Weedon, 27, of Innes Close, Wickford, was jailed for four years at Maidstone Crown Court on Friday 27 November. He played a part in a plot to recover cocaine worth an estimated £29.9m from the Dover Strait.

The National Crime Agency said the 374kg of cocaine was thrown into the sea from a shipping container in the early hours of 10 November 2024. It was wrapped in waterproof packaging fitted with trackers. Two skippers from Sheerness, Leonard Steadman, 66, and Wayne Bath, 47, used those trackers to find the packages. They worked from a small fishing boat, the Duke IV, which sailed from Queenborough Harbour on the Isle of Sheppey.

Weedon went out with them as a recovery specialist, passing on instructions and helping to pull the drugs aboard. He admitted encouraging or assisting offences, believing one or more would be committed.

## The gang leader

Elvis Mula, 38, an Albanian national of New Road, Rainham, was described by the agency as the ringleader. It said he tracked the drugs across borders before the drop and directed the two skippers. In the weeks before, they made practice runs, some lasting up to 13 hours at sea.

Mula, Steadman and Bath all admitted conspiracy to import cocaine. Mula and Bath were each jailed for 14 years and three months, and Steadman for 13 years and seven months.

## How they were caught

NCA officers had been running a long covert investigation. Working with Border Force officers on the cutter HMC Vigilant, they stopped the boat off the Kent coast, while other officers arrested Mula at the same time.

Jules Harriman, the NCA's senior investigating officer, said the haul was "a significant loss for the crime group". He added that its profits could no longer pay for more crime and that the drugs never reached the streets.
