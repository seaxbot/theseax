New rules for licensed pet and animal businesses across Basildon borough adopted until 2031

Basildon Council has formally adopted an Animal Activity Licensing Policy and Animal Charter for 2026 to 2031, replacing its old Animal Welfare Charter after a public consultation.

Basildon · Friday 13 November 2026, 06:20 · Original reporting from council papers
WhatsApp Facebook X

Basildon Council's Licensing Committee formally adopted a new Animal Activity Licensing Policy and Animal Charter for 2026 to 2031 on Monday 2 November 2026. The policy covers how the council licenses, inspects and takes enforcement action against licensed animal activities across all wards of the borough, which includes Basildon, Billericay and Wickford.

The report says the new policy replaces the council's previous Animal Welfare Charter and is intended to promote high standards of animal welfare and a consistent, transparent and proportionate approach to licensing. After a public consultation, the Licensing Committee backed amendments on 17 September 2026, including stronger provisions on intelligence sharing and recognition of the role played by reputable animal rescue and rehoming centres. The council's Cabinet endorsed the policy on 15 October 2026 and referred it back for final adoption.

The report says licence fees depend on the length of licence granted, which in turn depends on the risk rating of the premises, with lower risk premises generally granted longer licences. The adopted policy and charter are published with the committee papers, with the consultation amendments highlighted.

Key points

  • Adopted by the Licensing Committee on Monday 2 November 2026
  • Runs from 2026 to 2031 and replaces the previous Animal Welfare Charter
  • Endorsed by Cabinet on 15 October 2026 after public consultation
  • Lower risk premises generally get longer licences
