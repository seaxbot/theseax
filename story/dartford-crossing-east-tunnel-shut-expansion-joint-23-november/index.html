Dartford tunnel shut: 90-minute delays and eight miles of queues

Thurrock · Updated Monday 23 November 2026, 10:40 · First published Monday 23 November 2026, 07:37 · First reported by National Highways East · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

National Highways said shortly after 9am that the east tunnel at the Dartford Crossing remained closed because of an infrastructure defect, with delays of 90 minutes on the approach and building, and eight miles of congestion. The A2 was jammed in both directions on the approach to the Darenth Interchange and diversion routes were in place. No reopening time had been given by mid-morning.

The damage, to an expansion joint immediately in front of the tunnel entrance, was found during planned maintenance overnight. That work was due to finish at 5am, but the tunnel could not safely reopen. National Highways said specialist repair crews were being brought in on Monday morning and the closure would stay in place until the repairs were complete and the tunnel was confirmed safe.

The east tunnel carries two lanes of the anticlockwise M25, from junction 1a at Dartford to junction 31 at Lakeside, KentOnline reported. With it shut, every vehicle heading north into Essex is being squeezed through the older west tunnel, which has a 4.8m (15ft 9in) height limit, so taller lorries have to find another route. By 7.37am KentOnline was already reporting queues back to junction 3 of the M25 at Swanley and along the London-bound A2 past Gravesend.

The crossing is the only road over or under the Thames east of London. National Highways says it was designed for 135,000 vehicles a day but now averages about 150,000, with up to 180,000 on the busiest days, and almost 40% of them are lorries and other goods vehicles. The west tunnel opened in 1963, the east tunnel in 1980 and the QEII Bridge, which takes southbound traffic, in 1991. The nearest alternative, the Blackwall Tunnel, is about eight miles to the west.

The west tunnel is already booked to close overnight, from 9pm to 5.30am, from Monday 23 to Thursday 26 November for planned maintenance, according to National Highways' Dartford Crossing works page. It is not yet clear whether those closures will go ahead if the east tunnel is still shut this evening.

Relief is still years away. Work has started on the Lower Thames Crossing between Thurrock and Kent, which National Highways says will almost double road capacity across the Thames east of London and take 13 million vehicles a year off the Dartford Crossing. Early work began in March 2026 and the new road is due to open in the early 2030s. Until then, drivers are being told to plan ahead, allow plenty of extra time or avoid the crossing if they can. Key points

  • A2 jammed both ways towards the Darenth Interchange
  • Lorries over 4.8m cannot use the west tunnel
  • Crossing normally carries about 150,000 vehicles a day
