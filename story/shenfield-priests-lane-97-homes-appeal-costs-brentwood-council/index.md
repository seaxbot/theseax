# Brentwood Council must pay Cala's costs after losing Priests Lane appeal

**An inspector allowed 97 homes on a former school playing field near Shenfield, overturning a refusal made against planning officers' advice.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Shenfield, Priests Lane, Brentwood, Essex, England. Published: Friday 27 November 2026, 17:10 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Property.

## Key points

- Appeal allowed on 22 October
- Committee had refused it 11 votes to none
- 34 affordable homes, none with four bedrooms

## The story

Brentwood Borough Council has been ordered to pay a developer's full appeal costs after a planning inspector overturned its refusal of 97 homes off Priests Lane, between Brentwood and Shenfield.

Inspector B Pattison allowed the appeal by Cala Management Limited on 22 October, the Planning Inspectorate's case record shows. A separate costs decision issued the same day awarded Cala its full costs, the planning website Planning Geek reported. The amount has not been set and must be agreed or assessed.

The site is a former school playing field allocated for housing in the council's Local Plan. The scheme includes 34 affordable homes, about 35 per cent: 12 with one bedroom, 15 with two and seven with three.

The council's planning committee refused it on 10 February by 11 votes to none, with one abstention. Its reason was the affordable housing mix, which has no four-bedroom homes, against Local Plan policies HP01 and HP05. The housing association Ark said it could not support the mix, and council housing officers agreed.

Planning officers had told the committee that, on balance, there was not enough justification to refuse. The site is allocated, no statutory consultee objected, and an inspector had already examined an almost identical scheme, the minutes record.

That earlier version was dismissed on appeal in September 2025, because one plot would have overlooked a home in St Andrews Place. Cala redesigned that plot as a bungalow.

The new inspector found the 2022 South Essex Housing Needs Assessment showed most need was for one and two-bedroom affordable homes, with about 1 per cent of Brentwood's affordable need for four bedrooms. Ark's report was given limited weight.

Costs were awarded because the council treated two very similar schemes inconsistently and relied on contradictory evidence, Planning Geek reported. The mix had not been a reason for refusing the first scheme.

Residents and ward councillors had objected over density, road safety on Priests Lane and the loss of an oak tree at the site entrance. Cala has now submitted further details, including external materials and habitat management, The Brentwood Brief reported.
