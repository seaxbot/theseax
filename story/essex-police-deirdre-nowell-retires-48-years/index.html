One of Essex Police's first female detective sergeants retires after 48 years

Essex · Saturday 21 November 2026, 09:20 · Reported by Essex Police
WhatsApp Facebook X

Deirdre Nowell joined the force in 1978, when a woman officer's uniform came with a handbag and an allowance for tights.

She followed her older brother, Kevin, into Essex Police. The force describes her as a self-confessed maverick with a sharp sense of humour and a dedication to high standards, and calls her retirement the end of an era.

Her early years were eventful. One of her first jobs for CID involved having to drink in a student bar, and in her first year she was sent flying through the window of a shop in Colchester after being hit by a car.

A newspaper cutting from her recruit days, shared by the force, shows her in the front row of a new intake under the headline "Almost half recruit squad are women". It names her as the most successful recruit at training school and says she was posted to Colchester.

Later in her career she became an innovator. Her work on HOLMES, the computer system police use to manage information during major investigations, and on the National Police Chiefs' Council's Casualty Bureau saw her ideas adopted across the country.

She is a former winner of the force's Liam Brigginshaw Award for outstanding commitment, which she received in 2024.

Essex Police shared the news of her retirement on its Facebook page on Saturday morning, and says more about her career is on its website.

Key points

  • Hit by a car and thrown through a Colchester shop window in her first year
  • Her ideas for the HOLMES major investigation system were adopted nationally
  • Followed her older brother into the force
