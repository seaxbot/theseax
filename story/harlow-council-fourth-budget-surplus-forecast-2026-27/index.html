Harlow Council on track for fourth budget surplus in a row

Harlow · Monday 23 November 2026, 11:39 · Reported by Harlow Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

The council has kept its share of council tax frozen since 2021, and says its credit rating has been upgraded to AA−.

Harlow Council expects to finish 2026/27 with money to spare in its main day-to-day budget for the fourth year running. The forecast is in a financial monitoring report that went to the council's Cabinet last week. The budget in question, the General Fund, pays for most everyday council services, and a surplus means income is expected to more than cover spending.

The council describes the forecast surplus as small but healthy. Over the same period, the council's overall budget, which includes spending on council homes and rebuilding the town centre, has grown from £173 million in 2024/25 to about £350 million in 2026/27.

The council says it ended each of the past three financial years in surplus and met all its key financial tests in 2025/26. It has kept its share of council tax at the same level since 2021, and says an independent credit rating has been upgraded to AA−, putting Harlow ahead of comparable councils.

It puts the results down to making its teams more efficient, cutting costs that add little for residents, reviewing spending regularly, earning more from its commercial property and other assets, and tracking every agreed saving and income target.

Councillor Dan Swords, the council's leader, said: "When residents ask me how we can afford to rebuild the town centre, improve council homes and renew our neighbourhoods, I think the answer starts with how we run the council. Every pound belongs to the people of Harlow. When we cut an unnecessary cost, free up staff time or earn more from a property we own, we can put those resources to work on the things residents have asked us to deliver."

Councillor James Leppard, cabinet member for finance, said: "We take nothing for granted. Delivering a fourth surplus means keeping that discipline right through to the end of the financial year. If costs rise or income falls short, we act early."

The freeze covers only Harlow Council's part of the council tax bill. In 2026/27 its share for a Band D home is £288.90, out of a total of £2,298.69 that also pays for Essex County Council, Essex Police and Essex Fire and Rescue Service, the council's own figures show. The same figures put the council's net borrowing at about £297 million on 31 March 2025.

Key points

  • Overall budget up from £173m in 2024/25 to about £350m in 2026/27
  • Harlow's share of a Band D bill is £288.90 of £2,298.69
  • Forecast went to the council's Cabinet last week
