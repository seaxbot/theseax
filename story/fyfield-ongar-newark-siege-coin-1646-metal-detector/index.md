# Fyfield field gives up rare 1646 Civil War siege coin

**A detectorist has found a rare Civil War siege coin near Ongar. He fears he may not be allowed to keep it.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Fyfield, Ongar, Essex, England. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 06:31 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: History.

## Key points

- Found by Lukasz Cedzidlo of the Metal Detectives Group
- Newark's Royalist mint cut its coins from silver plate
- The British Museum is due to examine the find

## The story

Lukasz Cedzidlo, of the Metal Detectives Group, found the coin in a field at Fyfield, near Ongar. It is stamped "OBS Newark" with the year, and his group believes it was made during the siege of Newark in the First English Civil War. BBC News reported the find on Wednesday 25 November.

Newark-on-Trent was a Royalist stronghold, and its last siege began in November 1645. Cut off and short of money, the defenders set up their own mint and made coins from donated silver plate. "OBS" is short for the Latin obsidium, meaning siege. The town surrendered in May 1646 on the king's orders.

A small hole near the top of the Fyfield coin suggests someone once wore it, perhaps to show loyalty to the crown, the group believes. Mark Becher, who founded the Metal Detectives Group 16 years ago, said a find like this makes you "the first person to touch it" in centuries.

## What happens next

Under the Treasure Act 1996, a possible treasure find must be reported to the local finds liaison officer within 14 days. The BBC reported that the British Museum is due to examine the coin before it goes to a museum in Essex. Mr Cedzidlo told the BBC he would like to keep it but is not sure he will be allowed to.
