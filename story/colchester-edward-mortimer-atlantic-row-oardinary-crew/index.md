# Colchester's Edward Mortimer to row the Atlantic with three friends

**A Colchester man and his crewmates will row unsupported from the Canary Islands to the Caribbean in December. They are raising money for charity.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Colchester, Essex, England. Published: Saturday 28 November 2026, 09:44 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Community.

## Key points

- Fundraising target of £250,000
- Backed by the Civil Service Sports Council
- Each rower faces more than 1.5 million oar strokes

## The story

Edward Mortimer, 29, from Colchester, is one of four friends who will set off from La Gomera in the Canary Islands in December. Their goal is to row about 3,000 miles to the Caribbean, as first reported by the Daily Gazette.

The four call themselves The Oardinary Crew. Alongside Mr Mortimer are Jamie Gregory from Norfolk, Jasper Upton from near Newmarket in Suffolk and Matt Riall from Dorset. According to Suffolk News, they are taking part in the World's Toughest Row in a carbon-fibre boat named Stephen, about nine metres long.

## Life on the ocean

The East Anglia Gazette reported that the crew expect to spend about 40 days at sea with no support boat. They will row in shifts of two hours on and two hours off, often sleeping for less than 90 minutes at a time.

Each man is expected to make more than 1.5 million oar strokes. They will burn 6,000 to 7,000 calories a day and could lose up to a tenth of their body weight. "We are four friends with jobs, families and everyday commitments," Mr Upton said.

## Who they are rowing for

The crew is raising money for East Anglian Air Ambulance, which rescued two of Mr Upton's close family members. The other two charities are The Giving Tree, based in Higham in Suffolk, and James' Place, which supports men in crisis.

Suffolk News reported a fundraising target of £250,000 when the crew launched the challenge in March. Since then the Civil Service Sports Council has backed them through its Community Lottery Fund. They have trained for about two years.
