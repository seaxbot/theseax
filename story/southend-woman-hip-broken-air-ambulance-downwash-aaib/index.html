Southend woman broke her hip when a medical helicopter's downwash blew her over

Air accident investigators say the air ambulance crew could not see her behind a hedge as they took off from a field near her home in Southend.

Southend-on-Sea · Friday 13 November 2026, 11:29 · Updated Monday 16 November 2026, 13:21 · Reported by ITV News Anglia
WhatsApp Facebook X

The woman was filming the helicopter outside her house in Southend on the evening of 28 February 2026 when its downwash blew her over, about 50 seconds into its climb from a field where the crew had attended an emergency. She told the BBC on Monday 16 November that she felt excruciating pain and had broken her hip.

The Air Accidents Investigation Branch (AAIB) found she had been standing behind a hedge that hid her from the crew, close to a parked car that may have funnelled the air and made it stronger. A relative helped her and she was taken to hospital, ITV News Anglia reported. The helicopter, weighing about 4,300kg, flew back to its North Weald base with no idea anyone had been hurt.

The operator only learned of the injury four days later, when a relative got in touch. Since then it has sent all its pilots an instruction on downwash risks when taking off and landing, and the AAIB says it does not believe anyone else was affected. The charity told the BBC its commander had asked bystanders in view to move back before take-off, and that it has offered the family support.

Key points

  • Gill, 66, a mother of three from Southend Road, told the BBC she has needed a hip replacement
  • The AAIB decided not to take action against Essex & Herts Air Ambulance
