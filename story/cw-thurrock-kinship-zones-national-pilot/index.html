Thurrock picked as one of seven national pilots helping children stay with their wider family

The Kinship Zones pilot will give practical, emotional and financial support to relatives raising children, as Thurrock reshapes its children's social care.

Thurrock · Saturday 7 November 2026, 10:41 · Original reporting from council papers
WhatsApp Facebook X

Thurrock has been selected as one of seven national Kinship Zones pilot authorities, councillors heard at the Children's Services Overview and Scrutiny Committee on 22 October. The pilot aims to help children stay safely within their wider family networks, with practical, emotional and financial support for kinship carers and fewer care proceedings.

It is part of the council's Families First Partnership programme, which aims to step in earlier and support whole families. Officers said ten Family Help Teams had been set up and a single assessment and planning framework introduced, and that work was under way on a Multi-Agency Child Protection Team bringing together professionals from partner organisations.

The committee also heard that 75% of initial health assessments for children entering care were now completed within the statutory 20 days, up from 36% previously. Officers credited closer joint working, more paediatric capacity and better oversight, and said a formal review of the new nurse-led and paediatrician model would take place in December 2026.

Key points

  • One of seven national Kinship Zones pilot authorities
  • Ten Family Help Teams now in place
  • 75% of health checks for children entering care now on time, up from 36%
  • Review of the new health assessment model due in December
