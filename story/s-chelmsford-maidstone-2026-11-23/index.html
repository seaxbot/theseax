Chelmsford City climb into play-offs as Barham penalty beats Maidstone

Chelmsford · Updated Tuesday 24 November 2026, 06:45 · First published Monday 23 November 2026, 21:47 · First reported by Chelmsford City FC · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

Former Stones striker Jack Barham scored for the third time in two days as City edged a tight Monday night game.

Chelmsford City moved up to seventh in National League South, and into the play-off places, after a 1-0 win over Maidstone United at the EMG Inspire Stadium on Monday night. Jack Barham scored the only goal from the penalty spot ten minutes after half-time, sending Lenny Holden the wrong way against the club he used to play for.

It was Barham's third goal in two days. He scored twice at Walton & Hersham on Saturday, when City were pegged back to 2-2 by two late goals. Manager Mark Cooper made two changes for the visit of the Stones, bringing back Leo Sery and captain Cav Miley.

Maidstone were much the better side early on. Ben Tompkins was booked inside three minutes for bringing down Riley Court on the edge of the box, and Court's free kick went just past the post. Jayden Davis then lobbed a shot onto the crossbar and Stan Leech headed the rebound wide.

City's goalkeeper Alfie Marriott kept them level, diving to keep out Court and pushing a fierce Dermi Lusala strike wide. The home side grew into the game, and Sery forced a save from Holden from close range after 31 minutes before an Ashley Clarke shot took a double deflection and flew just wide. Sery also went down in the box in first-half stoppage time, but the referee waved play on.

The penalty came in the 55th minute after a foul in the Maidstone area, and Leo Hamblin was booked soon afterwards. Maidstone pushed for an equaliser but managed little, with Noah Nzuzi's low shot from distance held by Marriott with 13 minutes left and Lusala firing wide in stoppage time.

The result was a second 1-0 defeat in three days for Maidstone, who had lost at Farnham Town on Saturday. Maidstone's own report said they were made to pay for failing to take the chances they created in the first half.

Key points

  • Alfie Marriott made the key saves, from Court and Lusala before the break
  • A crowd of 1,421 watched, with Ayrton Hursey refereeing
  • Four Chelmsford players were booked, to Maidstone's one
