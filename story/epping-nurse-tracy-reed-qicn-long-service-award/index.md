# Epping nurse Tracy Reed honoured for 27 years in community care

**A west Essex nurse has won a national long service award. She joined the NHS as a clerk at 17.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Epping, Essex, England. Published: Tuesday 24 November 2026, 08:30 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Health.

## Key points

- Queen's Nurse since 2012
- Specialised in end-of-life care for 15 years
- Volunteered in Nepal after the 2015 earthquake

## The story

Tracy Reed is clinical lead for end-of-life care at Essex Partnership University NHS Foundation Trust (EPUT). She has received the Long Service Award from the Queen's Institute of Community Nursing, a charity that works to improve nursing care for people at home. EPUT announced the award on Tuesday 24 November. She has spent more than 27 years in community nursing and 45 years in the NHS.

Her career began at Warley Hospital, where she worked as an administrative clerk. Her mum and sister were both nurses, but she had not thought of joining them. That changed after a first aid course, when she found herself helping a person who was having a heart attack.

## A career across west Essex

She went on to train as a nurse and worked in general medicine, coronary care, community nursing and care of older people. For the last 15 years she has specialised in end-of-life care. Much of her career was spent in west Essex, at St Margaret's Hospital in Epping, the Herts and Essex Hospital and Princess Alexandra Hospital in Harlow, and in the Epping, Loughton and Stansted communities. For the past 12 years she has worked across the county.

Ms Reed has been a Queen's Nurse since 2012, a title the institute gives to nurses who show a high level of commitment to patient care. She volunteered twice in Nepal after its devastating earthquake and has kept supporting health projects there. She is also an independent adviser on end-of-life care for the Motor Neurone Disease Association and a Macmillan Professional with Macmillan Cancer Support.

"I realised a long time ago being a nurse is not what I do but who I am," she said. She added that she felt lucky to have cared for and worked with so many people.
