# Epping Forest District Council backs plan to prevent homelessness

**A new five-year plan aims to stop people in the Epping Forest district losing their homes. Requests for help are up a quarter since 2022.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Epping, Essex, England. Published: Friday 27 November 2026, 14:46 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Politics.

## Key points

- 1,357 households asked for help in 2025/26
- New customer services system approved
- Debt recovery policy also updated

## The story

Epping Forest District Council's cabinet approved a new Homelessness and Rough Sleeping Strategy at its meeting on Thursday 26 November. It sets out how the council and its partners will prevent homelessness, tackle rough sleeping and find better homes for residents over the next five years, to 2031.

Demand has grown sharply. The council says 1,086 households came to it for help with homelessness in 2022/23, and 1,357 did so in 2025/26. That is 271 more households a year. The plan leans on three things: stepping in early, before people lose their homes, working with partners, and getting more people into suitable housing. The previous strategy was written to cover 2022 to 2027.

Councillors also approved spending on a new communications system for the council's customer services. The council said digital tools will matter more in future, but residents will still be able to get help in person and in other ways that do not need a computer.

The meeting opened with a row over transparency. Council leader Cllr Chris Whitbread responded to concerns after public questions about Qualis Commercial, the council's own company, were turned down. He said the council was committed to openness and proper scrutiny, and that its constitution is designed to avoid repeating issues already dealt with at public meetings. Cabinet also took reports on budget monitoring, risk management and an updated debt recovery policy.
