# Tendring councillors to vote on sending Local Plan for 1,069 homes a year to a government inspector

**Tendring District Council's local plan committee meets on 17 November to decide whether to recommend submitting the plan, with Kirby Cross, Brightlingsea, Bradfield and Frinton sites drawing most objections.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Tendring, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 12 November 2026, 06:24 (UK time). Section: Council Watch. Topic: Council Watch.

## Key points

- Committee meets Tuesday 17 November 2026
- Plan must provide for 1,069 homes a year
- About 45 objections to 450 homes north of Thorpe Road, Kirby Cross
- 2,600-name petition to protect Lawford Tye

## The story

Tendring District Council's Planning Policy and Local Plan Committee will meet on Tuesday 17 November 2026 to hear the results of the final public consultation on the district's new Local Plan, held in September and October 2026. Councillors are asked to recommend to Full Council that the plan be submitted to the Secretary of State for independent examination by a planning inspector. Officers say they remain of the view that the plan is legally compliant and sound.

The report says government targets require Tendring to plan for 1,069 homes a year, nearly double the figure in its previous plan. The council aims to submit before the end of 2026, even though the government has extended the cut-off to the end of March 2027, to avoid restarting under a new system. Fewer people responded than at the previous stage, when 628 respondents made about 2,200 comments, mainly because the council dropped two of the most opposed sites, at Brightlingsea and Walton.

The most opposed remaining site is the planned 450 homes north of Thorpe Road, Kirby Cross, with about 45 objections, and the report notes Taylor Wimpey has been consulting on up to 500 homes there, with an application possible in December. A 2,600-name petition, around 500 of them from the Manningtree, Lawford and Mistley area, backed keeping Lawford Tye as protected open space, and 12 of Tendring's 27 town and parish councils responded. The final decision to submit rests with Full Council, and the inspector may require changes before the plan can be adopted.
