Hanningfield Reservoir hits record low of 35% as hosepipe ban goes on

Hanningfield, Chelmsford · Tuesday 24 November 2026, 18:15 · First reported by BBC News · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

Hanningfield, near Chelmsford, is at its lowest level on record. It would normally be about twice as full at this time of year.

Kieran Ingram, a director at Essex & Suffolk Water, told BBC Look East on Tuesday 24 November that Hanningfield Reservoir was at 35% of its capacity. In late November the company would expect it to hold 65 to 70%.

He said customers' supplies were not at risk, but added: "We're not out of the woods yet." The company has once again asked people to limit the water they use.

Why it is so low

Essex & Suffolk Water brought in its first hosepipe ban since 1997 on 3 August, after an exceptionally dry summer. Autumn rain has disappointed, so the ban is still in place. Rainfall from May to October was 61% of the average for those months, the company says.

Its own figures, published on its water resources page and dated 24 November, put Hanningfield and Abberton together at 38% full, which it describes as below average. BBC News reported that the two reservoirs held 83% in June. The firm has already put more crews on to finding and fixing leaks, as The Seax reported on 18 November.

Mr Ingram said leakage was now 12% of the water the company produces, the lowest on record, after more than 8,500 repairs. Household use is down 7% and business use down 6% since before the ban, according to the company, which supplies about 1.8 million people.

What happens next

To refill the reservoirs, the region needs about 20% more rain than usual over the next six months, Essex & Suffolk Water says. Mr Ingram warned that use can climb in winter, when people wash salt and grit off their cars and take longer showers. The company updates its reservoir figures once a month.

Key points

  • Hosepipe ban in force since 3 August, the first since 1997
  • Rain from May to October: 61% of average
  • About 20% more rain than usual needed over six months
  • Leakage at a record low of 12% of water produced
