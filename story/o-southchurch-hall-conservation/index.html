Medieval Southchurch Hall shuts until mid-2027 for £423,000 conservation work

The Grade I listed moated manor house in Southend has closed to visitors while its roof, windows and stonework are repaired.

Southchurch · Friday 30 October 2026, 10:00 · Reported by Southend-on-Sea City Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Southchurch Hall, the Grade I listed medieval manor house in Southend, closed to visitors on 1 November for a major conservation project. Southend-on-Sea City Council says the building is expected to stay shut until around the middle of 2027 while the work is carried out.

The £423,105 programme is funded by Arts Council England's Museums Estate and Development Fund and covers repairs to the roof and windows, conservation of the masonry, better drainage and refurbished visitor toilets. The moated hall dates from the mid-14th century and stands on the site of an earlier Saxon hall; it was a branch library from the 1930s before becoming a museum in the 1970s.

Councillor Matt Dent, cabinet member for culture, tourism and business, described the project as "once-in-a-generation conservation works" that will also improve the visitor experience. Southend Museums plans to share progress updates and behind-the-scenes stories on its social media channels while the hall is closed.

Key points

  • Closed to visitors from 1 November 2026
  • Reopening provisionally mid-2027
  • £423,105 from Arts Council England
  • Hall dates from the mid-14th century
