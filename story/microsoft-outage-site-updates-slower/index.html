Global Microsoft outage hits Outlook, Teams and other services worldwide

Microsoft is still struggling to restore email, video calls and other office tools for businesses and public bodies around the world.

Essex · Friday 13 November 2026, 09:30 · Updated 09:35
WhatsApp Facebook X

Many Essex workers logged on this morning to find their email and video calls not working, after a global Microsoft outage that began once most had gone home on Thursday evening.

The monitoring site Downdetector first showed a spike in UK reports of problems with Microsoft Teams at around 6.15pm. Reports of issues with Outlook alone peaked at about 140,000 worldwide.

Microsoft blames an "elevated load" after many of its servers became unexpectedly unavailable. It said it had got servers running several times overnight, only for them to crash again. Engineers have tried isolating parts of the company's infrastructure and moving traffic onto other routes, and by mid-morning Microsoft was reporting only limited progress in patching servers.

Its status page lists Exchange Online, Microsoft Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft Copilot 365 and Microsoft Defender XDR as potentially affected. Businesses and public bodies have been hit around the world, with Australia's spy agency and federal police among the organisations caught up in it.

The disruption is not confined to Microsoft's own products. By 9.17am on Friday, Downdetector's list of the most-reported services showed rising complaints about Gmail, Google, WhatsApp, Zoom, Vodafone, NHS England and even the 999 emergency number, alongside Outlook, Teams, Microsoft 365, OneDrive, Copilot, Xbox and Azure. Downdetector counts reports from users rather than confirmed faults, so a spike can reflect people struggling with a Microsoft service further down the line.

There is no sign of foul play. Microsoft executive vice-president Charles Lamanna said the outages did not appear to be the result of a cyber attack.

Sources: TechRadar, LADbible, CNN, Downdetector.
