# Funded PhD to tell the story of Marianne Straub, Braintree's Tube seat designer

**The University of Essex and Braintree's Warner Textile Archive want a researcher to explore the Swiss-born weaver whose fabrics covered London Underground seats and the QE2.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Wivenhoe, Colchester, Essex, England. Published: Saturday 21 November 2026, 12:00 (UK time). Updated: Sunday 22 November 2026, 20:45. Section: News. Topic: Education.

## Key points

- Applications close at midday on 10 February 2027
- Stipend of £21,805 a year plus fees, for three years from October 2027
- Includes cataloguing 11 boxes of Straub's diaries and letters

## The story

The University of Essex is offering a fully funded three-year doctorate on Marianne Straub, one of the most influential British textile designers of the 20th century. She spent two decades designing for Warner & Sons in Braintree. The project, Fabric of Everyday Life: Marianne Straub, Textile Innovation and Design, 1950-1970, is a partnership with the Warner Textile Archive, which is owned by Braintree District Museum Trust.

The university says Straub's work remains underexamined by academics despite how widely it was used: on the London Underground, London buses, in airports, public buildings and homes. She set out to make well-designed, practical fabrics that ordinary people could afford.

## From Switzerland to Great Bardfield

Born in Amriswil, Switzerland, in 1909, Straub came to England in 1932 to study power-loom weaving in Bradford, because Swiss technical colleges did not then admit women. She advised 72 Welsh woollen mills in the 1930s, became head designer and later managing director of Helios, and joined Warner & Sons in 1950.

Her Surrey fabric, based on a crystal structure, was shown at the Festival of Britain in 1951, and she lived among the artists of Great Bardfield from 1953. Her blue and green moquette, known simply as Straub, was used on London Transport buses and trains from 1969, including the Piccadilly line extension to Heathrow. Her fabrics for Tamesa also went into the QE2 liner and BEA's Trident airliners. She taught at the Central School of Art, Hornsey and the Royal College of Art, was made a Royal Designer for Industry in 1972, and died in 1994.

## What the researcher will do

Whoever takes the post will be the first to work through the Marianne Straub Collection, which the archive is acquiring: 11 boxes including her personal diaries and her letters to leading figures in industrial design. They will catalogue it to a professional standard and combine it with Warner's own design books, samples and business records.

Funded through the Essex AHRC Doctoral Landscape Awards, the post pays a stipend of £21,805 a year plus tuition fees, with up to £9,000 for travel to the archive. The student will spend a day a week at the archive in Braintree and be based at the university's Colchester campus. It is open to UK and international applicants, with interviews planned for early March 2027.

The Warner Textile Archive is the largest publicly owned collection from a luxury textile manufacturer in the UK, and University of Essex students already research its designers on placements there.
