Greater Anglia and c2c target under-16s as crossing misuse rises 65%

Essex · Updated Monday 23 November 2026, 10:55 · First published Monday 23 November 2026, 09:35 · First reported by Network Rail and GBR Anglia · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

A crossing near Upminster has been shut for almost eight weeks after children were filmed doing handstands and playing chicken with trains.

Rail chiefs have launched a safety campaign aimed at 10 to 15 year olds after a sharp rise in children and teenagers putting themselves in danger at level crossings on the lines that serve Essex. GBR Anglia, the joint team running Network Rail Anglia, c2c and Greater Anglia, backed the new phase of the national No Second Chances campaign on Monday 23 November. It warns young people about wearing headphones or looking at phones while crossing, and about filming or playing chicken on crossings.

Network Rail's Anglia route covers the lines out of Liverpool Street and Fenchurch Street through Essex and East Anglia. Its figures show incidents involving young people there rose from 37 in 2017/18 to 61 in 2025/26. More than 2,000 incidents were recorded at Anglia crossings over the past three years, and 153 of them, about 8%, involved under-18s. A further 42 have been logged so far in 2026/27, a provisional figure.

The campaign follows the emergency closure of Brickfields level crossing, just east of Upminster, where a footpath crosses the c2c line through the Cranham Brickfields nature reserve. About 230 trains a day use the line. The crossing was shut on 30 September after a camera survey caught young people doing handstands on the tracks and deliberately playing chicken with oncoming trains. The closure was first set for 21 days but has been extended, and the crossing will stay shut while staff visit local schools.

Shenel Bullock, who leads level crossing safety for GBR Anglia, said when it closed that the railway "cannot ignore the significant risks posed by this behaviour". She urged parents and carers to talk to their children about railway safety.

Across Britain, more than 11,000 safety incidents have been recorded at or near level crossings since 2024, and children and young people account for more than one in ten, Network Rail says. No Second Chances began in April with three 20-second films aimed at 18 to 34 year olds. An earlier level crossing campaign cut near misses by 69% at the crossings it targeted. Daniel Fisk, Network Rail's head of level crossings and public safety, said a familiar crossing "doesn't reduce the risk".

The dangers are well known in Essex. Two teenage girls were killed by a train at the crossing beside Elsenham station in 2005. Network Rail was later fined £1m, built a footbridge and now locks the gates when a train is approaching. In October, GBR Anglia fitted red and green lights and audible warnings at two footpath crossings: Spring Lane, between Hythe and Wivenhoe, and Woodgrange Close, between Southend East and Thorpe Bay.

Network Rail's advice is to stop, look and listen at every crossing, take headphones off, put phones away and cross quickly without stopping. British Transport Police asks anyone who sees unsafe behaviour on the railway to text 61016.

Key points

  • 153 of more than 2,000 Anglia crossing incidents in three years involved under-18s
  • Warning lights fitted in October at crossings near Wivenhoe and Thorpe Bay
  • Report unsafe behaviour to British Transport Police by texting 61016
