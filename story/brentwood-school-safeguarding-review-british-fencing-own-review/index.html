Brentwood School review finds fencing coach likely abused pupils for years

An independent review says the school failed to act properly on concerns about Gareth Stafford Bull, who coached fencing there in the 1980s and 1990s. British Fencing will now hold its own inquiry.

Brentwood · Friday 20 November 2026, 17:30 · Updated Friday 20 November 2026, 19:10 · Reported by Brentwood School independent review
WhatsApp Facebook X

A fencing coach at Brentwood School was able to abuse children there during the 1980s and 1990s, an independent review has found, and the school failed to deal properly with concerns about him both at the time and for years afterwards.

Gareth Stafford Bull, a former pupil who became the school's fencing instructor in about 1982, was suspended and then sacked for gross misconduct in April 1997, after police told the school that pupils had raised concerns about him. He died in May 1997 before the police investigation could go further, so he never faced trial. The review's findings about him are made on the balance of probability.

The 59-page review was commissioned by the school's governors after David Pickthall, the school's former director of music, was jailed for 12 years in November 2024 for sexual offences against pupils and former pupils. It was led by independent chair Louisa Spicer and independent reviewer Jane Foster (Sarre), and the school published it this month. The Seax has read the full report.

Governing body minutes from April 1997, quoted in the review, record that police enquiries had found prima facie evidence of indecent behaviour by Stafford Bull involving members of the school fencing team, particularly on trips away. The concerns included encouraging boys to shower after competitions and joining them. The headmaster at the time judged the evidence against him to be incontrovertible.

Former pupils told the review the abuse happened on what one called "an industrial scale", including on residential trips, where Stafford Bull travelled alone with pupils and sometimes shared rooms with them. Contributors also alleged that he abused other boys when he was himself a pupil at the school. The review says the full extent of his offending is not known and that more victim-survivors may come forward.

The review is critical of how the school responded after his death. Former pupils contacted the school in 2008, 2017 and 2024. In 2008 the headmaster of the time replied that he had seen no evidence that Stafford Bull had sexually assaulted boys, which the review says contradicts the evidence in his own personnel file and in governing body minutes. It found the school's responses to former pupils were inadequate and lacked empathy, were not followed up, and that there is no record of support being offered to those affected.

The review found no evidence that Pickthall and Stafford Bull worked together, but says the prestige both men brought, as a renowned musician and a fencing coach, may have made school leaders reluctant to believe concerns about them. On Pickthall, it found the school did not investigate anonymous allegations properly in 2007, 2009 and 2014, and that a misplaced personnel file meant earlier warnings were not seen when concerns were raised again.

The review also heard what it called credible accounts of abuse by other staff in the 1960s and 1970s, including harsh physical punishment, and of abuse by pupils. It says the staff named in those accounts have died or cannot be traced.

In a foreword, the chairman of governors, Lord Black of Brentwood, who was a pupil at the school in the 1970s and early 1980s, offers a "deep, sincere and unreserved apology" to everyone abused in the school's past. He says the governors have accepted the report and all its recommendations, which include the school publicly acknowledging the harm caused and that it failed to detect abuse during the 1980s, 1990s and 2000s. The review says safeguarding at the school today is strong and well resourced, after checks in 2022 and 2025.

British Fencing said on Friday 20 November that it would commission its own independent safeguarding review into the role, if any, of the sport's governing body and its predecessor, the Amateur Fencing Association. According to the review, Stafford Bull also coached the England under-20 fencing team, and a former pupil asked the school in 2017 whether the sport's authorities had been told in 1997.

Outside the school, Stafford Bull coached at Wickford Fencing Club from 1979 to 1987, according to the club's history page, which lists him among its previous coaches. As a Brentwood pupil he fenced for the school, finishing third in the boys' senior sabre at the 1974 Public Schools' Fencing Championships. The review makes no findings about his time at the Wickford club.

Brentwood School says victim-survivors can contact the headmaster or the chairman of governors in confidence. British Fencing has a form for reporting a safeguarding concern on its website. Adults affected by abuse in childhood can call the NSPCC on 0808 800 5000, and Samaritans is available any time on 116 123.

Key points

  • Suspended and sacked in 1997 after police passed on pupils' concerns
  • Later complaints from former pupils were not followed up, the review says
  • Governors apologise and accept every recommendation
