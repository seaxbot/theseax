Essex ambulance service rated good after 2025 warning notice

Essex · Wednesday 25 November 2026, 06:00 · First reported by Cambridge Independent · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

Inspectors found quicker call handling, more staff and better training. The East of England Ambulance Service answers 999 calls across Essex.

The Care Quality Commission has rated two core parts of the East of England Ambulance Service good, the Cambridge Independent reported on Wednesday 25 November. One is the emergency operations centre, where 999 calls are answered and crews are sent out. The other is urgent and emergency care, the ambulances and crews that reach patients across Essex and the rest of the region.

Both services were rated good in all five areas the inspectors looked at. The trust is no longer in breach of the rules on safe care and staffing. It has also met every demand set out in the warning notice. Inspectors pointed to better call handling and dispatch, improved ambulance response times and higher staffing levels. Mandatory training, staff appraisals and safeguarding had all improved too. They also singled out the trust's Cardiac Arrest Desk.

How far the service has come

The regulator served the warning notice in February 2025, when it rated the trust as requiring improvement. It cited failings on staff training, staffing levels, call waiting times and the handling of controlled drug incidents. Its concerns also covered the culture of the service and how managers acted on staff feedback.

Problems had built up over several years. After inspections in April and May 2022, the CQC said ambulance response times were well below national standards. It also found staffing shortages that affected patient safety, and said not enough had been done to tackle a culture of bullying.

Neill Moloney, the trust's chief executive, said the findings recognised 'the enormous amount of work' staff had put in. He said there were still areas to work on. Leadership and management across urgent and emergency care must be made consistently effective, he said.

Key points

  • Good in all five areas inspected, for both services
  • Cardiac Arrest Desk singled out by inspectors
  • 2022 inspection found response times well below standard
  • Trust says leadership in emergency care still needs work
