# Essex ambulance crews go from inadequate to good, inspectors say

**Inspectors found faster 999 answering, more staff and better care. But ambulances are still stuck queuing outside hospitals.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Essex, Essex, England. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 06:00 (UK time). Updated: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 08:20. Section: News. Topic: Health.

## Key points

- Rated good in all five areas, for both services
- Demand up 19% since 2022, says the regulator
- 90% of older people who fall treated at the scene, against about 55% nationally
- Chelmsford control room among those inspected

## The story

The East of England Ambulance Service, which answers 999 calls across Essex, has been rated good by the Care Quality Commission. It comes 21 months after the regulator served it with a warning notice. The Cambridge Independent, BBC News and Suffolk News all reported the findings on Wednesday 25 November.

Its urgent and emergency care, the crews and ambulances that reach patients, has gone from inadequate to good. The emergency operations centres, where 999 calls are answered and crews sent out, have moved up from requires improvement. The trust as a whole is now rated good.

Both services were rated good in all five areas inspectors look at: whether care is safe, effective, caring, responsive and well led. The control rooms are no longer breaking the rules on safe care and staffing. Urgent and emergency care has met every demand in the warning notice.

## What inspectors found

Inspectors spent three days at the service in July. BBC News reported that they visited six ambulance stations and the control rooms in Bedford, Norwich and Chelmsford, and spoke to about 105 staff. They found quicker call handling and dispatch, better response times and more staff. Mandatory training, appraisals and safeguarding had all improved.

That came as the service got busier. The CQC found demand for ambulances in the region had risen by 19% since 2022. According to the BBC, most of the growth was in category 2 and 3 calls. Category 2 includes suspected strokes, seizures and burns. Category 3 covers less serious burns, women in late labour and people with diabetes.

Since its last inspection the trust has set up a Cardiac Arrest Desk. It puts 999 callers on a live video link to an advanced paramedic, who can talk them through CPR before the ambulance arrives. Inspectors said it was helping people get effective CPR sooner and supporting survival rates.

The BBC also reported that 90% of older people who call after a fall are now treated at the scene, compared with about 55% nationally. Compliments to the service outnumber complaints by nine to one.

## The problem that remains

The reports are not all good news. Ambulances are still being held in queues outside hospitals, and handover delays were rated the trust's highest operational risk. While a crew waits to hand over a patient, that ambulance cannot answer another 999 call. Suffolk News reported that response time targets for some patients were missed over the winter.

## How far the service has come

The regulator served the warning notice on 10 February 2025. It cited failings on staff training, staffing levels, call waiting times and the handling of controlled drug incidents. Its concerns also covered the culture of the service and how managers acted on staff feedback.

Problems had built up over several years. After inspections in April and May 2022, the CQC said ambulance response times were well below national standards. It also found staffing shortages that affected patient safety, and said not enough had been done to tackle a culture of bullying.

## What they said

Neill Moloney, the trust's chief executive, said the findings recognised 'the enormous amount of work' staff had put in. He said the trust still had to make sure leadership and management were consistently effective across urgent and emergency care.

Sarah Ivory Donnelly, the CQC's deputy director of hospitals in the East of England, said the trust 'should be happy with the improvements they've made'.
