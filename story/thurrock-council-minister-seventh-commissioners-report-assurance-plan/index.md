# Thurrock Council 'substantially stronger' but not yet fixed, minister says

**A minister says Thurrock Council has improved greatly since commissioners were sent in during 2022. But its finances remain a major challenge.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Grays, Thurrock, Essex, England. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 14:33 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Politics.

## Key points

- No third commissioner will be appointed for now
- Next commissioners' report is due in spring 2027
- Objections to the Tilbury land sales close at 5pm on 27 November

## The story

Baroness Taylor of Stevenage, the housing and local government minister, gave the government's verdict on Wednesday 25 November. Her letter replies to the seventh report from the commissioners sent in to run parts of Thurrock Council. She agreed with their finding that the council is far stronger than when the government intervened, with the clearest gains in leadership, governance, financial management and grip on the organisation.

Her letter, published on GOV.UK, warns that recovery is not yet secured. Thurrock must set and deliver a balanced budget for 2027/28, backed by a pipeline of savings that recur year after year. It must keep paying down its debt and finish rewriting its constitution, its financial procedure rules and its scheme of delegation. She also wants the council's change programme to move to what she calls "disciplined delivery, clear accountability" and measurable results.

Commissioners Gavin Jones and Denise Murray proposed an assurance plan setting out the evidence needed before the government steps back any further, and the minister backed it. It will set measures and milestones that give the council and ministers a shared route to ending the intervention. She also decided not to appoint a third commissioner for now, though that stays under review. The commissioners' next report is due in the spring and will help decide the intervention's future.

Thurrock's finances collapsed in 2022, and selling land and buildings is part of its recovery plan. The council has a £104.1m target for asset sales over five years. Four more council plots in Tilbury are proposed for sale, including nine acres north of Parker Avenue, [as The Seax reported on 13 November](https://theseax.com/story/tilbury-four-council-land-plots-sale-objections/). Objections can be lodged until 5pm on Friday 27 November.

The council has other deadlines too. Earlier this month the Department for Education told Thurrock to rewrite its special educational needs strategy before help with its deficit could be released, [as The Seax reported on 7 November](https://theseax.com/story/thurrock-send-strategy-deferred/). In January, answering the sixth report, ministers let Thurrock recruit its own chief executive, Local Government Lawyer reported.
