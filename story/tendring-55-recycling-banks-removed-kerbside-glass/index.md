# Tendring to remove 55 recycling banks after kerbside glass rounds begin

**Bottle banks across Tendring are going. Homes can now put glass out with their recycling instead.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Clacton-on-Sea, Essex, England. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 09:25 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Environment.

## Key points

- Banks go in stages through December and January
- Glass, Tetra Pak cartons and more plastics collected from homes since October
- Contract to run the banks will not be renewed

## The story

Tendring District Council will take away all 55 of the recycling banks it runs across the district during December and January. The council announced the change on Wednesday 25 November, saying the banks are no longer needed now that glass, cartons and more plastics are collected from people's homes.

Since October, households have been able to put glass bottles and jars, Tetra Pak cartons and extra types of plastic out with their regular kerbside recycling. The rounds are run by the council's contractor, Veolia. Council figures show 281.4 tonnes of glass was collected in October, from the kerbside and the banks together. That compares with 165.8 tonnes in September, a rise of about 70%.

Doorstep glass collections began after [the council sent out 70,000 extra recycling boxes](https://theseax.com/story/cw-tendring-glass-joins-kerbside-recycling-boxes/), and the first month's rounds were [covered by The Seax earlier this month](https://theseax.com/story/o-tendring-glass-recycling-first-month-200-tonnes/).

Councillor Adrian Smith, cabinet member for environment, said people across Tendring had been doing "a fantastic job in recycling" and had stopped using the bottle banks. He said the council will not renew the contract to provide them, a decision he said "provides best value for the taxpayer". He also hopes it will cut fly-tipping, because rubbish is often dumped beside the banks.

## What happens next

Where a town or village has more than one bank, they will be taken away in stages, so people still using them have time to adjust. Posters have gone up on the banks, and the council has written to the owners of the land where they stand. The council says the move follows the government's Simpler Recycling rules. Residents can check what goes in which container on the council's Rubbish and Recycling web page.
