# Essex energy bills to rise 26% in January as price cap hits £2,166

**Homes across Essex on standard tariffs face dearer gas and electricity from New Year's Day. The regulator blames conflict in the Middle East.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 07:51 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Business.

## Key points

- Applies from 1 January to 31 March 2027
- About 20 million homes on standard tariffs affected
- Fixed-deal customers protected until their deal ends
- Direct Debit saves about £160 a year, Ofgem says

## The story

Energy regulator Ofgem announced on Wednesday 25 November that its price cap will go up for the three months from 1 January to 31 March 2027. For a typical home using gas and electricity and paying by Direct Debit, the yearly figure rises from £1,723 to £2,166. That is £443 more.

The cap limits the unit rates and standing charges suppliers can set on default tariffs. It is not a ceiling on the total bill, so homes that use more energy pay more. Ofgem said about 20 million households on standard variable tariffs will be affected. About 10 million on fixed deals are protected until their deal ends.

## Why prices are going up

Ofgem said continued conflict in the Middle East is pushing up international gas prices. Neil Kenward, its director general for markets, said the conflict "continues to drive energy costs higher". Gas is rising faster than electricity, the regulator said, because more of Britain's power now comes from renewables rather than gas.

## Help with bills

Ofgem said the government will give about 20 million households £100 towards their energy bills from January. In Basildon alone, about 65,000 homes in council tax bands A to D are in line for that payment, [as The Seax reported on 21 November](https://theseax.com/story/basildon-100-household-energy-payment-65000-homes-bands-a-d/).

Anyone struggling to pay should contact their supplier, which must offer support and advice, Ofgem said. It has written to suppliers asking them to spot people who need help early and to keep repayment plans affordable. Moving to Direct Debit saves the average customer about £160 a year, according to the regulator.

## What happens next

Ofgem reviews the cap every three months, so the next change will apply from 1 April 2027. Households on a standard tariff can also look at fixed deals, the regulator said, and the Energy Saving Trust gives advice on cutting energy use.
