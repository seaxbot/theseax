# Hatfield Peverel man, 26, jailed for five years for sexual assaults

**David Cooney is now on the sex offenders' register. The woman he assaulted says she finally has closure.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Hatfield Peverel, Essex, England. Published: Tuesday 24 November 2026, 12:09 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Court.

## Key points

- Sentenced on Tuesday 17 November
- Cleared of rape and attempted rape at a 2025 trial
- Free support: Synergy Essex, 0300 003 7777

## The story

David Cooney, 26, of Church Road, Hatfield Peverel, was jailed for five years at Chelmsford Crown Court on Tuesday 17 November, Essex Police said. He was also placed on the sex offenders' register indefinitely.

The woman he assaulted says her 'world was destroyed' by what he did. In a statement released by the force, she said: 'He got what he deserved. I feel like he tried to ruin my life and my reputation.'

'I was very mentally unwell after what he did to me,' she said. 'It affected every area of my life. I lost my home and everything fell apart.'

## How the case unfolded

An investigation began in 2022, after the woman reported a sexual assault. Cooney was arrested on suspicion of rape, attempted rape and sexual assault. He denied the allegations when detectives interviewed him.

Detectives charged him with all three offences. At a trial in August 2025, a jury found him not guilty of rape and attempted rape. That jury was discharged on the other counts.

A retrial was held at Chelmsford Crown Court in October. This time the jury found him guilty of two counts of sexual assault by penetration.

## 'Consequences are very clear'

She found the court process hard, she said. But she praised the investigating officer, Detective Sergeant Jade Lawton, and her independent sexual violence advisers, Jemma and Claire. 'What an amazing job they did,' she said.

She also thanked her family for believing in her over four years, and herself 'for not giving up'.

'My attacker thought he could just sexually assault me, take what he wanted without consequences,' she said. 'Well this week the consequences of those actions are very clear.'

## 'True strength'

Det Sgt Lawton said the woman had shown 'true strength' through a long investigation. She had no faith in the police before the assault, the officer said, so reporting it and staying with the case 'was no mean feat'.

'She stood up to the perpetrator by coming to the police and has prevented him from hurting anyone else,' Det Sgt Lawton said. She urged anyone struggling with something similar to reach out to support services.

## Where to get help

Synergy Essex offers free, confidential support to anyone affected by rape, sexual abuse or sexual violence, whether it happened recently or long ago. You do not need to report it to the police first.

Its services include counselling in person, online or by phone, practical and emotional help, and independent sexual violence advisers. Its First Contact Navigator team can help with next steps on 0300 003 7777.

By law, the woman has lifelong anonymity.
