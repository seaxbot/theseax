FeatureSouth Ockendon

An eight-mile ramble along the Mardyke, one of Essex's least-known rivers

A countryside circuit from South Ockendon's round-tower church follows the Mardyke Way past fields, solar farms and wildlife.

South Ockendon · Reported by Essex Life (Great British Life)
WhatsApp Facebook X

Essex Life's latest walk starts at St Nicholas of Myra Church on The Green in South Ockendon and loops for about eight miles along the Mardyke Way. The Mardyke, sometimes written Mar Dyke, is a small, overgrown river in a wide, flat valley crossed by the M25 and A13 near junction 30.

South Ockendon has been a village since at least Saxon times, with signs of Roman settlement near South Ockendon Hall. Its church keeps fine Norman details and is one of only six in Essex with a round tower. The route heads out past the Royal Oak pub and the hall before crossing the river by a metal footbridge.

Ockendon station is about 500 metres from the start along West Road. Davy Down Riverside Park, just west of the route, has parking and toilets.

Key points

  • About eight miles, starting at St Nicholas of Myra Church
  • One of six round-tower churches in Essex
  • Ockendon station 500m from the start
