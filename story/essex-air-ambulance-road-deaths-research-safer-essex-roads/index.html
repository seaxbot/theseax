Air ambulance charity to study how to cut Essex road deaths

Essex · Thursday 26 November 2026, 08:28 · Reported by Your Harlow
WhatsApp Facebook X

Essex & Herts Air Ambulance will study Essex crash data to learn how more lives could be saved. Road safety money is paying for it.

Essex & Herts Air Ambulance has won funding from the Safer Essex Roads Partnership to find out what more could be done to stop people dying on the county's roads. Your Harlow reported the grant on Thursday 26 November. The money comes from the partnership's Road Safety Intervention Funding.

What the research will look at

The charity will go through road crash records with the police, the highways authority and the fire and rescue service, starting with five years of figures. Researchers will look at the injuries people suffered and when they got medical help. The aim is to see whether new or faster treatment could change the outcome for future patients.

It builds on work the charity already does. After every death on one of its missions, its team reviews the case with a forensic pathologist.

Who is involved

Dr Gareth Grier, the charity's associate medical director, leads its Centre for Excellence, which will oversee the project. An academic research fellow is being recruited to run it. “We know that dispatching an air ambulance can save vital minutes,” Dr Grier said.

Roger Hirst, Police, Fire and Crime Commissioner for Essex, chairs the partnership's governance board. He said every minute counts when a serious incident happens. Volunteers from the charity's patient and public involvement group, including bereaved families, will help shape the work.

The bigger goal

The partnership launched its Vision Zero strategy in November 2020. It aims for no deaths or serious injuries on roads in Essex, Southend and Thurrock by 2040, and to halve them by 2030. At the launch, the air ambulance charity said 42 people had died on Essex roads in 2019. The charity has not said when the research will report its findings.

Key points

  • Five years of crash data will be studied first
  • A research fellow is being recruited to lead it
  • Bereaved families will help shape the work
