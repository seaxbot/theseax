# Brightlingsea promenade at Splash Point to reopen before Christmas

**Brightlingsea's seafront path by the paddling pool is being resurfaced. The first stretch should be open again by Christmas.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Brightlingsea, Essex, England. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 13:19 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Environment.

## Key points

- Limited or no access at Splash Point from Monday 30 November
- Phase two starts in the first week of February 2027
- About five to six working weeks still needed, around the tides

## The story

Tendring District Council said on Wednesday 25 November that the first phase of work on the promenade around the paddling pool at Splash Point in Brightlingsea is nearly finished. The new surface is meant to make the seafront path easier to use, deal with years of wear and tear and drain better when it rains.

There will be limited or no access around Splash Point during the week starting Monday 30 November, while workers join the new surface to the existing stretch of promenade. Weather permitting, the council expects the first section to be fully open before Christmas. Councillor Adrian Smith, the cabinet member whose brief covers coast protection, said the aim was to have it "fully accessible before Christmas".

The project has taken longer than first planned. When Tendring District Council announced it in September, it said work would start in October and finish in November. The plan was to resurface whole sections rather than patch holes, with the path sloping gently from the sea wall towards the beach and raised slightly to make it easier for people with mobility problems.

## What happens next

The rest of the work has to fit around the tides, and there are no suitable working tides in January. The second phase is due to start in the first week of February 2027, with work on alternate weeks and a break for the Easter holidays. The council says it needs about five to six working weeks in total. Councillor Mick Barry, cabinet member for leisure and public realm, said he was pleased the council had secured the money for the work; the council has not said how much it costs.
