Essex County Council asks residents where to spend as it faces a £170m funding gap next year

County Hall's consultation on its 2027/28 budget runs until 6 December, with the Reform-led council blaming government funding changes for one of its hardest budgets this century.

Chelmsford · Friday 6 November 2026, 12:00 · Reported by Essex County Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Essex County Council has opened a public consultation on its budget for 2027/28, asking residents, businesses and community groups which services matter most to them and where money should be focused. The council says it faces a funding gap of about £170 million next year, and describes the coming budget as one of the toughest it has faced this century.

Council leader Peter Harris said the choices made now would shape the county for years and argued that the government's funding settlement had not been fair to Essex taxpayers. Bo Davis, the cabinet member for finance, said the council expects cuts of more than £50 million a year after the national funding review, or about £200 million over four years, while demand and costs keep rising, adding that "doing nothing isn't an option".

The consultation closes on 6 December 2026 and can be completed on the council's consultation website. The council says the responses will feed into its 2027/28 budget proposals, which the cabinet is due to consider on 15 January 2027, alongside a review of how the organisation works.

Key points

  • Funding gap of about £170m forecast for 2027/28
  • Council says government funding cuts total over £50m a year
  • Consultation closes on 6 December 2026
  • Cabinet to consider budget proposals on 15 January 2027
