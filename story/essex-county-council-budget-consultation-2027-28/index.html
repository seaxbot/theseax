Essex County Council asks residents to help close a £170m gap in one of its toughest budgets this century

The county council has opened a month-long consultation on its 2027/28 budget, saying government funding changes will cost it more than £50m a year.

Chelmsford · Friday 6 November 2026, 12:00 · Reported by Essex County Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Essex County Council has launched a public consultation on its budget for 2027/28 and beyond, describing it as one of the toughest it has faced this century. Residents, businesses and community groups are being asked what the council should prioritise and where its money should be focused.

The council says it faces a funding gap of around £170 million in the coming year. It blames cuts following the government's Fair Funding review, which it puts at more than £50 million a year, or £200 million over four years. Cabinet member for finance Bo Davis said doing nothing was not an option and every pound had to work harder.

The consultation closes on 6 December 2026 and is open online through the council's consultation website. Council leader Peter Harris said choices made now would shape Essex for years to come, and the council says it is also reviewing how it operates and where it focuses its resources.

Key points

  • Funding gap of around £170m next year
  • Council says Fair Funding changes cost over £50m a year
  • Consultation closes on 6 December 2026
  • Covers the 2027/28 budget and beyond
