Epping Forest councillors asked to back raising the top fly-tipping fine from £1,000 to £5,000

A motion to full council called for the district's maximum fixed penalty for fly-tipping to rise fivefold now that the law allows it.

Epping · Monday 9 November 2026, 06:20 · Original reporting from council papers
WhatsApp Facebook X

A motion put to Epping Forest District Council's full council meeting on 22 October 2026 asked councillors to support, in principle, raising the council's maximum fixed penalty notice for fly-tipping from £1,000 to £5,000. It was proposed by Councillor K Rizvi and seconded by Councillor C McCann. The published papers do not yet record the outcome.

The motion says the council can currently issue fines of up to £1,000 for fly-tipping, but recent changes to the law now allow councils to set a maximum of £5,000. It argues that people who deliberately dump waste should face a penalty reflecting the damage to the environment and the costs borne by residents.

If backed, the motion asks the Cabinet Member for Environment and Regulatory Services to work with the council's environmental enforcement team to bring in the higher fine as soon as practicable, subject to legal and constitutional requirements. It also calls for clear public communication and continued enforcement, and encourages residents to keep reporting fly-tipping with photographs, CCTV or dashcam footage where they have it.

Key points

  • Current maximum fly-tipping fixed penalty: £1,000
  • Proposed maximum: £5,000
  • Motion considered at full council on 22 October 2026; outcome not yet published
