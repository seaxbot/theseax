# Chelmsford MP backs Paul's Law after Birch care home choking death

**Marie Goldman wants every care worker trained to spot and manage swallowing problems, after a jury found failures in the care of Paul Spearing, who choked on unsuitable food at his care home near Colchester and died in November 2023.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Birch, Colchester, Essex, England. Published: Monday 23 November 2026, 19:53 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Politics.

## Key points

- The petition runs until 17 May 2027
- The letter asks for CQC regulation to change as well as training
- The care home's procedure for sharing a buzzer delayed help, the jury found

## The story

Ms Goldman, the Liberal Democrat MP for Chelmsford, and Barking's Labour MP Nesil Caliskan wrote to Health and Social Care Secretary Yvette Cooper on Thursday 19 November. They want national training on dysphagia, cerebral ataxia and choking risks written into the government's coming Social Care Workforce Action Plan, and Care Quality Commission rules updated to match. The campaign, launched by Paul's family, is called Paul's Law.

## The law now

Care providers in England must already make sure their staff are properly trained. But there is no national training standard for care workers on dysphagia awareness or choking prevention, the MPs' letter says. Dysphagia is a condition that makes swallowing food and drink unsafe.

## What happened to Paul

Paul Spearing had cerebral palsy, a learning disability, autism, cerebral ataxia and dysphagia. He had lived for more than 20 years at a home run by Essex Care Consortium in Birch, needed one-to-one care day and night, and relied on staff for everything he ate and drank.

Speech and language therapists had advised in 2018 and 2019 that he must not have mixed-texture food or thin drinks such as milk. At breakfast on 12 November 2023 he was given a croissant softened in milk and choked. He died at Colchester General Hospital three days later, aged 41.

## What the jury found

An eight-day inquest at Essex Coroner's Court, before Senior Coroner Sonia Hayes, ended on Friday 11 September. The jury found the meal broke his speech and language guidance and caused his death. It said the home's mealtime guidance was inadequate and inconsistent, and staff did not know his needs well enough.

His assessment called dysphagia training essential, but not everyone caring for him had been given it. The jury heard the course cost £14 a person, while the home was paid £3,672 a week for his care, his family's barristers' chambers, 1 Crown Office Row, reported. The jury also found his room, in a separate pod, was not linked to the emergency buzzer system, which delayed first aid.

The coroner ruled that Article 2 of the European Convention on Human Rights, the right to life, applied. That means an inquest looks more widely at how a death happened. The MPs say this is unusual for a privately run care home, and they have asked the Department of Health and Social Care to consider what families lose when it is not applied.

## The petition

A petition on the UK Parliament website, started by Rachel Uddin, asks for compulsory dysphagia training for staff supporting people with learning disabilities and other vulnerable adults. It says the condition affects one in five older adults. It opened on Tuesday 17 November and had 425 signatures by the evening of 23 November. The government replies to petitions that reach 10,000 signatures, and Parliament considers a debate at 100,000.

Sharing the letter on Facebook, Ms Goldman said: "No family should have to endure what Paul's family has."
