# Anglia Ruskin study finds most dog attacks are preventable

**Owners losing control of their dogs is the biggest single cause of bites, university research for Battersea finds. Many breeds are involved.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Published: Friday 27 November 2026, 21:38 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Education.

## Key points

- Court cases studied ran from 2020 to 2025
- Survey and hospital data were also analysed
- Report backs a compulsory code of practice for owners

## The story

Most dog attacks on people in the UK could be prevented, according to research by Anglia Ruskin University (ARU), which has a campus in Chelmsford. The attacks involve a wide range of breeds, not only the banned types.

Battersea Dogs and Cats Home commissioned the report, called Safety Around Dogs: A Process Analysis of Dog Bites. ARU researchers studied court records, hospital admissions, policy papers and answers to a survey. The university did not say which of its campuses led the work.

Of 130 dog bite cases that reached court between 2020 and 2025, an owner failing to control their dog was blamed in 32%. Most of those were unprovoked bites on passers-by in public places. In 24 cases the dog had escaped from a home or broken free from a tether before it bit someone.

Adults, not children, were most of the victims in both the court cases and the hospital figures, even though public worry often centres on attacks on children. Of the people who answered the survey, 14% said they had been bitten three or more times.

The report calls for better teaching about how to behave around dogs, leads in busier public places and homes made safer for visitors, postal workers and delivery drivers. It also asks for stronger dog registration, closer links with microchipping and a compulsory code of practice for owners. Its authors said attacks "do not have a single cause" and that policy should focus on the situations where bites happen.
