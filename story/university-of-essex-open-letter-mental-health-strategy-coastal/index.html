University of Essex experts lead call for coastal focus in mental health plan

Colchester, Wivenhoe Park · Thursday 19 November 2026, 12:00 · Reported by University of Essex
WhatsApp Facebook X

Close to 150 researchers, health leaders and charities have signed a letter, coordinated from the University of Essex in Colchester, warning that England's new Mental Health Strategy could overlook coastal, rural and former industrial communities.

The letter to Alison McGovern, the minister for social care and mental health, is being coordinated by Professor Susan McPherson and Professor Victoria Bird of the university's Institute for Public Health and Wellbeing, the University of Essex announced on Thursday 19 November 2026. Their research found that young adults in the most deprived coastal areas are three times more likely to have undiagnosed mental distress than those in equally deprived inland areas.

Signatories include the chief executives of Mind, YoungMinds, Place2Be and the Centre for Mental Health, more than 60 professors and former care minister Sir Norman Lamb. Going by published affiliations, seven in ten contributors to the government's independent review of mental health conditions, ADHD and autism were based in London, Oxford or Cambridge, the letter notes. Professor McPherson said geography, as well as deprivation, can shape mental health outcomes.

The group wants the strategy to recognise geographical inequalities and to draw on research leaders, local partners and people with lived experience from the worst-affected places. It is also asking for funding so council public health teams can invest in community-led mental health work, and for the Prevention Concordat for Better Mental Health to be revived and refreshed.

Key points

  • Addressed to minister Alison McGovern
  • Coastal young adults three times likelier to have undiagnosed distress
  • Signed by more than 60 professors
