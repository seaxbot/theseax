Southend presses its case for five Essex councils as ministers weigh a ruling due by early 2027

Southend City Council is standing by the five-council model as the government weighs it against Essex County Council's three-council plan, with a decision promised by early 2027.

Chelmsford, Southend & Thurrock · Sunday 1 November 2026, 07:00 · Updated Wednesday 4 November 2026, 06:08 · Reported by East Anglia Bylines
WhatsApp Facebook X

Greater Essex is once again waiting for ministers to choose how its councils will be reorganised, after the government set out the first results of its review of the shake-up. The area now faces a choice between the three unitary councils backed by Essex County Council and the five-council model ministers chose in March, a decision that will shape who runs services such as bins, social care and planning for every resident.

Ministers originally picked five unitary councils for Essex, Southend and Thurrock in March, a model proposed by ten of the area's councils. After Reform UK took control of the county council in May it launched legal action, with leader Peter Harris calling the plan expensive and ill-thought. On 7 September the government withdrew its decisions for Essex and four other areas, citing updated legal advice, although no court had ruled them unlawful. Local Government Minister Jim McMahon announced the first review results on 29 October, East Anglia Bylines reports.

Southend-on-Sea City Council, which has consistently backed five councils, says it will keep making a robust case for that model during the review, arguing it best balances financial sustainability, local services and community identity; its leader, Councillor Daniel Cowan, said the update gives greater certainty about the timescales. Every area should know its outcome by early 2027. Elections to the existing councils go ahead in May 2027, the replacement councils would be elected in May 2028 and take over on 1 April 2029, and until then councils say services carry on as normal.

Key points

  • Southend backs five unitary councils for Greater Essex
  • Essex County Council favours three
  • Ministers due to decide by early 2027
  • Replacement councils would take over in April 2029
