# Brightlingsea councillors fight 550-home bid for Church Road land

**Brightlingsea's councillors oppose a bid for 550 homes on the town's edge. They want the land kept out of Tendring's new Local Plan.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Brightlingsea, Essex, England. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 15:47 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Property.

## Key points

- First proposal for 300 homes was removed from the draft plan
- Tendring must plan for 18,000 homes up to 2043
- Inspector's hearings expected in 2027

## The story

Brightlingsea's ward councillors have come out against a bid by Trinity College to have its land on the edge of the town included in Tendring District Council's updated Local Plan. Brightlingsea Town Council set out their opposition in a statement on Wednesday 25 November.

The college owns land on both sides of Church Road. It has put forward detailed plans to use a wider area there for 550 homes, the town council says.

## How we got here

Earlier this year the college's first proposal, for 300 homes, was taken out of the draft plan after local opposition. Tendring District Council said in June that it would remove the proposal for 300 homes on land east of Church Road after significant public objections and concerns raised by the town council. It said it would look instead at spreading growth across a number of smaller sites.

The Local Plan decides where new homes go across the district. Tendring must plan for 18,000 new homes up to 2043, after government targets almost doubled its yearly requirement from 550 homes to nearly 1,070.

## What councillors say

Councillor Jayne Chapman raised the issue at the district council's Planning Policy and Local Plan Committee on 17 November. Councillor Mick Barry spoke against the plans at the full Tendring council meeting the following Tuesday, 24 November.

## What happens next

The ward councillors say they will coordinate a response from the town council and community groups to back the district council's planning officers in opposing the land's inclusion. They plan to ask for more information and hold meetings in the town to gather local knowledge. That will feed into submissions to the independent planning inspector, whose hearings are expected in 2027.
