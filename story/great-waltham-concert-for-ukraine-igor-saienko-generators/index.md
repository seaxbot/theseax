# Great Waltham concert to fund generators for Ukraine's winter

**Ukrainian accordionist Igor Saienko plays the village church on 9 December. Entry is free, with donations going to a Felsted charity which helps fund generators for Ukraine.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Great Waltham, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 10:26 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: What's On.

## Key points

- Doors 7pm for a 7.30pm start
- Second concert at Stebbing on 10 December
- Donations buy portable generators

## The story

A leading Ukrainian accordionist is coming to Great Waltham next month to raise money for portable generators, as families in Ukraine face another winter with little heat or power.

Igor Saienko, a soloist with the National Philharmonic of Ukraine, plays Great Waltham Church on Wednesday 9 December. Doors open at 7pm for a 7.30pm start, and the programme mixes popular classics with festive music.

Entry is free, but the audience is asked to give towards the generators. Gift Aid is available, and refreshments will be served in the interval.

Organisers say many Ukrainians face the winter with limited heat and electricity, often in ageing apartment blocks where temperatures fall well below freezing.

## The music

Saienko plays the bayan, the button accordion of Eastern Europe. He was a member of the Mykola Rizol Bayan Quartet of the National Philharmonic of Ukraine from 2002 to 2023, and its artistic director from 2016 to 2019.

He also co-founded the Kyiv Classic Accordion duo and made his UK debut in Glasgow in 2023, with a chamber ensemble from the Royal Scottish National Orchestra. He has held the honorary title of Merited Artist of Ukraine since 2010.

He is touring the UK to raise money for the appeal. His second Essex date is at St Mary's Church, Stebbing, on Thursday 10 December.

## The Felsted charity behind the generators

The generators will be supplied by UK-Aid, the working name of Felsted Aid for Deprived Children. Alan Hilliar and Pauline Craven started the charity in 2001, and it became a registered charity in 2003.

It has supported the Revival Centre in Chernihiv, northern Ukraine, since 2001. The centre was set up to help children affected by the Chernobyl disaster, and now helps disabled and sick children get specialist treatment. The charity also sends humanitarian aid to vulnerable families.

In its latest accounts with the Charity Commission, UK-Aid reported three aid trips that took 98,624 kilos of supplies to Ukraine.

People who cannot get to the concert can donate on the UK-Aid website, adding "Generators" in the "Write a Note" box. The concert is organised by the Great Waltham Community Network.
