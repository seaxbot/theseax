# Nearly 100 Colchester households stuck in B&Bs as housing demand climbs

**Officers warn the city's homelessness budget will be heavily overspent this year as more families are placed in temporary homes.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Colchester, Essex, England. Published: Monday 16 November 2026, 05:00 (UK time). Updated: Monday 16 November 2026, 10:05. Section: News. Topic: Politics.

## Key points

- The city is above its own target for households in temporary accommodation
- Former student flats are being used as temporary homes
- Cabinet takes the council's half-year report on 2 December

## The story

Colchester had 588 households in temporary accommodation at the end of September, 98 of them in bed and breakfasts, according to the council's latest quarterly housing report, which goes before councillors shortly. The total was 550 three months earlier and 472 in mid-November last year, a rise of almost a quarter, the Daily Gazette reports. It puts the city at seven households in temporary accommodation for every 1,000, above the council's own target of six.

The council's half-year performance report, discussed by its scrutiny panel on 11 November and due before cabinet on 2 December, says 616 households facing homelessness were being supported by 31 October. Council leader Paul Dundas said demand for housing "remains extremely high". The pressure has built quickly: the council's own figures showed 326 households in temporary accommodation in March 2024 and 449 a year later, while Colchester Borough Homes, which runs the city's homelessness service, assessed 1,563 new homelessness cases in 2024/25.

The council has turned former private student flats into temporary homes, a scheme that won a bronze award at the Local Government Chronicle awards in June, and plans to buy 65 homes during 2026/27 to cut its reliance on costly temporary accommodation. It has previously said it uses B&Bs only when it is urgent and for short periods. Nationally, the government has committed to ending the unlawful use of B&Bs for families, and a new Colchester homelessness and rough sleeping strategy for 2026 to 2031 aims to step in before households reach crisis point.
