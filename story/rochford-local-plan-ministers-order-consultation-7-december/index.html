Rochford Local Plan must go to inspector by 31 March, minister rules

Rochford · Updated Tuesday 24 November 2026, 15:39 · First published Monday 23 November 2026, 18:00 · First reported by Rochford District Council · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

Housing minister Matthew Pennycook wants Rochford's housing plan examined within months. He says the district has too little land set aside for homes.

Rochford District Council must send its Local Plan to a government inspector for examination by the end of March 2027, the BBC reported on Tuesday 24 November. In his letter to council leader Cllr Adi Malviya, housing minister Matthew Pennycook said he was "not convinced" by the council's reasons for removing six of its biggest housing sites. He also wants monthly progress reports until the plan is submitted.

The BBC's account of the letter sets out why the minister acted. Rochford has built 11% more homes than its targets required. But it has found less than two years' supply of land for new homes, when national rules expect five. Average house prices in the district are more than 11 times local earnings, against 7.6 times across England. Mr Pennycook said the policies in the district's last plan, adopted in December 2011, were likely to be significantly out of date.

How we got here

The government has stepped in to make Rochford District Council push ahead with its plan for where new homes should go, after councillors cut out the largest housing sites. Housing minister Matthew Pennycook has ordered the council to put the plan out for public comment, in the version that still includes those sites.

What is a Local Plan?

Every council is expected to have one. It is the rulebook for an area: it says where new homes, shops, jobs and roads can go, and council planners use it to decide planning applications. Rochford's covers the whole district, including Rayleigh, Rochford, Hockley, Hawkwell, Hullbridge, Ashingdon, Canewdon and Great Wakering, and runs up to 2043.

The district is still relying on old plans, from 2011 and 2014, which the council itself says are out of date. Until a new one is adopted, planning applications are decided against those and national planning rules.

Why the government stepped in

Why so many homes? Under the government's new housing targets, Rochford's requirement was raised to 689 new homes a year, more than double the old figure, the council said in December 2025. At the time it said it wanted a plan "shaped by local evidence, local needs and local voices, not by arbitrary targets".

The Local Democracy Reporting Service reported that the draft put to councillors in September made room for 11,783 homes between 2026 and 2043. That is slightly more than the 11,710 the council had been asked to find land for. Almost half, 5,886, were on six large sites.

On 23 September councillors approved the plan only after removing all six of those sites, along with other changes suggested by a group of councillors. The vote was 13 for, none against and 19 abstentions. The council is run by a Reform UK minority administration.

A week later Mr Pennycook froze the plan with a "holding direction", an order stopping it going any further. According to Planning Geek's account of the letter, he said the changes left a significant shortfall against the district's identified housing need and risked a plan unlikely to pass examination. Council leader Cllr Adi Malviya asked for an urgent meeting and later urged ministers to lift the freeze, as The Seax reported, warning that a consultation could end up running over Christmas.

What ministers have ordered now

Mr Pennycook wrote to Cllr Malviya on Monday 23 November. He told the council to start the consultation on 7 December, using the version of the plan put to councillors before they took the six sites out. The council may not withdraw the plan, must report to ministry officials every month and must see it through to the end. Small changes are allowed after the consultation, but any big change has to go to the Secretary of State.

What it means for residents

The consultation is the public's chance to comment. At this late stage, comments have to be about whether the plan is legally compliant and sound, meaning workable and properly justified. Objections to particular sites can still be made on those grounds. After that, a planning inspector appointed by the government examines the plan before it can be adopted. The council has not said how long the consultation will run; in September it planned six weeks. Earlier this month it said the plan had to be submitted by 31 December, though Cllr Malviya said the government had indicated the deadline could move to March. The minister's letter has now fixed it at the end of March 2027.

"Many people in our district feel strongly about the Local Plan," Cllr Malviya said. The council says it is considering how to respond to the letter and will update residents in the coming days.

Key points

  • Plan makes room for about 11,800 homes across the district by 2043
  • Public consultation on the full plan starts on 7 December
  • Council cannot withdraw it and must report to ministry officials every month
  • Leader: 'Many people in our district feel strongly about the Local Plan'
