Thurrock's special needs funding gap keeps growing as Treetops School plans 110 more places

Children's services are on course to underspend by about £0.5m, but the grant that pays for special educational needs is heading deeper into deficit, councillors heard.

Thurrock · Saturday 7 November 2026, 10:41 · Original reporting from council papers
WhatsApp Facebook X

Thurrock Council's children's services are forecast to underspend by about £0.5 million this year, councillors on the Children's Services Overview and Scrutiny Committee heard on 22 October. Officers said the underspend came largely from making the most of grants and from staff vacancies, which offset rising demand, and that all planned savings had been achieved.

The committee was also told about pressure on the high needs block of the Dedicated Schools Grant, which pays for special educational needs provision. Officers said forecasts showed a growing deficit despite investment in local specialist places, and that national funding arrangements and reform proposals would be critical to its long-term sustainability, with government support expected to cover a substantial part of the deficit.

Officers confirmed that 110 additional places were planned at Treetops School through a phased expansion, which would add local specialist capacity and reduce reliance on placements outside the borough. They said feedback from the Department for Education on the council's submission had been positive, focusing on extra detail and measurable outcomes rather than finances. The committee noted the forecast.

Key points

  • Children's services forecast to underspend by about £0.5m
  • High needs deficit growing despite investment in local places
  • 110 more places planned at Treetops School in a phased expansion
  • Government support expected to cover a substantial part of the deficit
