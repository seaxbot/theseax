# Grays prison tutor spared jail over secret relationship with inmate

**Oksana Antanaitiene, 52, a maths tutor at HMP Chelmsford, used a false name and a secret phone to keep in touch with him.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Grays, Chelmsford, Essex, England. Published: Friday 27 November 2026, 16:15 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Court.

## Key points

- Eight months, suspended for two years
- Also 240 hours of unpaid work
- Admitted misconduct in a public office

## The story

A maths tutor from Grays who formed a secret relationship with an inmate at HMP Chelmsford has been given a suspended prison sentence.

Oksana Antanaitiene, 52, of Bradleigh Avenue, was sentenced at Chelmsford Crown Court on Friday 27 November to eight months in prison, suspended for two years. She must also do 240 hours of unpaid work and a rehabilitation activity requirement, Essex Police said.

She admitted misconduct in a public office at the same court on Tuesday 22 September, having been charged in July. Sentencing had first been listed for 28 October.

Police said she wrote to the prisoner and spoke to him by phone using a false name, "Agata Diaz". She registered a second, secret phone on the prison's contact system so the pair could talk without raising suspicion at first.

She also sent him sexually explicit pictures of herself and gave him music and television files on a USB drive. The prisoner was not fully named in court.

The court heard she was employed by a contractor delivering education courses at the prison and started there in September 2024. She later arranged for the man to join one of her classes. On 21 August 2025 she was found alone with him in a locked staff office, the BBC reported.

Officers searched his cell on 22 October 2025 and found explicit pictures of a woman, and a USB device hidden in a dressing gown. At an internal meeting the next day she admitted the relationship, police said.

After her arrest, officers rang the number registered to "Agata Diaz". A phone seized from her car began ringing inside its evidence bag.

Judge Alexander Mills said she had let herself be influenced by the prisoner's attention and apparent affection. James Reilly, mitigating, said she had been a teacher for 30 years and had made a bad mistake. "She's lost her career; she's been absolutely humbled," he told the court.

She was ordered to pay £150 costs and a victim surcharge. The judge also made a deprivation order covering USB devices, phones, photographs and a SIM card.

DC Matthew Parker, of the Prison Investigations Team, said people working in prisons are "placed in positions of trust" and must keep clear professional boundaries. "Our investigation established that those boundaries had clearly been breached," he said.
