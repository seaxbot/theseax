Southchurch Library: 2,600 have their say as four options stay open

Southchurch, Southend-on-Sea · Wednesday 25 November 2026, 15:19 · Reported by Southend-on-Sea City Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

More than 2,600 people took part in Southend City Council's review of Southchurch Library. No decision has been made on its future.

Southend-on-Sea City Council said on Wednesday 25 November that more than 2,600 people took part in its review of Southchurch Library. The consultation ran from 28 August to 8 October and drew 712 formal responses, as well as visitors to a series of drop-in sessions around Southchurch.

The library has been in trouble for years. It moved into its current building in 1972 as a temporary home after leaving Southchurch Hall. That building is a prefabricated timber structure that was expected to last about 25 years. More than 50 years on, the council says it has been assessed as being in very poor condition and needs significant investment.

The four options

Four choices are still on the table: refurbishing the existing building, a new build on the same site, moving the library into Southend City Community College, or finding another location in the Southchurch area. The council puts the likely costs at between about £150,000 for relocation and about £750,000 for alternative provision. It has not published a figure for each option.

Most people who responded wanted to keep a library on the current site. They spoke about how easy it is to reach and its value as a place for the community.

Councillor Matt Dent, cabinet member for business, culture, music and tourism, said "the level of engagement has been extremely strong". Councillor Kevin Robinson, cabinet member for planning, property and governance, stressed that no decision has yet been made.

What happens next

The council's Place Overview and Scrutiny Committee will look at the findings on 3 December. Councillors there will give feedback but will not be asked to choose a preferred option. Cabinet will then consider recommendations and decide the next steps on 17 December.

Key points

  • 712 formal responses to the consultation
  • Building opened in 1972 as a temporary home
  • Scrutiny committee meets on 3 December
  • Cabinet decides next steps on 17 December
