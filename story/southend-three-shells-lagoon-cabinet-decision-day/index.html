Southend cabinet backs £800,000 plan to remove Three Shells Lagoon

Southend-on-Sea City Council's cabinet agreed on Monday night to clear the fenced-off lagoon beside Southend Pier and return the site to natural beach, a decade after it was built for nearly £2m.

Southend-on-Sea · Monday 16 November 2026, 05:58 · Updated Monday 16 November 2026, 20:01 · Reported by Southend-on-Sea City Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Councillors backed a council report that found removal was the most practical long-term answer once engineers had weighed the alternatives. The report recommended taking out the whole structure, including the rock walls and the steel piling beneath the sand, which would bring the combined cost of building, repairing and dismantling the attraction to about £2.84m.

Three Shells Lagoon opened in July 2016, paid for mostly by public money: £1.25m from the government's Coastal Communities Fund and £600,000 from the council, with £100,000 from Adventure Island owner Philip Miller. It has been fenced off for most of 2026 after a mother and daughter had to be pulled out with ropes in March when they sank into waist-deep sand. The council spent £90,000 covering the silt with sand to tackle complaints about the smell, but the surface never became firm enough everywhere to let the public back in.

Before the meeting Cllr Matt Dent, cabinet member for business, culture, music and tourism, called the lagoon "a monstrosity that is no longer fit for purpose". Mr Miller says he offered other ways to fix it and blames a lack of maintenance by the council. The council has said any work on the site is likely to need planning permission and coastal licensing approval before the beach can be restored.

Key points

  • The lagoon opened in July 2016 beside Adventure Island
  • It has been closed since a sand rescue in March
  • The council has already spent £90,000 trying to fix the smell
  • Removal is likely to need planning and coastal licences
