Essex falls silent for Remembrance Sunday as parades close town centres from Colchester to Leigh-on-Sea

Services at war memorials across the county mark the 11am two-minute silence today, with First Essex changing buses in 16 towns and villages while roads are closed for parades.

Chelmsford, Colchester, Brentwood, Saffron Walden · Saturday 7 November 2026, 18:45 · Updated Sunday 8 November 2026, 13:45 · Reported by First Essex
WhatsApp Facebook X

Road closures for Remembrance Sunday parades will disrupt bus services across Essex on Sunday 8 November, with First Essex listing changes in 16 towns and villages from Chelmsford and Colchester to Leigh-on-Sea and Great Dunmow. Most closures fall between mid-morning and lunchtime around the 11am two-minute silence, so anyone travelling on Sunday morning should check their route before setting out. In Chelmsford the service begins at 10.55am at the war memorial in Duke Street, led by the Dean of Chelmsford, while in Southend the parade assembles at Prittlewell Square at 10.15am and moves along Clifftown Parade to the Cenotaph for an 11am service.

In Chelmsford, city centre roads close from 10am to 12.30pm and the bus station cannot be used, so passengers are asked to board at Coval Lane, the Retail Market or Parkway instead. In Colchester, Head Street and High Street close from 8am to 1.30pm, affecting routes including the 51 to 57, 68, 75, 87 and X20. Closures also hit Brentwood High Street from 10.15am to 11.45am, Rayleigh High Street from 9.30am, Wickford's Runwell Road from 10am to 1pm, and the high streets in Maldon, Burnham-on-Crouch and Great Dunmow during the morning.

Afternoon parades bring later closures, with Halstead High Street shut from 2pm to 3pm, Witham's Newland Street from 2.30pm to 3.45pm and Leigh Broadway from 2pm to 5pm. In Saffron Walden, the town council says London Road, High Street, King Street, Market Place and Market Hill close from 9.45am to 12.30pm, with no parking in the affected spots from 5am. First Essex says drivers may also pause for the two-minute silence at 11am where it is safe to do so. Ahead of the services, Essex County Council placed poppy emblems on carriageways at 12 locations, near war memorials where possible, and Thurrock Council teams tidied and repainted memorials in Tilbury, Grays, Aveley, Purfleet and East Tilbury. The BBC reports that soldiers from 16 Air Assault Brigade and the Band of the Brigade of Gurkhas were due to join civic leaders at Colchester's war memorial at the bottom of High Street, with the mayor taking the salute at the Town Hall afterwards, while Chelmsford marks its first Poppy Festival with 75,000 handcrafted poppies on display around the city. In Harlow, hundreds of people gathered at the Netteswell Cross in Harlow Town Park for a service organised by the Royal British Legion's Harlow branch and led by the Reverend Michael Ryman, with Harlow MP Chris Vince and council leader Dan Swords among those attending.

Key points

  • Two-minute silence at 11am on Sunday 8 November
  • First Essex bus changes in 16 Essex towns and villages
  • Colchester Head Street and High Street closed 8am to 1.30pm
  • Hundreds attended the Royal British Legion service at the Netteswell Cross in Harlow Town Park
