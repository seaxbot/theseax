Essex student Moyin Odeniran wins The Great British Bake Off

Loughton · Updated Tuesday 24 November 2026, 23:25 · First published Tuesday 24 November 2026, 06:15 · First reported by Channel 4 · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

The 22-year-old beat Tom Whittaker and Mo Mahir in the Channel 4 final on Tuesday 24 November, with friends and family watching at his local church in Loughton.

Moyin Odeniran watched himself win The Great British Bake Off at a watch party in his church in Loughton. "I don't know how that happened, I don't know how I got here," he said after the final. He beat Tom Whittaker from West Yorkshire and Birmingham student Mo Mahir. Tom, 25, had been seen as the favourite, according to the Evening Standard.

The final

His final began with a lemon chiffon cake with raspberry compote and lemon curd for the citrus signature challenge. Judge Paul Hollywood said it was one of the best cakes he had eaten "for a long, long time".

He came third in the technical, a batch of nine crème brûlée doughnuts. For the showstopper, a tribute to a British landmark, he built London's Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park. He admitted it ended up messy because he went too big. Nigella Lawson still called him a worthy winner, and Hollywood said the reaction from his family was pure magic.

Who he is

Odeniran lives with his mother, sister and grandmother, Channel 4 says. He started baking with his mum when he was five and had mastered macarons by 14. His bakes often use flavours from his Nigerian heritage, such as grains of paradise and groundnuts.

He graduated from King's College London with a first in political economy, with his dissertation due a month after filming ended. He is also a sprinter, and a former county under-20 champion over 100m.

In the tent he was star baker in caramel week. In Audience Choice week he won a Hollywood handshake for a cheesecake he made with his mother, which he called his best moment of the series.

The church watch party

On the morning of the final he told BBC Essex that more than 100 friends and family had watched the first episode with him at his church. He baked the cake for that night too. For the final, he said, "we did the same thing, but with more cake".

What next

He plans to keep the trophy in his kitchen for a couple of months, His dream, he says, is to become one of the best bakers in the world. Applications for the next series are open at applyforbakeoff.co.uk.

Key points

  • Showstopper was an edible Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park
  • Third in the crème brûlée doughnut technical
  • First series with Nigella Lawson judging beside Paul Hollywood
  • A county under-20 100m champion as well as a baker
