Suspected drug-drivers in Essex barred from driving for up to six weeks while police investigate

Essex Police says it is one of the first forces in the country to use bail conditions this way, as drug-driving arrests across the county keep rising.

Essex · Thursday 12 November 2026, 06:18 · Updated Thursday 12 November 2026, 14:12 · Reported by BBC News
WhatsApp Facebook X

Essex Police is using bail conditions to stop people suspected of drug-driving from driving while its inquiries continue, the BBC reported on Thursday 12 November 2026. Adam Pipe, head of the force's roads policing unit, said the aim was to speed up justice, because court backlogs and delays in testing blood samples can leave suspects driving for months before any disqualification.

Your Harlow reports that the policy, introduced in July, lets a custody sergeant attach a condition not to drive to pre-charge bail when a suspect cannot be charged straight away, for example while blood or urine results are still awaited. The suspect can make representations before the custody sergeant decides.

Essex Police says the condition may be used after collisions causing serious injury, after dangerous driving following a police pursuit, or when a driver is already awaiting prosecution for another drink or drug driving offence. Mr Pipe described it as an interim driving restriction before a case reaches court.

Key points

  • Almost 2,000 drug-driving arrests in the first 10 months of 2026, against 886 for drink-driving
  • Drug-driving arrests rose from 971 in 2024 to 1,874 in 2025
  • A custody sergeant decides after a risk assessment
