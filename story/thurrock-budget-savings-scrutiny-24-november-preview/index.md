# Thurrock says SEND support is safe as councillors scrutinise budget cuts

**Thurrock's draft savings for 2027/28 have been before scrutiny councillors. The council insists SEND support will not be cut.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Grays, Thurrock, Essex, England. Published: Monday 23 November 2026, 23:40 (UK time). Updated: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 14:43. Section: News. Topic: Politics.

## Key points

- Second car permit would go from £40 to £62
- SEND savings cover taxi transport, not education support
- Full council sets the 2027/28 budget in February

## The story

Thurrock councillors examined the council's draft savings for 2027/28 at the corporate overview and scrutiny committee on Tuesday 24 November. Your Thurrock reported on 25 November that the plans lean on cutting temporary accommodation costs through homelessness prevention, reforming adult social care and SEND transport, using more technology and getting more from council property. The council said it is not proposing any cut to SEND provision. The transport savings relate to taxi arrangements, not to children's education support.

Council leader Richard Bingley said the council "still faces significant financial challenges". He said new financial controls now cover procurement, property and community grants. Councillor Sara Poyntz, cabinet member for finance, called them "challenging decisions, but they are responsible decisions".

## What else is proposed

Two of the plans reach straight into residents' pockets and staff desks. Your Thurrock reported on 23 November that the first residents' parking permit would stay at £30, but a second would rise from £40 to £62 and a third from £50 to £72. A household with three permits would pay £164 a year instead of £120. Council papers say those who take up the most parking space should contribute the most. The change would start on 1 April 2027 and is forecast to raise about £50,000 a year. No consultation is planned, because the permit zones are covered by traffic regulation orders.

The council would also stop buying new mobile phones for staff. Checks found a significant number of handsets that were unused or had never been switched on. Instead it would recycle devices, move more calls to Microsoft Teams and offer willing staff an eSIM work number on their own phone. It spends about £20,000 a year on handsets, and the plan could save up to £25,000. A council spokesman said any use of personal phones would meet data protection rules. The Seax first reported both plans last week, on [parking permits](https://theseax.com/story/thurrock-parking-permits-more-than-one-car-higher-charges/) and [staff phones](https://theseax.com/story/thurrock-council-staff-mobile-phones-budget-savings/).

## Why the council is cutting

Thurrock is still recovering from failed investments that left it deep in debt. According to the council, a quarter of its yearly revenue budget goes on debt linked to those investments. It still needs more than £60m of exceptional financial support from the government. Councillor Sara Poyntz, cabinet member for finance, said: "I want to be clear that these are just proposals at this stage." She added that residents would be consulted on the plans.

## What happens next

Scrutiny councillors can make recommendations but cannot block the plans. The council says the proposals now move through the budget process, including a public consultation, and the full council will consider the 2027/28 budget in February 2027. Even if the permit rises survive that process, they would need a separate decision before they start.
