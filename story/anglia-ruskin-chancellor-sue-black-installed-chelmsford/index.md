# ARU installs forensic scientist Sue Black as chancellor in Chelmsford

**One of the world's leading forensic scientists, named Anglia Ruskin University's chancellor in September, has been formally invested at its Chelmsford graduations.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Chelmsford, Essex, England. Published: Friday 27 November 2026, 20:38 (UK time). Updated: Friday 27 November 2026, 22:25. Section: News. Topic: Education.

## Key points

- Led British forensic team's anthropology work in Kosovo in 1999
- Her Dundee team identified child abusers from their hands
- Succeeds Lord Ribeiro as ARU chancellor

## The story

The investiture of Professor Lady Sue Black as Chancellor of Anglia Ruskin University (ARU) took place at Chelmsford Cathedral during this month's graduation celebrations, the university said. ARU first named her as its new chancellor on 1 September.

In her address she called the role "a tremendous honour" and promised to bring "a healthy dose of Scottish Highland determination" to it. She told graduates that universities have always held a special place in her heart, as places where curiosity is encouraged and people discover who they might become.

ARU's Vice Chancellor, Professor Roderick Watkins, described her as "a passionate advocate for widening opportunity through education". She succeeds Lord Ribeiro, the surgeon who held the post before her, and her appointment in September came as ARU launched its ten-year strategy, Transforming Lives. The university gave her an honorary Doctor of Science degree in 2024.

## From the Highlands to the mortuary

Born in Inverness, she grew up in the coastal hamlet of Strome, the place she later took her title from. She studied human anatomy at the University of Aberdeen, graduating in 1982, and stayed for a PhD on identifying people from their skeletal remains.

She then lectured in anatomy at St Thomas' Hospital in London. It was there, ARU's honorary degree citation says, that the Metropolitan Police first asked for her help in identifying human remains, and her career in forensic anthropology began.

## Kosovo and the world's disaster zones

In 1999 she went to Kosovo as lead forensic anthropologist with the British Forensic Team, which exhumed mass graves to gather evidence for international war crimes investigations. She was made an OBE in 2001 for that work.

Her work for the UK Government and international agencies also took her to Sierra Leone, Grenada and Iraq. The University of Dundee says she was deployed to help identify victims of the Asian tsunami in 2004.

## Catching abusers by their hands

She was Professor of Anatomy and Forensic Anthropology at the University of Dundee from 2003 to 2018, where she led the creation of the Centre for Anatomy and Human Identification. Dundee says her team there developed techniques to identify child abusers from the vein and skin patterns on their hands, as seen in their own images.

She was made a Dame in 2016 for services to forensic anthropology and education. In 2018 she moved to Lancaster University as Pro Vice Chancellor for Engagement.

## Peer, college president, author

She entered the House of Lords in 2021 as a crossbench peer, Baroness Black of Strome, and sits on the all-party parliamentary group on miscarriages of justice. In September 2022 she became the 37th President of St John's College, Oxford.

She was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 2023. In 2024 she was appointed to the Order of the Thistle, which ARU describes as the highest honour in Scotland.

She has been a guest on BBC Radio 4's Desert Island Discs and The Life Scientific, and has written three books. The latest, An Expert Witness, came out in July.

## A busy month for ARU

ARU says 3,140 students are receiving their degrees at its autumn ceremonies in Chelmsford, Cambridge and Peterborough. The university is also seeking voluntary redundancies to cut its staff bill, [as The Seax reported](https://theseax.com/story/anglia-ruskin-university-voluntary-redundancies-staff-bill/).
