Essex commissioner brands regional 'mega force' plan not fit for purpose as merger review awaits publication

Roger Hirst told the BBC there is no evidence bigger forces perform better, a day after the Met chief called for the 43 forces in England and Wales to shrink to as few as eight.

Essex · Friday 6 November 2026, 15:48 · Updated Saturday 7 November 2026, 06:21 · Reported by Your Thurrock
WhatsApp Facebook X

Essex's Police, Fire and Crime Commissioner Roger Hirst has come out strongly against reported plans to merge police forces, which could see Essex Police lose its standalone status and become part of a larger regional force. The proposals would change how policing is organised for the county's roughly 1.9 million residents.

A review for the Government led by former Metropolitan Police Commissioner Bernard Hogan-Howe is reported to consider cutting England's 39 forces to as few as nine regional forces, plus one for Wales, sitting above more than 150 local policing units. Met Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley used a speech in Manchester on Friday to argue that the 43 forces in England and Wales should become eight to ten, under a new National Police Service, warning that policing faces decline without reform. Mr Hirst said most people at three public meetings he chaired opposed the idea, and a summer survey by his office found 69% of respondents against regional mergers.

The Hogan-Howe review has been sent to the Home Secretary but not published; The Guardian reports it is due out before Christmas, with a white paper in the new year, and that police and crime commissioners would be abolished under the Government's plans. Mr Hirst argues policing should stay locally led and says Essex should not pay the price for problems at the much larger Met, and he wants a fairer funding formula instead. Speaking to the BBC on Saturday, Mr Hirst called the plan not fit for purpose and said there was no evidence that so-called mega forces perform better; the idea has been on pause since Andy Burnham became prime minister in July.

Key points

  • Rowley wants 43 forces cut to eight to ten
  • Review reportedly considers as few as nine in England
  • Essex Police serves about 1.9 million people
  • Hirst says no evidence mega forces perform better
