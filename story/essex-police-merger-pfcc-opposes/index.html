Essex police commissioner vows to fight plan that could fold Essex Police into a regional super force

Roger Hirst says there is no public appetite for change in Essex, as a Government-commissioned review is reported to recommend cutting England's 39 forces to as few as nine.

Essex · Friday 6 November 2026, 15:48 · Reported by Your Thurrock
WhatsApp Facebook X

Essex's Police, Fire and Crime Commissioner Roger Hirst has come out strongly against reported plans to merge police forces, which could see Essex Police lose its standalone status and become part of a larger regional force. The proposals would change how policing is organised for the county's roughly 1.9 million residents.

A review for the Government led by former Metropolitan Police Commissioner Bernard Hogan-Howe is reported to recommend cutting England's 39 forces to as few as nine regional forces, plus one for Wales, sitting above more than 150 local policing units. The review has not been published, but its recommendations were expected to be outlined on Friday by Met Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley. Mr Hirst said most people at three public meetings he chaired opposed the idea, and a summer survey by his office found 69% of respondents against regional mergers.

Mr Hirst argues policing should stay locally led, warning a merger could disrupt work on crime and anti-social behaviour, and says Essex should not pay the price for problems at the much larger Met. He wants the Government to strengthen links between communities and local police and to bring in a fairer funding formula, and says he will keep making the case for Essex Police in its current form.

Key points

  • Review reportedly proposes as few as nine forces in England
  • Essex Police serves about 1.9 million people
  • 69% opposed mergers in PFCC survey
  • Review not yet published
