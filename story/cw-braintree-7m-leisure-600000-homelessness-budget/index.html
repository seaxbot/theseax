Braintree plans £7m for leisure centres and £600,000 on homelessness

Braintree · Thursday 26 November 2026, 06:15 · Original reporting from council papers
WhatsApp Facebook X

Braintree's first budget plans for 2027-28 promise no cuts to services. A gap of £542,000 still has to be closed.

Braintree District Council published its first budget proposals for 2027-28 on Friday 20 November. They put money into leisure centres, homelessness and council buildings, and the council says they contain no cuts to services. Councillors on the Corporate Scrutiny Committee were due to look at the plans on Wednesday 25 November, before they go to the Cabinet. Full Council will set the final budget, including the council tax rate, in February 2027.

Leisure centres get the biggest sum, more than £7m spread over 2027-28 and 2028-29. It would pay for pool pods to help disabled swimmers, refurbished pools, toilets and showers, solar panels, new lighting and better heating. A further £600,000 a year would go on stopping people losing their homes, including help to keep private tenancies going and to prevent rough sleeping. The council is also drawing up plans to add dedicated social work support to its housing help.

Other plans include £3.5m to repair council-owned buildings such as community centres, along with more work on play areas and open spaces. More than £100,000 would go on the customer service centre, which the council says faces growing demand. Its council tax support scheme continues, with discounts of up to 77% for eligible working-age households. Together with the government scheme for pensioners, almost 8,000 households are expected to benefit.

Money is still tight. The council forecasts a shortfall of £542,000 in 2027-28, rising to £2.6m the year after, and plans to close it through savings, new income and modernised services. Cllr Kevin Bowers, Deputy Leader and Cabinet Member for Finance, Assets and Performance, said the council remained "in a strong position to manage the shortfall". He warned that difficult choices may lie ahead.

Key points

  • Leisure centres: more than £7m over 2027-28 and 2028-29
  • Homelessness prevention: £600,000 a year
  • Council buildings, play areas and open spaces: £3.5m
  • Forecast shortfall: £542,000 in 2027-28, rising to £2.6m the year after
