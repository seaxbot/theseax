# Thurrock Council says sorry over Grays Town Park cafe rent demand

**A Reform councillor says the demand was sent without authorisation. The council will meet the volunteers to agree a way forward.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Grays, Grays Town Park, Essex, England. Published: Friday 27 November 2026, 06:19 (UK time). Updated: Friday 27 November 2026, 15:40. Section: News. Topic: Community.

## Key points

- Council: cafe has no lease and has paid no rent for years
- Statement does not say whether the demand is withdrawn
- Other community groups also face new charges, Nub News says

## The story

Thurrock Council has apologised to the volunteers who run the Grays Town Park cafe for the way they were told to start paying rent, and says it will meet them to agree a way forward.

"We are sorry that they received this email out of the blue, which we recognise will have caused them a good deal of stress and upset," the council said. Its statement was shared on Facebook on Friday afternoon by Reform UK councillor Steven Pester. The council added that it wanted to see the cafe "continue and thrive" in the park.

The council said it was reviewing all its historic arrangements with businesses, charities and community groups based in council-owned buildings, to make sure taxpayers get value for money. It pointed out that the cafe has no lease, and has paid no rent for several years while taxpayers covered its utility bills, but said it recognised the group's "huge contribution" to Grays.

Cllr Pester, who represents Little Thurrock Blackshots, went further. He wrote that a council department had sent the rent demand "without authorisation, or accuracy", against the new administration's planned policy on fair use of council buildings. He said he had asked the council to apologise and withdraw the demand, and that other letters had been stopped. Reform UK has run the council since May, when it won 45 of the 49 seats.

The council's statement did not say whether the demand has been withdrawn, or what happens next for the other community groups that have been told to expect new charges.

## How the row began

Grays Town Park Cafe opened in 2021 in a disused shelter built in 1930 in memory of the Grays brewery owner Jonathan Seabrooke. Thurrock Council backed the project with a grant and waived the rent and utility bills. The park itself was given to the people of Thurrock in 1887.

Thurrock Nub News first reported the row on Tuesday 24 November. The Friends of Grays Town Park, who run the cafe, say the council told them the new rent reflects the building's valuation and current market conditions. Once running costs are added, the bill comes to almost £1,000 a month, which the group says it cannot sustain.

Tina Holland, who founded the cafe, told BBC News the money would have to come out of what the cafe does for the town. It is home to a book club, a yarn group, a men's health group, bike rides, free summer yoga, monthly music nights and a dementia group. Prices are kept low, and the kitchen has only a toaster and a microwave.

Volunteers also pick litter and look after the park's gardens and public toilets, Ms Holland said. The Friends warn that "we will have no option but to consider closing the cafe" unless the rent is cut sharply. Their hopes of resurfacing the park's games courts and extending the cafe are now in doubt.

Money is tight at the council, which was effectively declared bankrupt in 2022 with debts that reached £1.5bn. Councillors went through its [latest savings plans on 24 November](https://theseax.com/story/thurrock-budget-savings-scrutiny-24-november-preview/). Nub News says it has been told of several other community groups facing new charges or steep rent rises, among them a nursery for disabled children.
