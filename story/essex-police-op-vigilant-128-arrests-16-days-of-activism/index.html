Essex patrols targeting predatory behaviour made 128 arrests in a year

Essex · Thursday 26 November 2026, 12:03 · Reported by Essex Police
WhatsApp Facebook X

The figures come as the force marks 16 Days of Activism. Its chief constable says tackling violence against women matters every day.

Essex Police officers on Operation Vigilant made 128 arrests over the last year, the force said, as it joined the global 16 Days of Activism against gender-based violence.

Op Vigilant puts officers alongside CCTV operators and local partners to spot concerning and predatory behaviour in public places and step in early. In the last year 811 officers took part, with more than 2,500 interactions and 1,261 vehicles stopped, according to Chief Inspector Sam Girdlestone, the force's tactical lead on violence against women and girls.

Teams are being sent where they can have most impact across the county in the run-up to Christmas and through the festive period, the force said. The campaign runs until 10 December.

'This matters every day'

Chief Constable Rachel Nolan, who has served with Essex Police for more than 27 years, said every woman and girl should be able to go about her life without fear of violence, abuse or harassment.

'Awareness events give us an important opportunity to stop, reflect and talk about these issues,' she said. 'But I want to be clear that, for us, this is about far more than marking a moment in the calendar.'

Rape cases and new protection orders

The force says Operation Soteria, brought in across Essex in February 2025, puts more scrutiny on suspects in rape and serious sexual offence investigations. It says rape solved rates have improved since, working with the Crown Prosecution Service.

Domestic Abuse Protection Orders are also being rolled out more widely. They let police and partners put restrictions and requirements on abusers. Each month an Essex Stalking Intervention Panel brings police and partner agencies together to look at individual stalking cases.

Help to change

The force's Reflect campaign is aimed at people who recognise controlling, coercive, stalking or other abusive behaviour in themselves. It urges them to get help to change through Essex Compass, at essexcompass.org.uk or on 0330 333 7444.

Key points

  • 811 officers took part in Op Vigilant
  • 1,261 vehicles stopped
  • Help to change: Essex Compass, 0330 333 7444
