# Auditors expect to give no opinion on three years of Basildon Council's accounts

**Basildon Borough Council's external auditors, EY, told councillors that the 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26 accounts are all expected to receive disclaimed opinions, and listed borrowing, Sempra Homes and housing among areas of significant risk.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Basildon, Essex, England. Published: Monday 16 November 2026, 06:20 (UK time). Section: Council Watch. Topic: Council Watch.

## Key points

- 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26 accounts all expected to get disclaimed opinions
- Auditors cite delays and poor-quality evidence for the 2025/26 audit
- Value for money risks include borrowing, Sempra Homes and housing performance
- Formal statutory recommendations to be considered once audit work ends

## The story

Basildon Borough Council's Audit and Risk Committee was told on Wednesday 4 November 2026 that all three sets of outstanding accounts, for 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26, are expected to receive disclaimed audit opinions. The update came from the council's external auditors, EY, according to the published minutes of the meeting. A disclaimed opinion means the auditor is unable to say whether the accounts give a true and fair view.

The minutes say the 2023/24 disclaimer follows statutory backstop arrangements that prevented audit work being completed. For 2024/25, councillors were told that despite substantial work by officers, not enough assurance had been obtained, and a partial audit report would be issued. For 2025/26, the minutes record that the council had been unable to adequately support the audit, with delays in providing evidence and problems with the quality of supporting documents, so only limited work will be done on areas including property valuations, loans and investments relating to the council's housing company Sempra Homes, and the Minimum Revenue Provision.

EY also set out significant risks for its value for money assessment, including the council's borrowing and financing costs, procurement and capital programme governance, Sempra Homes, internal control weaknesses, councillor conduct, and housing performance, including the council's C4 grading from the Regulator of Social Housing. Councillors were told several independent investigations commissioned by the council are still under way and will inform that assessment. The minutes say EY will consider whether to use formal statutory recommendations or other reporting powers once its work is complete.
