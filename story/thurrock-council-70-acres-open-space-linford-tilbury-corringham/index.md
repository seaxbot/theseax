# Thurrock Council says no decision yet on selling 70 acres of open space

**Three sites in Linford, Tilbury and Corringham could be sold. Residents have until 27 November to object, and cabinet would have the final say.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Linford, Tilbury, Corringham, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 15:40 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Politics.

## Key points

- Linford site alone covers about 60 acres
- No sale price or future use has been given
- Parker Avenue land in Tilbury covers 9.3 acres

## The story

Thurrock Council has advertised that it intends to sell three areas of public open space covering almost 70 acres between them. The largest is about 60 acres west of Buckingham Hill Road in Linford, roughly the size of 35 football pitches. The others are 9.3 acres north of Parker Avenue in Tilbury and a third of an acre between The Manorway and Silvertown Avenue in Corringham.

Your Thurrock reported on Thursday 26 November that the council has stressed no decision has been made. A spokesperson said cabinet must approve any sale, and that the council is "not yet at that stage" for these sites. No sale price has been revealed, and the notices do not say what the land would be used for.

## Why the council has to advertise

Under the Local Government Act 1972, a council that wants to sell open space must publish its plan and consider any objections before deciding. Residents have been invited to object by 5pm on Friday 27 November. BBC Essex also reported the plan, describing a proposal to dispose of about 70 acres across three sites.

## Earlier sites

The Seax has found the council has used the same process for other sites. In October 2025 it advertised a lease of about 2,000 square metres beside the Flagship Centre in Tilbury, under a third of a football pitch, for a Youth Zone. In June, horse riders and walkers held a protest ride over a proposed sale of land at South Hill, near the Basildon border, and the Essex Bridleways Association objected.

Thurrock is still overseen by government commissioners. [Ministers said on Wednesday 25 November](https://theseax.com/story/thurrock-council-minister-seventh-commissioners-report-assurance-plan/) that the council is much stronger but must still set a balanced budget for 2027/28 and keep paying down its debt.
