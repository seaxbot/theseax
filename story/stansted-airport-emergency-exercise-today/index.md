# Stansted plane crash drill went well, airport and police say

**More than 400 people rehearsed a plane crash at Stansted Airport on Tuesday 24 November. The services will now share what they learned.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Stansted, Essex, England. Published: Tuesday 24 November 2026, 06:01 (UK time). Updated: Thursday 26 November 2026, 13:22. Section: News. Topic: Transport.

## Key points

- A decommissioned aircraft was used on a remote stand
- The Civil Aviation Authority requires a full-scale drill every four years
- Ryanair, Border Force and the British Red Cross were among those helping

## The story

London Stansted said on Thursday 26 November that its large emergency exercise had gone well, two days after more than 400 people rehearsed the response to a simulated aircraft accident. Operations director Nick Millar said the airport was pleased with how the day went. Chief Inspector Julia Finch, Essex Police's Stansted Airport commander, called it a very successful exercise.

Station Manager Amir Williamson, of Essex County Fire and Rescue Service's emergency planning team, said the drill tested the response at operational, tactical and strategic levels. Essex & Herts Air Ambulance clinicians worked through a scenario designed by their own team, with manikins and actors playing casualties. The organisations involved will now review the day and share the lessons.

Anyone near London Stansted on Tuesday 24 November may have seen a large number of emergency vehicles. The airport was running a full-scale training exercise that simulated a major aircraft accident, to check how its teams and the emergency services would cope with the real thing.

The drill took place at Compass Base, a remote aircraft parking stand away from the terminal, using a decommissioned aircraft. The airport said it had been planned so there is no impact on flights or passengers. Civil Aviation Authority rules require every UK certificated aerodrome to stage an exercise like this at least once every four years.

About 400 people took part. Airport teams worked alongside Essex Police, Essex County Fire and Rescue Service, the East of England Ambulance Service and Essex & Herts Air Ambulance. The British Red Cross, Uttlesford District Council and Essex County Council also supported it. So did the Rapid Relief Team, the Air Accidents Investigation Branch and the housing ministry, along with Ryanair, Border Force and National Express.

The scenario called for a long and complicated response involving many organisations. It was designed to test life-saving procedures, who takes command and how, communications, and the support offered to passengers, their families and others caught up in it.

Nick Millar, operations director at London Stansted, said an aircraft accident was "an extremely unlikely event". He added that it "remains the most significant emergency scenario we could face", so the plans must be tested regularly. He said the exercise would let the airport rehearse how it works with partners in a highly challenging situation and find ways to strengthen its response.

Chief Inspector Julia Finch, Essex Police's Stansted Airport commander, said: "We regularly test our plans and capabilities, and this is no exception." She said exercises like this make sure the force is ready for any eventuality, however unlikely, and that it has a really positive working relationship with the airport.
