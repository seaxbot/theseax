Braintree district homes can put glass out at the kerb after £12m recycling plant upgrade

A rebuilt Biffa sorting plant near Ipswich, part-funded by Braintree District Council, can now handle glass bottles and jars from the district's new two-bin system.

Braintree & Witham · Thursday 5 November 2026, 16:37 · Reported by Braintree District Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Households in the Braintree district will be able to recycle glass bottles and jars from home rather than taking them to a bottle bank, after a £12m redevelopment of the materials recovery facility that sorts their recycling. Biffa's Masons plant near Ipswich now supports the district's Simpler Recycling kerbside system.

Biffa paid £6m towards the work, Suffolk County Council £5m and Braintree District Council £1m, using money from the national packaging Extended Producer Responsibility scheme, which makes manufacturers help pay for their packaging. Under the new system homes use a red-lid bin for paper and card and a blue-lid bin for glass, plastics and cans. Once fully running, the plant will process about 65,000 tonnes a year.

The site has new fire detection and suppression systems to deal with fires caused by vapes and batteries in recycling bins. The council says more recycling will mean less waste sent to energy from waste, cutting carbon emissions.

Key points

  • £12m upgrade of Biffa's Masons plant
  • Braintree council contributed £1m
  • Glass now collected at the kerb
  • About 65,000 tonnes a year once fully running
