Basildon Hospital gas and air halt 'completely unacceptable', says midwife union

Basildon · Updated Tuesday 24 November 2026, 17:19 · First published Monday 23 November 2026, 14:59 · First reported by Royal College of Midwives · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

The RCM says Basildon Hospital has had years to make gas and air safe for staff. Women in labour are offered alternative pain relief.

The Royal College of Midwives (RCM) says Basildon Hospital's managers have had years to make the maternity unit safe, and have not done it. The union says recent tests show nitrous oxide levels on the unit remain unsafe. It comes six weeks after the trust paid out to 121 staff who had been exposed to the gas.

Robbie Turner, the RCM's chief membership officer and deputy general secretary, said the safety problems with Entonox had left staff at risk. Now, he said, they were leaving women without safe and effective pain relief once more. "This is completely unacceptable," he said.

He said the union's health and safety reps at the hospital had been raising staff concerns about poor ventilation and leaking equipment. The RCM says it is disappointed that, rather than investing in better facilities and working with staff, the trust's decision simply reduces choice and the level of service for women.

What the union wants

The RCM is urging the trust to find a long-term fix and to build it with staff and union reps. It says investment is needed urgently so the gas stays at safe levels across the whole maternity unit.

What the trust says

In a letter to staff seen by the Daily Gazette, Mid and South Essex NHS Foundation Trust said the unit will stop using gas and air from Thursday 26 November. It called it a "difficult decision". The trust says it has brought in specialist equipment and followed advice, but air-quality tests still sometimes show higher levels than it would like. Gas and air will not come back until there is a permanent solution, and the trust warned that could need a significant programme of building works.

How we got here

The BBC reported on Monday that the decision came shortly after the trust's own specialist said a permanent fix to the unit's ventilation was a high priority. No date has been given for gas and air to return.

Figures released to the BBC under the Freedom of Information Act show the problem carried on into 2026. The legal workplace exposure limit was broken twice between January and July. Five people had raised readings in October, although the trust said none of those was over the limit. The trust says the unit is safe for families, and that the health risk is to staff who breathe the gas over long periods.

Concerns about gas levels on the unit were first raised in July 2021. An independent review by the Good Governance Institute found the trust should have treated them as a serious health risk from then, but did not do so until October 2022. The trust said in February 2024 that the review blamed weak governance, poor management of medical gases and unstable leadership. Its chief executive, Matthew Hopkins, apologised to staff and said it was "simply not acceptable that we took so long to act".

An internal hospital report found some staff breathed in up to 30 times the legal limit between 2021 and 2023. The BBC found staff were not told about test results showing the breaches for 16 months. In October, the trust settled claims from 121 staff, 85 of them midwives, for a total of £735,000, and apologised. It says some other claims are still outstanding.

The staff reported tiredness, anxiety, headaches and brain fog after long shifts on the unit. The Royal College of Midwives, which had more than 80 members among them, called it the largest group legal action ever brought by midwives in the UK. Daniel Poet of Thompsons Solicitors, who acted for most of the staff, told the BBC the trust knew about the danger in 2021 but did not tell staff for a year.

Entonox is a 50:50 mix of nitrous oxide and oxygen. The Health and Safety Executive sets a workplace limit for nitrous oxide of 100 parts per million, averaged over an eight-hour shift. It says high exposure can cause nerve problems and anaemia. It published new guidance on using the gas safely in maternity units in February 2025.

Basildon's maternity unit has stopped using the gas before. Basildon Nub News reported in December 2022 that Entonox was suspended there after a leak, while a new ventilation system was put in. The Princess Alexandra Hospital in Harlow also suspended gas and air in January 2023 after finding higher levels than usual, The Telegraph reported.

Key points

  • RCM says gas levels on the unit are still unsafe
  • RCM reps raised concerns about ventilation and leaks
  • 121 staff shared a £735,000 payout in October
  • No date set for gas and air to return
