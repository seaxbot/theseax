Harwich lifeboat volunteers treat unconscious crew member on commercial ship before helicopter airlift

An injured sailor with head injuries was winched from a commercial vessel at Bawdsey anchorage and flown to Ipswich Hospital after Harwich RNLI was called out.

Harwich · Tuesday 10 November 2026, 06:45 · Reported by BBC News (via Gateway 97.8)
WhatsApp Facebook X

Harwich RNLI's all-weather lifeboat was sent to help a crew member who had been knocked unconscious on board a commercial vessel at Bawdsey anchorage, off the Suffolk coast just north of Harwich, on Monday afternoon.

The lifeboat, Albert Brown, launched at about 1.28pm, Hits Radio reports. Two of its volunteer crew went aboard the ship to give first aid and prepare the injured sailor, who had head injuries, to be taken off. The casualty was then winched up into a helicopter.

The helicopter flew the sailor to Ipswich Hospital, the BBC reports. No update on the sailor's condition has been given, and the lifeboat returned to its Harwich station once the job was done.

Key points

  • Harwich all-weather lifeboat Albert Brown launched at about 1.28pm on Monday
  • Two RNLI volunteers boarded the commercial vessel to treat the casualty
