Southend's fenced-off Three Shells Lagoon should be removed and the beach restored, council report says

Southend-on-Sea City Council says taking out the failed lagoon is its preferred long-term option, and the proposal goes to Cabinet on 16 November.

Southend-on-Sea · Monday 9 November 2026, 16:17 · Reported by Southend-on-Sea City Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

A council report recommends that the Three Shells Lagoon on Southend's seafront be removed and the site returned to a natural beach. Southend-on-Sea City Council says this is its preferred long-term option after a technical assessment of several possibilities, and that the Joint Administration has committed to resolving the long-running problems with the scheme.

The council said the lagoon has already used a significant amount of public money and remains fenced off and unusable despite past investment. Cllr Matt Dent, Cabinet Member for Business, Culture, Music and Tourism, described it as no longer fit for purpose and said the council needed to "cut our losses". The council's announcement did not give a cost for removal, but said no realistic option would avoid further substantial cost.

The proposal will be considered by the council's Cabinet on Monday 16 November 2026. The council published the report on 9 November, setting out the recommended way forward for the site.

Key points

  • Report recommends removing the lagoon structure and restoring a natural beach
  • Site remains fenced off and unusable, the council says
  • No realistic option avoids further substantial cost, according to the council
  • Cabinet to consider the proposal on 16 November
