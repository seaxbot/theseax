Southend's fenced-off Three Shells Lagoon should be removed and the beach restored, council report says

Southend-on-Sea City Council says taking out the failed lagoon is its preferred long-term option, and the proposal goes to Cabinet on 16 November.

Southend-on-Sea · Monday 9 November 2026, 16:17 · Updated Tuesday 10 November 2026, 08:08 · Reported by Southend-on-Sea City Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Returning the site to a natural beach came out on top after a technical assessment of several options, the report says. The Joint Administration has committed to resolving the long-running problems with the seafront scheme.

The council said the lagoon has already used a significant amount of public money and remains fenced off and unusable despite past investment. Cllr Matt Dent, Cabinet Member for Business, Culture, Music and Tourism, described it as no longer fit for purpose and said the council needed to "cut our losses". Your Southend reports that demolition would cost about £800,000, taking the total spent on the lagoon to around £2.84 million once its £1.95 million build and roughly £90,000 of attempted fixes are included.

The proposal will be considered by the council's Cabinet on Monday 16 November 2026. If it is approved, and planning permission, marine licences and final funding are secured, work could start in spring 2027 and take eight to ten weeks, with some of the rock possibly reused in the East Beach dune restoration at Shoeburyness.

Key points

  • Report recommends removing the lagoon structure and restoring a natural beach
  • Demolition would cost about £800,000, Your Southend reports
  • Work could start in spring 2027 if Cabinet agrees
