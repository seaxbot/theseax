Maldon council says national rules leave little room to refuse new homes in Tollesbury, Great Totham and Latchingdon

The district's leader says unpopular village schemes cannot be turned down without sound planning evidence, as a new national scheme also takes many decisions away from committee.

Maldon, Tollesbury, Great Totham, Latchingdon · Monday 9 November 2026, 12:00 · Reported by Maldon District Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Maldon District Council has responded to anger over housing decisions in Tollesbury, Great Totham and Latchingdon, saying national policy limits its ability to refuse development on the edge of villages. It says the Government's December 2024 change to the housing formula raised the district's annual need from 310 to 586 homes.

The council says the revised National Planning Policy Framework, published in August 2026, is more supportive of some housing outside settlement boundaries where there is unmet need, and that strong local opposition or a site being outside a boundary is not on its own a reason to refuse. Leader Cllr Simon Morgan said any refusal must rest on sound planning evidence, and warned that unreasonable decisions can lead to costs being awarded against the council on appeal. In a second statement, the council explained that a National Scheme of Delegation in force since 31 October means many routine applications must now be decided by officers, and councillors can no longer call schemes in to committee.

The council says it has written to Housing Secretary Angela Rayner about the impact on rural districts. It agreed on 22 October to raise residents' speaking time at planning committee to five minutes, and says each application will still be judged on its merits, with highways, school and health advice from Essex County Council and the NHS.

Key points

  • Annual housing need rose from 310 to 586 homes
  • Opposition alone is not grounds to refuse, council says
  • New national scheme in force since 31 October limits committee call-ins
  • Residents now get five minutes to speak at committee
