Essex gets £1.1m to tackle stalking

Essex · Updated Friday 27 November 2026, 09:53 · First published Friday 27 November 2026, 08:04 · First reported by Your Harlow · Additional reporting by The Seax
WhatsApp Facebook X

Stalkers across Essex will be offered help to change their behaviour. Victims get more specialist support, including help with online tracking.

Roger Hirst, the Police, Fire and Crime Commissioner for Essex, has secured £1,135,634 from a national Home Office fund for work with domestic abuse and stalking perpetrators. The money runs from October 2026 to March 2029. Your Harlow first reported the award.

The biggest share, £658,538, pays for a Stalking Intervention Service run by The Change Project, a charity that already runs behaviour change programmes for domestic abusers in Essex. It will work with people whose obsessive behaviour includes stalking, whether of a former partner or of someone they were never in a relationship with. The aim is to change that behaviour before it does more harm.

A further £338,015 goes to victims through Ending Violence in Essex, a partnership of local domestic abuse services that includes Changing Pathways and Next Chapter. Of that, £118,709 pays for a specialist in digital safety. They will help people whose stalker uses phones, apps or other technology to monitor, track or harass them.

"No one should have to change their route to work, stop seeing friends or feel unsafe in their own home," Mr Hirst said. "The test is whether people are safer after they come forward."

The money was announced in the same week the government said it would create a single criminal offence of stalking for England and Wales. It would replace the two stalking offences in force since 2012, after a review by Richard Wright KC found the law confusing. The maximum sentence stays at 10 years, and the change will be made when parliamentary time allows.

Mr Hirst called the single offence a positive step. But he said it had to be matched by a longer-term commitment to tackle what drives obsessive offending.

Essex Police dealt with 2,883 stalking crimes in 2024, according to figures it released under the Freedom of Information Act. That compares with 489 in 2018. Much of the rise followed changes to how police record stalking, including a 2020 rule that harassment between ex-partners is recorded as stalking unless officers are satisfied it is not.

Essex has had Home Office money for this work before. In 2023 the government named a stalking intervention project in the county among those backed by up to £1.7m from an earlier round of the fund.

The latest award was announced during the 16 Days of Activism against gender-based violence, which runs until 10 December.

Anyone worried they are being stalked can call the National Stalking Helpline, run by the Suzy Lamplugh Trust, free on 0808 802 0300. Stalking can be reported to Essex Police on 101 or online. In an emergency, always call 999.

Key points

  • Money runs from October 2026 to March 2029
  • £118,709 for a digital safety specialist
  • National Stalking Helpline: 0808 802 0300
