Transport Secretary tours Stansted as airport pushes on with plan for 51 million passengers

Heidi Alexander saw the new skylink walkways, the enlarged security hall and the airport's solar farm during a visit to London Stansted.

Stansted · Friday 6 November 2026, 11:38 · Reported by Bishop's Stortford Independent
WhatsApp Facebook X

Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander has visited London Stansted to see progress on its five-year transformation programme, as the airport works towards handling 51 million passengers a year over the next two decades without a new runway. Stansted is Essex's biggest single employer site and carried more than 30 million passengers in 2025, with record monthly numbers so far in 2026.

Capital delivery programme director Rob Stewart briefed her on the skylink walkways that will link the terminal directly to the satellite buildings, allowing the track transit people mover to be taken out of service and freeing space for a three-bay, 16,500 square metre terminal extension. She also toured the enlarged security hall and the airfield, and saw the on-site solar farm that opened in September. Ms Alexander said the government backs airport growth that balances the economy with climate, noise and air quality duties, and praised the solar farm.

Managing director Gareth Powell said the visit showed the momentum behind the airport's plans. Stansted says it contributes £11.3 billion a year to the economy, according to York Aviation, and is aiming for net zero in its direct operations by 2038 at the latest. No completion date was given for the walkways or later phases.

Key points

  • More than 30 million passengers in 2025
  • Target of 51 million a year with no new runway
  • Terminal extension of 16,500 sq m planned
  • Net zero for direct operations by 2038
