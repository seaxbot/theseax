New image in bid to name man found dead at Southend East 26 years ago

Locate International hopes a forensic artist's picture of 'Southend Station Man' as he may have looked around 1980 will be recognised by someone who knew him then.

Southend-on-Sea, Southend East station · Sunday 15 November 2026, 12:32 · Updated Monday 16 November 2026, 00:26 · Reported by Locate International
WhatsApp Facebook X

The picture is the work of forensic artist Hew Morrison and imagines the man roughly two decades before he died. Locate International, a charity that traces missing people and tries to put names to the unidentified, took up the case in March and has gone back over the evidence with police.

A train driver on the Shoeburyness to Fenchurch Street line saw the body beside the tracks at about 6am on 15 November 2000 and called British Transport Police. The night had been bitterly cold, yet the man appeared to have taken off the clothes from his upper body, which lay close by.

Tests found no drugs and very little alcohol in his system, though the plastic tube from a breathalyser was in his pocket. The cause of death was recorded as undetermined, with hypothermia thought the most likely explanation. DNA searches in the UK and, through Interpol, in 26 countries have never produced a match.

He had been seen the previous day walking along the platform at Southend East in a green jacket, brown trousers, a leather belt with a gold buckle and white Tommy Hilfiger trainers.

The charity believes he had been homeless for about 20 years and used a homeless centre in Southend. A former director of Skylark Tours, a pleasure boat operator in the town, who later worked at the centre, remembered him as Steve, Steven or Stephen, a regular on Southend Pier in the early 1990s.

Investigators are also trying to reach a possible relative, Chris or Christopher Gilbert, who they think may hold information that could identify the man. Police are seeking him too, in what the charity describes as a non-criminal inquiry.

Dr Mark Greenhalgh, the charity's chief executive, said the face might be known to "an old friend, workmate or distant family member". Anyone with information can contact Locate International in confidence on 0300 102 1011 or at appeals@locate.international.

Key points

  • A distinctive mark on his left cheek appears in both depictions
  • He was known on Southend Pier in the early 1990s as Steve, Steven or Stephen
  • Information: 0300 102 1011 or appeals@locate.international
