Thurrock's baby death rate is 37% above the national average, new health strategy warns

A draft Health and Wellbeing Strategy for 2027 to 2030 puts pregnancy and the early years at the centre of plans to give Thurrock babies a healthier start.

Grays & Thurrock · Friday 6 November 2026, 18:09 · Reported by Thurrock Nub News (Local Democracy Reporting Service)
WhatsApp Facebook X

Thurrock records 6.2 infant deaths for every 1,000 live births, against a national average of 3.9, according to the council's proposed Health and Wellbeing Strategy for 2027 to 2030. The figures mean the borough's infant mortality rate is 37% higher than the England average, and the strategy sets out how the council and NHS plan to respond.

Priorities include cutting smoking and obesity in pregnancy, raising breastfeeding rates and improving childhood vaccination uptake, with families offered support earlier. Cllr Rob Willoughby, cabinet member for adults, health and housing, said the causes of the higher rate are complex and that the council will work with health partners so expectant and new parents get the help they need. The report also notes adult life expectancy in Thurrock is the lowest in mid and south Essex.

Councillors on the Adult and Health Services Overview and Scrutiny Committee are due to consider the strategy next week. NHS Essex says every child death in the borough is reviewed for lessons, and the Integrated Care Board is running a survey of parents and carers on how safer-sleep advice reaches families.

Key points

  • 6.2 infant deaths per 1,000 births in Thurrock
  • National average is 3.9
  • Strategy covers 2027 to 2030
  • Scrutiny committee to consider it next week
