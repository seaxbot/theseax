Thurrock's baby death rate of 6.2 per 1,000 births puts new health plan's focus on pregnancy and early years

A draft Thurrock health and wellbeing strategy for 2027 to 2030 aims to cut smoking and obesity in pregnancy and raise breastfeeding and vaccination rates, after figures showed infant deaths running far above the comparison average.

Grays, Thurrock · Saturday 7 November 2026, 15:58 · Reported by Your Thurrock (Local Democracy Reporting Service)
WhatsApp Facebook X

Thurrock records 6.2 deaths of babies for every 1,000 live births, well above the comparison figure of 3.9, according to the borough's draft Health and Wellbeing Strategy for 2027 to 2030. The plan puts health in pregnancy and the first years of life at the centre of what the council and the NHS will do next.

The strategy aims to reduce smoking and obesity during pregnancy, raise breastfeeding rates and improve childhood vaccination uptake, with support offered to families earlier. Cabinet member Rob Willoughby said the causes of the higher rate are complex, and Clare Angell of NHS Essex said every infant death is a profound loss for a family. Nub News reports that adult life expectancy in Thurrock is also the lowest in mid and south Essex.

Councillors on the adult and health services scrutiny committee are due to consider the draft this week. NHS Essex says every child death in Thurrock is reviewed for lessons, and the integrated care board is asking parents and carers of babies to take part in a safer-sleep survey.

Key points

  • 6.2 infant deaths per 1,000 live births in Thurrock, against 3.9
  • Draft strategy covers 2027 to 2030
  • Targets smoking and obesity in pregnancy, breastfeeding and vaccinations
  • Parents asked to join an NHS safer-sleep survey
