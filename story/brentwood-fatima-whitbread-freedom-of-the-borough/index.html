Javelin great Fatima Whitbread made a Freewoman of Brentwood for her work with children in care

The former world champion, who grew up in children's homes, is the borough's newest honorary freewoman, just over a year after Thurrock gave her its own freedom.

Brentwood · Saturday 14 November 2026, 16:50 · Reported by Brentwood Borough Council
WhatsApp Facebook X

Brentwood Borough Council admitted Fatima Whitbread MBE as an Honorary Freewoman of the Borough at an extraordinary council meeting on Wednesday 11 November. It is one of the highest honours the council can give, and recognises both her sporting career and her campaigning for children in care and young people leaving it.

Ms Whitbread, who lives in the borough, said she was “deeply honoured” and called Brentwood a place she is proud to call home.

Council leader Barry Aspinell said her advocacy had shaped the way the council supports care leavers and encouraged it to adopt the Care Leavers Pledge, which strengthens the help young people get as they move into adulthood. She has also visited Brentwood Ursuline Convent High School to tell pupils her story.

That story began in Stoke Newington, where she was born Fatima Vedad on 3 March 1961. Abandoned as a baby, she spent around 14 years in children's homes. Her life turned when she met Margaret Whitbread, a PE teacher and javelin coach whose family went on to adopt her, and she spent her teenage years in Chadwell St Mary.

Coached by Margaret, she became one of the best-loved British athletes of the 1980s. She won Olympic bronze in Los Angeles in 1984, and in August 1986 threw a world record 77.44 metres in qualifying at the European Championships in Stuttgart before going on to take the European title. World gold followed in Rome in 1987, the year she was voted BBC Sports Personality of the Year and appointed MBE, and she added Olympic silver in Seoul in 1988. A long-running shoulder injury ended her career in 1990.

She has since become a familiar face on television, finishing third on I'm A Celebrity in both 2011 and 2023, and one of the country's loudest voices for children in care. She received the Helen Rollason Award at BBC Sports Personality of the Year in 2023 and an honorary doctorate from the University of Roehampton in 2025, and her charity, Fatima's UK Campaign, works to open up opportunities for care leavers.

The Brentwood honour comes after Thurrock Council made her and Margaret Freewomen of Thurrock, with the awards presented to them in August 2025.

Key points

  • Approved at an extraordinary council meeting on 11 November
  • Council adopted the Care Leavers Pledge after her advocacy
  • Set a javelin world record of 77.44m in 1986
