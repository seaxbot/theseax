# Anglia Ruskin wins £250,000 to ready its harassment early-warning tool

**Researchers built it to show employers where staff feel unsafe. Government money will now help get it ready for organisations to buy.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Chelmsford, Essex, England. Published: Saturday 28 November 2026, 06:21 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Education.

## Key points

- Backed by UK Research and Innovation
- Created by Prof Matt Fossey and Dr Ashley Perry
- Grew from NATO research into armed forces misconduct

## The story

Researchers at Anglia Ruskin University have been awarded almost £250,000 of government money to turn a workplace safety tool into something employers can use. The Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Auditing Service, known as SASHAS, has won Proof of Concept funding from UK Research and Innovation, the university said.

SASHAS was developed by Professor Matt Fossey and Dr Ashley Perry. It gathers staff experiences and views, then turns them into figures for a whole organisation, a department or a single team. That is meant to show where inappropriate behaviour is happening and where risks are building, before anything more serious takes place.

Most reporting systems only start once someone makes a formal complaint, by which point harm has been done. According to ARU, many incidents still go unreported because victims fear what will happen to them or lack confidence in the process.

## From military research to the workplace

The idea grew out of a NATO research programme on sexual misconduct in the armed forces. Estimates quoted by the university suggest military sexual trauma can affect more than one in five serving personnel.

ARU's Veterans and Families Institute for Military Social Research leads the work, alongside its International Policing and Public Protection Research Institute and its Centre of Excellence for Equity in Uniformed Public Services. The platform has already been tested and piloted with several organisations.

The new money pays for better analytics, wider user testing and the groundwork needed before organisations in the UK and abroad can buy it.
