# Tube strike: Essex commuters face late start and disrupted lines until midday

**Central line passengers from Epping, Loughton and Buckhurst Hill face a disrupted journey. Transport for London expects a normal service by early evening.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Published: Wednesday 25 November 2026, 09:38 (UK time). Updated: Thursday 26 November 2026, 06:57. Section: News. Topic: Transport.

## Key points

- Elizabeth line, DLR, Overground and buses unaffected
- First of eight walkouts over the winter
- Next strikes on 17 and 19 December

## The story

Tube trains start late on Friday 27 November, from about 7am, and every line is disrupted until the RMT drivers' 24-hour walkout ends at 11.59am. No trains ran anywhere on the London Underground from 9pm on Thursday 26 November, after last-ditch talks at the conciliation service Acas ended without a deal.

Services run on all lines after midday, but Transport for London does not expect a normal service until about 6pm.

For Essex, the line that matters most is the Central line, which runs out to Buckhurst Hill, Loughton, Debden, Theydon Bois and Epping. Passengers using it on Friday morning should expect a late first train and disruption until midday, and allow extra time for the journey home.

The Piccadilly, Circle and Waterloo & City lines stay shut until the walkout is over. Travellers heading from Essex to Heathrow can use the Elizabeth line instead, which runs direct from Shenfield, Brentwood and Romford.

## What is still running

TfL says the Elizabeth line, DLR, London Overground, buses and trams will run as normal, but will be busier than usual. Greater Anglia and c2c trains into Liverpool Street and Fenchurch Street are not part of the strike, though passengers changing onto the Tube at either end will be affected.

## Why drivers are striking

The row is over plans to move Tube drivers from a five-day to a four-day week. The RMT says drivers would have to give up existing protections whether they chose four days or five, and that those who refused could be moved away from their depots. Aslef, the other drivers' union, supports the four-day week.

TfL told the BBC it had promised during the talks to work on proposals covering several of the union's concerns, and had asked the RMT to postpone the strike while it did so. The union refused. Acas says it remains in contact with both sides.

This is the first of eight strikes. The others are planned for 17 and 19 December, 4 and 6 January, and 17, 23 and 25 February. TfL is asking passengers to check before they travel and allow extra time.
