# Cocaine kingpin who sent couriers to Harwich and Southend jailed for 18 years

**Bradley Stainer ran a network that moved more than a tonne of cocaine. His couriers were paid more than £1,000 a day.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Harwich, Essex, England. Published: Thursday 26 November 2026, 11:48 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Courts.

## Key points

- Unmasked as "Bugsy" through encrypted messages
- Ringleader Deborah Mason jailed for 20 years in 2025
- Arrested on 19 May after the gang's convictions

## The story

A man who ran a nationwide cocaine network, whose couriers made regular trips to Harwich Port, has been jailed for 18 years.

Bradley Stainer, 46, of Gatton Park Road, Redhill, was sentenced at Woolwich Crown Court on Wednesday 25 November. He had admitted conspiracy to supply cocaine and possession of criminal property in June.

The Metropolitan Police says the network moved more than a tonne of cocaine into the UK. Stainer bought the drug on a commercial scale and used a team of couriers to move it around the country.

## The Essex link

Detectives tracked the couriers between April and November 2023. They found regular trips to Harwich Port, as well as journeys to Southend, South London, Rotherham, Leicester, Walsall and other places.

Packages of imported cocaine were collected and driven across London and the rest of the UK, as far as Bradford, Birmingham, Bristol and Cardiff. The Met has not said what the trips to Harwich involved.

## A grandmother's courier gang

The couriers were run by Deborah Mason, 66, of Crayford Road, Tufnell Park, who recruited members of her own family. She was convicted of conspiracy to supply Class A drugs in April 2025 and jailed for 20 years that July.

Mason and seven other members of the gang were jailed for a total of 106 and a half years at Woolwich Crown Court. The court heard they supplied nearly a tonne of cocaine over seven months, with a street value of about £80 million.

Stainer, who called himself "Bugsy", contacted the group through encrypted messaging apps, including Signal, and used false names. He agreed their pay, more than £1,000 a day, and told them where to go.

He travelled between the UK and Spain, leaving with large amounts of cash hidden in his luggage and taking the profits back to his home in Marbella. Messages showed he and Mason also talked about spending time in Dubai and Marbella.

## How he was caught

Officers went through thousands of encrypted messages, mapped the whole network and kept the gang under surveillance. The messages kept mentioning a man called "Bugsy", who arranged wages, directed shipments and collected money every month.

Detectives linked the alias to Stainer and showed that he was the head of the operation. He was arrested on 19 May.

Detective Constable Jack Kraushaar, who led the investigation, said officers had "scoured through thousands of encrypted messages and identified Stainer as the next link in the chain".

"This is more evidence of the Met's battle to prevent more drugs entering the UK, where they cause violence, antisocial behaviour and tear communities apart," he said.
