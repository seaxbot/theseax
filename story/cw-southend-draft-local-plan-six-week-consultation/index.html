Government direction pushes Southend's draft local plan towards a six-week public consultation

The new plan would set the rules for where homes go and how the council handles applications for shared houses, affordable housing and hot food takeaways.

Southend-on-Sea · Tuesday 10 November 2026, 06:24 · Original reporting from council papers
WhatsApp Facebook X

Southend-on-Sea City Council's cabinet held an extraordinary meeting on 5 November 2026 to consider the final draft of the city's new Local Plan. A report from the Executive Director (Environment and Place) sought agreement to comply with a government direction by publishing the Regulation 19 Proposed Submission version of the plan for a consultation lasting at least six weeks. At this stage residents and others can comment on whether the plan is sound and legally compliant.

The minutes say national policy requires every council to keep an up-to-date local plan, which allocates sites for development and sets the policies used to decide planning applications. Councillors were told the existing Development Plan is out of date and no longer reflects major social, environmental and economic changes. The reasons recorded for the decision warn that without a plan development would not stop, but would be more likely to come forward in an uncoordinated and speculative way, with less supporting infrastructure and fewer protections for existing residents.

The papers say new criteria-based policies would give the council a more robust, evidenced approach to applications including Houses in Multiple Occupation, affordable housing and hot food takeaways. The meeting papers include the draft plan, a policies map, a report on the earlier Regulation 18 consultation, a sustainability appraisal and an updated Local Development Scheme setting out the timetable. Three questions from councillors were answered at the meeting and published with the minutes.

Key points

  • Extraordinary cabinet meeting held on 5 November 2026
  • Report sought agreement to publish the Regulation 19 draft plan under a government direction
  • Consultation to run for a minimum of six weeks, on soundness and legal compliance
  • Plan would include policies on HMOs, affordable housing and hot food takeaways
