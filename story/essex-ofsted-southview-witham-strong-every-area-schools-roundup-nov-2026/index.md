# Witham special school rated strong in every area by Ofsted

**Southview School got the second-highest grade in all eight areas. Four other Essex primaries also had new report cards published this week.**

Published by The Seax (https://theseax.com/), independent Essex news. Place: Witham, Great Notley, Chelmsford, Tiptree, Fordham, Essex, England. Published: Friday 27 November 2026, 09:00 (UK time). Section: News. Topic: Education.

## Key points

- Southview praised for preparing pupils for adult life
- Safeguarding met at all five schools
- Parkwood and Milldene: attendance needs attention

## The story

Southview School in Witham, a special school for pupils with physical and neurological needs, has been rated strong standard in all eight areas Ofsted checked.

Under Ofsted's new report cards, schools are graded in each area from exceptional through strong standard, expected standard and needs attention to urgent improvement. There is no single overall grade.

Inspectors visited Southview, on Conrad Road, on 20 and 21 October. They praised teaching that builds pupils' skills step by step, close work with families and therapists, and the school's preparation of pupils for adult life. Pupils said they feel safe, and bullying is described as extremely rare. Julian Cochrane is the headteacher, and the school is part of the Hope Learning Community trust.

## The other new reports

Fordham All Saints CofE Primary, near Colchester, was rated strong standard for attendance and behaviour and for personal development, and expected standard in its other areas. Inspectors said attendance had improved rapidly and behaviour was excellent, but leaders need to check pupils' existing knowledge more consistently.

White Court School in Great Notley was rated strong standard for inclusion and expected standard elsewhere. Reading and maths results were above the national average in 2024/25, but inspectors said writing needs strengthening, especially for disadvantaged pupils.

Parkwood Academy in Chelmsford, rated Requires Improvement in 2024, was graded strong standard for early years and expected standard in five areas. Attendance and behaviour needs attention: persistent absence was 20.8 per cent last autumn, against 14.4 per cent nationally.

Milldene Primary in Tiptree was rated expected standard in five areas, with attendance and behaviour and inclusion both needing attention. Inspectors said support for some pupils was not removing their barriers to learning quickly enough, and some parents were unhappy with communication after staffing changes.

Safeguarding standards were met at all five schools. Ofsted carries out monitoring inspections at schools with any area graded needs attention.
