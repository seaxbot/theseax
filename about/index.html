About The Seax

The Seax is a free, independent website for the people of Essex: the county's news, sport, weather, public services, community life and what's on in one place, updated through the day, plus our own investigations and data journalism.

How The Seax works

We watch hundreds of trusted sources across Essex and beyond: local newspapers and radio, hyperlocal and independent newsrooms, Essex Police, the fire and ambulance services, councils, the NHS, courts, charities, clubs and venues, plus national and specialist outlets when they report on Essex.

The Seax is produced with the help of AI. Automated research tools gather the latest reports each hour, and AI writes our headlines, summaries and short story pages in our own words, following our editorial rules. Every story names the outlet that reported it and links directly to the original, so you can always read the full report and support the journalists who did the work. When only one outlet has reported a story, our summary is deliberately short and points you to theirs.

We do not copy articles, take photographs from other publishers, or publish anything that is not reported by a named source or set out in a public document.

Our own journalism

As well as bringing Essex's news together, The Seax does its own reporting. You can spot it by the gold banner on the story.

  • Seax Investigates is our original reporting. We go to the primary source, such as a published independent review, an official register or a government list, and report what it means for Essex, often before anyone else has.
  • Seax Analysis is our data journalism. We take official figures, such as police recorded crime, and work them out for Essex and its towns and neighbourhoods, with our own charts, maps and look-up tools like the neighbourhood crime checker.
  • Exclusive: a red Exclusive tag means The Seax was first to report the story.
  • The Weekend is our home for longer reads and analysis to take your time over, at the top of the front page every weekend.

Every piece of our own reporting names and links the documents and data it is based on, so you can check it yourself. All of it is collected on the Seax Investigates page.

How we check our own reporting

When a story is ours alone, no other outlet's report backs it up, so before anything we originate is published it has to pass three checks:

  1. Figures: every number in the headline, summary and story is worked out again from the original source data.
  2. Independent check: the key facts are confirmed a second way, from the official document itself, another dataset or a separate recount, and every name, place and label is checked against how the source writes it.
  3. Read-back: the finished story is read as a reader would read it, to make sure the headline is exactly true, nothing overstates the evidence and the caveats a fair reader needs are there.

If any check fails, the story is held back until it is fixed. If a single word changes afterwards, all three checks are done again. Our own reporting is researched and written with the help of AI, working only from public documents and data, and is chosen and directed by our editor.

Council Watch

Council Watch is also The Seax's own reporting. Twice a day we read the public meeting papers of Essex's councils (agendas, reports, decisions and minutes), politely and within each website's published rules, and write short stories about the decisions that matter to residents. Every Council Watch story links to the council documents, and the pages, it is based on, passes the same three checks, and is written with the help of AI from those documents only.

Know something we should look into? Email news@theseax.com. We never publish anything about a person from a tip alone.

Our editorial rules

  • Facts only as the source states them. No invented quotes, figures, scores or details.
  • Crime and court reporting is limited to what police and courts have said. People who have been arrested or charged are presumed innocent, and we do not report anything that could prejudice a trial.
  • We never name victims of sexual offences or identify children involved in criminal cases.
  • We do not report suicide or self-harm.
  • We report inquests only where there is a clear public interest, from the Essex Coroner's published court list and official or established sources. We never speculate about how someone died and never name children.
  • We never identify a survivor of abuse. Stories about abuse carry a warning at the top and support lines at the end.
  • Every party and every MP is treated the same way, and where a story makes a serious claim about a person or organisation we include their published response where there is one.

Our full editorial standards and public corrections log are on their own pages.

Why "The Seax"?

The seax is the short sword of the Saxons, and three seaxes appear on the arms of Essex: gules, three seaxes fesswise in pale argent, pommels and hilts or. Our badge follows that blazon.
